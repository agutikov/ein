# Q-M1e.11 — H holds what a debt only a commitment brings would propose

H is taken once, at root. An obligation's activator is an ordinary fact, so a rule can derive one at a node, and that
node owes what root never did. The obligations rung therefore also proposes, at root, what such a debt would, read off
the rules with no saturation:

1. the activators a commitment can derive: each conclusion that holds no variable and would activate an obligation
   rule, in the `:assert` of a rule that reads a relation a commitment can change, or is activated by one;
2. for each such activator root does not hold, the rung's own scan at root of the instances its plan matches, each
   guard read with its witness step skipped;
3. a candidate that adds a relation widens what a commitment can change, so both steps repeat until they add nothing.

A plan read this way is held to every decline of the rung. Where H holds a candidate for such a debt, every question
prints a `later debt` row under `universe`, and the summary carries `root.hypgen.later_debts`. The reading is at
relation grain and inexact both ways: H may hold a candidate for a debt no node owes, and an activator a rule writes
with a variable is not read. A program it adds a candidate to derives an activator from a commitment, and the static
check flags it.

**Why.** A node owing what H cannot pay is judged against a debt no commitment discharges, and R loses the model;
reading the rules keeps H one set, taken once, which every route and the oracle read alike.
