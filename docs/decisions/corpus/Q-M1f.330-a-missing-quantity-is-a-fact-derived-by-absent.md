# Q-M1f.330 — A missing quantity is a fact derived by `absent`, in a module of its own

Where a question rests on a quantity no sentence states, the word theory names it by a derived fact. `gaps.ein`, a
module a problem imports, derives by `absent` `(not-stated Q)` for a quantity that is wanted, has no value and
needs nothing, and `(unanswered QUESTION Q)` for a question that rests on one. The read-out then says what is known,
which question has no answer, and which quantity is missing, with no search.

**Why.** Of the forms a gap could take, this one says all three things in the read-out: an obligation names the gap
but answers nothing, not even what is known, and a hypothesis over a list of numbers answers only where such a list
is stated. It is the form the examples are written in; a theory of what a problem leaves out may take another.

**Held by.** `examples/word_problems/gap.ein`, whose claims state Bob's 3 as known and the `unanswered` fact naming
Alice's earlier amount; `kinds.ein`; `gaps.ein`'s rules.
