# Q-M1f.333 — What is not listed is zero only where a problem adopts it

*Alice has 3 pears* does not say that Alice has no apples: in the word theory, a quantity no sentence states is a
gap ([Q-M1f.330](Q-M1f.330-a-missing-quantity-is-a-fact-derived-by-absent.md)). *What is not listed is zero* is an
assumption a problem may adopt, a module of its own, `listed_is_all.ein`: imported, it derives by `absent` over `has`,
for every holder and kind the `has` sentences name at a moment with no amount listed, `(assumed (amount WHO KIND
MOMENT) z)`, resting on `(adopted listed-is-all has)`. A problem that adopts it states that `has` is no hypothesis
relation, `(solve :no-hypothesis (has))`.

**Why.** It is a reusable assumption and not a law: it holds of a school problem that lists what each person has,
and not of every relation; in the Zebra puzzle a house whose owner is not stated is one whose owner is not known
yet. Adopting it by an import makes the assumption visible in the program, and the answer says it rests on it
([Q-M1f.331](Q-M1f.331-an-assumption-is-a-marked-fact-and-the-answer.md)).

**Held by.** `examples/word_problems/kinds.ein`, the gap, and `kinds_listed.ein`, the same problem with the
assumption adopted, each with its claims.
