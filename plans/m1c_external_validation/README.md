## The thesis

**Every check this repo has is relative.** The conformance tiers compared two
engines; the goldens compare ein.rs to its own past; after
[P1a.10](../m1a_rust/p1a.10_single_implementation/README.md) the second engine
is gone, so what is left compares ein.rs to yesterday's ein.rs. All of it
answers *did this change?* None of it answers *is this right?*

There are exactly two ways to answer the second question, and this milestone
is one phase for each:

1. **What the rules say they do.** An expectation written next to a rule, run
   by the engine — [P1c.1](p1c.1_stdlib_conformance/README.md).
2. **What other systems answer.** The same problem stated for Z3, CVC5,
   SWI-Prolog, Soufflé, Clingo and Lean, run by one harness, compared on the
   *answer* first and the clock second —
   [P1c.2](p1c.2_external_benchmarks/README.md).

The precedent is specific, recent and expensive. `disjunctive-prune`'s
`(neq ?h_other ?h1)` guard was wrong for a year — through five phases of
byte-exact parity — and what found it was an **independent enumeration** of a
puzzle's models, written outside the engine on the day. Both engines agreed
with each other the whole time, and agreement was all anything checked.
P1c.1 makes that kind of check cheap for a *rule*; P1c.2 makes it permanent
for a *puzzle*.

### The two phases are one pipeline

P1c.2's ground truth lands in P1c.1's form. When Clingo enumerates 32 models
of `zebra2-minus-15` and Z3's blocking-clause loop agrees, that answer is
written into the `.ein` file as an `:expect`, and from then on `ein test`
re-checks it on a machine with no external solver installed at all. **The
external tools are needed to establish the answer, not to keep it.** That is
why P1c.1 goes first and why the two live in one milestone rather than in
two.

## Acceptance for the milestone

- **No claim in the repo rests only on self-agreement.** Every stdlib rule has
  a program that activates it and states what it derives; every benchmark
  problem's answer set is confirmed by at least one independent system.
- The confirmations are **checked in as `:expect`**, so they survive the
  absence of the tools that produced them.
- The report is **regenerated by one command** and carries its own conditions:
  system versions, machine state, what was missing.
- **A missing external tool is reported, never skipped past.**
  [S1a.10.1](../m1a_rust/p1a.10_single_implementation/s1a.10.1_bank_the_oracle.md)
  found 42 tests that started a Python process and skipped invisibly when one
  would not start; a benchmark harness has exactly the same failure mode and
  a louder incentive to hide it.
- Where Ein loses, the report says so, with the number.

## Non-goals

- **A solver back-end.** M3 (SMT integration) was dropped 2026-08-18 and stays
  dropped — see [`plans/README.md`](../README.md) § Roadmap. Ein never hands a
  slice to Z3. Comparing against a solver is the opposite of integrating one,
  and the drop note already says the comparison axis survives.
- **A benchmark *suite*.** P1c.2's corpus is small, curated and cross-language
  by construction; the sweep over reasoning benchmarks — BBH `logical_deduction`,
  ProofWriter, LogiQA — is [F13](../followups/f13_puzzles_beyond_zebra/ideas.md)'s
  territory and needs [M2](../m2_nl_to_ir/README.md)'s NL frontend to be
  interesting.
- **Ein's own performance work.** [P1a.6](../m1a_rust/p1a.6_performance/README.md)
  owns that and closed on its targets. A comparison that shows Ein slow
  somewhere is a *finding*; acting on it is a phase somewhere else.
- **Winning.** A benchmark built to be won gets tuned until it is. The
  deliverable is a table with its caveats — including the rows where a CP-SAT
  solver beats the graph engine by orders of magnitude on a problem that is
  all arithmetic, which is the expected result on n-queens and is not a
  defect.
- **New reasoning features.** If a comparison shows that a problem cannot be
  *stated* in ein-lang, that is a finding for
  [M1d](../m1d_satisfiability/README.md) or a followup, not a stage here.

## Open questions

[`open_questions.md`](open_questions.md) — `Q-M1c.<n>`. The two inherited from
M1a came with P1c.1 and keep their text: **Q-M1c.1** (ex Q-M1a.19) how a
program states what it expects, and **Q-M1c.2** (ex Q-M1a.20) what an
expectation may say. P1c.2 adds the ones about method: what makes an encoding
fair, whether a proof assistant belongs in a timing table, and where the
harness lives.
