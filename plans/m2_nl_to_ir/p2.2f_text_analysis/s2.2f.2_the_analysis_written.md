## Context

What passed the check has to reach the model. Two ways, both measured by S2.2f.3: **as text in the prompt**, the
graph as the parser printed it or as plain lines, a node a line, which the model reads as context and cannot copy
into its program; and **as Ein facts**, a vocabulary of the analysis's own (a node, its concept, a role between two
nodes, a name, a polarity), each fact with the sentence it came from as its `:source`, which the kernel loads, `ein
render kb` draws, and step 1 could be given as a program to read.

## Acceptance

- The two forms, written by a committed script from the parser's output; the facts load under `ein solve
  --no-search` with no refusal, and `ein render kb` draws them. What of AMR's spelling Ein's names do not take
  (a role `:ARG0`, a sense `drink-01`, a quoted name) and how it is written instead, stated once.
- The alignment: each node with its sentence, and with its words where an aligner gives them; how many nodes have
  none, per text.
- EinAf reads an analysis from a file (a slot of the steps' prompts, a flag of `einaf steps run` and `einaf
  from-text`), and its tests replay the recorded analyses of the six texts, committed under
  `einaf/tests/fixtures/`, with no Python and no GPU.
- What the facts cost the kernel (facts, a load's time) on the six texts; anything of Ein to the evaluation.
- The gate is green on the stage's last commit.

### The two forms, on a Zebra sentence

*The Old Gold smoker owns snails.* (sentence 7), SPRING's graph, with spaCy's entities beside. **As text**, PENMAN as
the parser printed it, the aligner's markers removed (the default form), or a node a line:

```text
# ::snt The Old Gold smoker owns snails.
# named entities: Old Gold (PRODUCT)
(z1 / own-01
      :ARG0 (z2 / person
            :ARG0-of (z3 / smoke-02
                  :ARG1 (z4 / gold
                        :mod (z5 / old))))
      :ARG1 (z6 / snail))

Sentence 7: The Old Gold smoker owns snails.
  named entities: Old Gold (PRODUCT)
  z1 own-01 :ARG0 z2 :ARG1 z6
  z2 person
  z3 smoke-02 :ARG0 z2 :ARG1 z4
  z4 gold :mod z5
  z5 old
  z6 snail
```

**As Ein facts** (the vocabulary's eight declarations above them, `(solve :no-search)` after the last sentence):

```ein
;; 7. The Old Gold smoker owns snails.
(amr-top S7 s7-z1 :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-node S7 s7-z1 own-01 :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-role s7-z1 ARG0 s7-z2 :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-role s7-z1 ARG1 s7-z6 :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-node S7 s7-z2 person :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-node S7 s7-z3 smoke-02 :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-role s7-z3 ARG0 s7-z2 :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-role s7-z3 ARG1 s7-z4 :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-node S7 s7-z4 gold :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-role s7-z4 mod s7-z5 :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-node S7 s7-z5 old :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(amr-node S7 s7-z6 snail :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
(named-entity S7 Old_Gold PRODUCT :source "The Old Gold smoker owns snails.")
```

SPRING reads *Old Gold* as gold that is old (L-M2.41) and spaCy as one product: the analysis carries both, as the
check proposed. IBM's graph of *Coffee is drunk in the green house.* (sentence 4), whose aligner is learned, carries
each node's word, `[drunk]` in a line and `(amr-word s4-d drunk)` as a fact; it reads *Coffee* as a person and *drunk*
as intoxicated (L-M2.41), and so it is written.

### The spelling: what of AMR's Ein's names do not take

Stated once, `einaf::analysis::name`, [the page's § 3](../../../docs/einaf/09-analysis.md#3-the-forms):

| AMR writes | Ein takes it? | written |
|---|---|---|
| a sense `drink-01`, `have-rel-role-91`; a number `5`; the polarity `-`; `amr-unknown` | yes, a name as it stands | as it stands |
| a role `:ARG0` | no: a colon begins a keyword | `ARG0` |
| `:ARG0-of`, a role read the other way | (the reader's) | `ARG0`, its two nodes swapped |
| a string, a name's words `"Old" "Gold"`, a value `"Kool-Aid"` | no: a string is no object | a name, its words joined by `_`: `Old_Gold`, `Kool-Aid` |
| a character outside `A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -` (the names a step's grammar lets the model write; Ein takes more) | partly | `_`: `"St. Louis"` is `St__Louis` |
| `and`, `or` (and the other words of Ein's grammar) | no: `and` and `or` are never a name (E-143) | `and_`, `or_` |
| a name of `_` alone, or of nothing | no: a bare `_` is refused | `__` |
| a variable `z1` of sentence 4 | yes, but one sentence's | `s4-z1` |

### Defaults

Each is the stage's, for the user to revise.

1. **The converter is EinAf's, in Rust**, and the phase's script only gathers what the parsers wrote into one file a
   text: the vocabulary is EinAf's, and its tests hold the forms, the facts' load and the prompt with no Python.
2. **One file a text holds all three parsers and spaCy's entities**; a run chooses the parser (SPRING by default, the
   check's proposal), the form, and the entities beside or not (not, by default).
3. **The default text form is PENMAN.** Both text forms fit the prompt's budget for the Zebra text by far (1 465 and
   1 440 tokens of block against 53 248 left), so the stage's rule does not choose; PENMAN is what the parser printed,
   with nothing of EinAf's in it, and the notation AMR is published in. The lines are 2 % shorter under SPRING and 17 %
   longer under IBM.
4. **A template of its own for step 1 given an analysis**, `steps/step1-analysis.v1.md`, and not a slot added to
   `steps/step1.v1.md`: the recorded runs name step 1's template by its digest, which a new slot would move; with no
   analysis a run renders `steps/step1.v1.md`, request for request, and records no new key.
5. **Step 1 alone is given the analysis**, after the text and before the answer line; steps 2 and 3 are not, and the
   whole program in one call, which has no step 1, refuses one.
6. **Each form's paragraph says what the form is and that the parser can read a sentence wrong**; the facts' says
   they are for reading and none of them is a statement. The misreadings stay (S2.2f.1's fourth proposal).
7. **A node's words are shown only where the aligner is learned** (IBM's), on Q-M2.50's default and the brief;
   amrlib's RBW markers are kept and counted (L-M2.46).
8. **The facts end with `(solve :no-search)`**, also in the prompt, so that what the model reads is what the kernel
   loads (E-140, E-141); step 1's grammar allows no `solve`, so it cannot be copied under it.
9. **The vocabulary**: eight relations, `amr-` and `named-entity`; a sentence `Sn`, a node `sn-VAR`; a name's words
   one name on its name node (`amr-name`), not its `:op` strings; `:polarity -` a fact of its own (`amr-negative`);
   every other constant and string `amr-const`; an inverted role normalized; every fact quoting its sentence, as the
   plan says (Q-M2.51).
10. **The spelling keeps only what a step's grammar lets the model write in a name**, `A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -`, narrower
    than Ein's, so that no name of the analysis is one the model could not write.
11. **The named entities beside the AMR in all three forms** (the check proposed them as text): the facts arm can then
    be compared with them as well.
12. **A pronoun is carried as the parser wrote it** (L-M2.42): no resolver's chain, and no text given whole.
13. **The tokens are counted by llama.cpp's `llama-tokenize`** with the model's own vocabulary, on the CPU, not by
    EinAf's binding, which the gate's build does not have.
14. **The forms written of the six texts are not committed**: `tools/write_forms.py` writes them again; the recorded
    analyses, which EinAf's tests read, are.
