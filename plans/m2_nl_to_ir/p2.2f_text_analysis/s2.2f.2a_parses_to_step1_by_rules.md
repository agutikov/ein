## Context

How far does a parse alone go toward step 1, with Ein rules and no model? Two parses are asked, each read by its own
rules:

- **AMR**, a graph of concepts and roles (SPRING's, the phase's default; XFM's and IBM's where a case asks);
- **spaCy's dependency parse**, a tree over the sentence's words: `en_core_web_trf`, the tool S2.2f.1 found
  wrong on the fewest cells, and the only one to read *Old Gold*, *Chesterfields* and *Lucky Strike* as products.

The user's words of 2026-11-27 hold for both:

- **The condition**: *"arm that turns AMR into step-1 facts - in a matter of curiocity - it is an option, if rules
  relies on finite amount of terms that AMR uses, not rely on NL words"*. A parse's rules match only that parse's
  closed vocabulary, together with the names of EinAf's vocabulary.
  - **AMR's** closed vocabulary is its roles (`:ARG0` to `:ARG5`, `:polarity`, `:name`, `:op1`…, `:quant`,
    `:location`, `:mod`…) and its fixed special concepts (`name`, `and`, `or`, `have-rel-role-91`, `date-entity`…).
  - **spaCy's** is the label sets its model declares: the dependency labels (`nsubj`, `nsubjpass`, `dobj`, `pobj`,
    `prep`, `neg`, `amod`, `compound`… in the model's own scheme, which is not UD v2's), the coarse tags (`PROPN`,
    `VERB`, `NUM`…) and the fine ones, the morphological features and their values (`Number=Plur`, `Voice=Pass`…),
    and the entity labels (`PERSON`, `PRODUCT`, `CARDINAL`…).

  A concept, a word or a lemma (`drink-01`, `drunk`, `in`) is carried through as data and never matched. A case that
  would need a rule naming an English word is not written, and is counted as one the parse alone does not reach. A
  preposition is the plain case: *lives in* and *lives next to* differ by words alone, so their difference can only
  be carried, into a name.
- **The way round what Ein cannot do**: *"the only one is not possible to do in Ein - from fact (is drunk by) make
  is-drunk-by atom. but it can be done outside Ein, e.g. if Ein rules will produce some special forms like e.g.
  (defrel (is drunk by)) and EinAF post process the output"*. A rule never writes an atom the program lacks, so a
  name made of words is EinAf's to make. The rules conclude a request, `(defrel (is drunk in))`. EinAf reads the
  requests off the fixpoint, writes the declarations and the facts under the new names into the program's text, and
  the kernel loads it again. A declaration written that way is one the loader's readers see (a query, the blind
  enumerator), which a declaration a rule concludes is not.

## Acceptance

- **spaCy's parse as S-forms**: a vocabulary of EinAf's for a dependency parse, written once with its `:why` texts:
  a token with its sentence and position, its word and lemma as data, its tags, its features, its head and label,
  and its entity.
  - Every fact quotes its sentence (Q-M2.51, ruled *yes*).
  - The parse is recorded for the six texts beside the AMR, in the recorded analysis or a file of its own, by a
    committed script from S2.2f.1's runs.
  - EinAf writes it (`einaf analysis show … --form facts`, or a form of its own).
  - It loads under `ein solve --no-search` with no refusal, and its spelling of words as Ein names is S2.2f.2's
    five rules.
- **Each parse's closed vocabulary, written down once**, with its source cited: AMR's from the AMR guidelines;
  spaCy's read off the model (`nlp.get_pipe(…).labels`, the tagger's and the morphologizer's sets, the entity
  recognizer's labels) at its pinned version. Each is a committed list.
- **A check that holds each rule set to its list**: every constant a rule writes is in its parse's list, is a name
  of EinAf's vocabulary, or is a word of Ein's language. The check fails on a rule naming anything else, and runs
  among EinAf's tests.
- **The rules, for each parse, in both shapes the plan asked for**, on the six texts:
  - **a concept or a head word as an argument**: `(event e drink-01)`, or a token and its dependents as facts over
    tokens;
  - **made a relation**: through `defrel` requests and EinAf's pass. A relation made that way needs a derived
    activator, since a rule cannot write a fact whose relation a `:match` bound, only one a parameter of its
    activator binds.
- **EinAf's `defrel` pass**, one for both parses: it reads the requests off root's fixpoint and spells each name by
  S2.2f.2's rules (a phrase's words joined). It writes the declarations, each with a `:why`, and the facts under the
  new names, and loads the program again. One command does all of this, and a test replays it on the six texts, for
  each parse, with no Python and no GPU.
- **The programs read against the hand's step 1**, per text and per parse:
  - on S2.2f.1's failure classes, cell by cell, with the same right / wrong / not carried judgement S2.2f.1 used, in
    one table with AMR's rules, spaCy's rules, the model's columns and the parsers' own;
  - by the contract's check, and whether each program loads.
- **Counted, per parse**:
  - what the parse alone reaches;
  - what needs an English word, and so is not reached;
  - what the parser misread and the rules carried through, such as the brands and the passive of *drink* (L-M2.41).
- **The two parses against each other**: where AMR's roles reach what spaCy's labels do not (the passive's roles, a
  negation's scope), and where spaCy's tree reaches what AMR's graph does not (the brands as products, a number as
  a word). And whether the two together reach more than either: a run over both, if the table suggests it.
- What Ein showed of itself, filed in the phase's evaluation; what each program costs the kernel (facts, a load's
  time).
- **EinAf's tests and the link check pass**; the gate is the coordinator's, on master after the merge.
