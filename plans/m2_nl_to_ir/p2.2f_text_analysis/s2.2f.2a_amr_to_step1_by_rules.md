## Context

How far does the parse alone go toward step 1, with Ein rules and no model? It was arm 5 of S2.2f.3, on the user's
two words of 2026-11-27:

- **The condition**: *"arm that turns AMR into step-1 facts - in a matter of curiocity - it is an option, if rules
  relies on finite amount of terms that AMR uses, not rely on NL words"*. The rules match only AMR's closed
  vocabulary, its roles (`:ARG0` to `:ARG5`, `:polarity`, `:name`, `:op1`…, `:quant`, `:location`, `:mod`…) and its
  fixed special concepts (`name`, `and`, `or`, `have-rel-role-91`, `date-entity`…), together with the names of
  EinAf's vocabulary. A concept such as `drink-01` or a name's words is carried through as data and never matched.
  A case that would need a rule naming an English word is not written, and is counted as one the parse alone does
  not reach.
- **The way round what Ein cannot do**: *"the only one is not possible to do in Ein - from fact (is drunk by) make
  is-drunk-by atom. but it can be done outside Ein, e.g. if Ein rules will produce some special forms like e.g.
  (defrel (is drunk by)) and EinAF post process the output"*. A rule never writes an atom the program lacks, so a
  name made of words is EinAf's to make. The rules conclude a request, `(defrel (is drunk in))`. EinAf reads the
  requests off the fixpoint, writes the declarations and the facts under the new names into the program's text, and
  the kernel loads it again. A declaration written that way is one the loader's readers see (a query, the blind
  enumerator), which a declaration a rule concludes is not.

## Acceptance

- **AMR's closed vocabulary, written down once**: the roles and special concepts the rules may name, taken from
  the AMR guidelines with the source cited, as a committed list.
- **A check that holds the rules to it**: every constant a rule writes is in that list, is a name of EinAf's
  vocabulary, or is a word of Ein's language. The check fails on a rule naming anything else, and runs among
  EinAf's tests.
- **The rules, in both shapes the plan asked for**, on the six texts:
  - **a concept as an argument**: `(event e drink-01)`, its roles as facts over nodes;
  - **a concept or a phrase made a relation**: through `defrel` requests and EinAf's pass. A concept made a relation
    needs a derived activator, since a rule cannot write a fact whose relation a `:match` bound, only one a parameter
    of its activator binds.
- **EinAf's `defrel` pass**: it reads the requests off root's fixpoint and spells each name by S2.2f.2's rules (a
  phrase's words joined). It writes the declarations, each with a `:why`, and the facts under the new names, and
  loads the program again. One command does all of this, and a test replays it on the six texts with no Python and
  no GPU.
- **The programs read against the hand's step 1**:
  - per text, on S2.2f.1's failure classes, cell by cell, with the same right / wrong / not carried judgement S2.2f.1
    used, and beside the model's columns and the parsers';
  - by the contract's check, and whether each program loads.
- **Counted, separately**:
  - what the parse alone reaches;
  - what needs an English word, and so is not reached;
  - what the parser misread and the rules carried through, such as the brands and the passive of *drink* (L-M2.41).
- What Ein showed of itself, filed in the phase's evaluation; what a program costs the kernel (facts, a load's
  time).
- **EinAf's tests and the link check pass**; the gate is the coordinator's, on master after the merge.
