## Context

The instrument stage, and it comes first for the reason every measurement
phase in this repo has come first: a thing scheduled against what the engine
is *assumed* to have is scheduled wrong. The phase README's
[A1–A5 table](README.md#what-stage-a-asks-for-against-what-ships) was taken on
2026-08-23 from a reading of the crates; this stage re-takes it with
`file:line`, so that every cell is a citation and every *missing* becomes
either a task of [S2.1.2](s2.1.2_feedback_object.md) or a named owner in
another milestone. The census already knows where it will land — the
findings below are what the reading found — and the stage's job is to make
them checkable and to write the two lists the plan asks for and the tree
does not have: the **six outcomes** and the **six invariants**, as defined
behaviour.

What the reading found, in the order the plan asks:

- **The run record exists and is versioned.** `--json-summary` writes
  `ein-summary/1` ([`summary.rs:26`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-cli/src/summary.rs)):
  `schema`, `source`, `verdict {type, k, exhausted, unsat_core, solutions}`,
  `stats`, `root`, `config` — the models sorted by fact list, the core as
  s-expressions, `type` one of `Solution | Ambiguity | Contradiction |
  Aborted`. It is additive (never stdout, stderr or the exit code), order-free
  and self-describing, and it was written for a parity harness that has
  retired; what reads it now is `ein-cli/tests/summary_properties.rs`.
  `--events` writes `ein-events/1` ([`events.rs:32`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-infer/src/events.rs);
  [`events.md`](../../../docs/kernel/inference/events.md)), with `fire`
  carrying `rule`, `bindings`, `premises`, `derived` — the provenance DAG as
  a chronological log — and `verdict` carrying `core` and `models`.
- **There is no `unknown`.** [`verdict.rs:31-38`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-infer/src/verdict.rs)
  has three variants; `Answer::Aborted { reason }` sits outside the enum on
  purpose (*not a proven verdict — `solution_nodes == 0` there means
  unexplored, not proven unsatisfiable*, `verdict.rs:52-57`). The two caps
  say different things: `--max-time` / `--max-enterings` raise
  `SolveError::Budget`, print `** aborted: … **` and **exit 2** — the
  usage-error code, deliberately (`ein-cli/src/lib.rs:9-11`); `--max-set-size`
  with a non-empty frontier only clears `exhausted` (`ein-infer/src/solve.rs:1065-1069`)
  and reports **`Contradiction`, exit 0, empty core** — [Q-M1d.6](../../m1d_satisfiability/open_questions.md#q-m1d6--may-contradiction-be-said-with-exhausted--false)'s
  ten entries.
- **Only one error is typed.** `ParseError` is a struct — `file, line, col,
  context` (`ein-ir/src/parse.rs:33-40`). `KbLoadError(pub String)`
  (`from_ir.rs:35`), `LoadError` (`imports.rs:56`), `MacroError`
  (`macros.rs:26`) and `CompileError(pub String)` (`ein-infer/src/compile.rs:42`)
  are newtypes over the message; the enums that exist (`SaturateError`,
  `FireError`, `SolveError`) classify *runtime* failures, not a program's.
  No error carries a kind or an id, and [`defined_behaviour.md`](../../../docs/kernel/defined_behaviour.md)
  § 1 and § 4 pin the *messages* as normative.
- **Nothing computes a dependency graph, nothing diffs two models, and the
  provenance DAG is a Rust value rendered only to DOT.** `naf_deps.rs` is
  an advisory NAF map; `trace/relevance.rs` is a post-hoc backtrack over
  recorded firings, not a static query; `KbDotOpts::since` thickens a
  rendering. Of the DOT views in `ein-render/src/lib.rs:31-44`, four reach
  the CLI (`render rules | rule | constraints | lattice`).
- **The binary names its own instrument.** `ein --version` prints the engine,
  the events protocol, the container format, the features and the SHA-256 of
  the stdlib manifest as resolved (`version.rs:41-58`, `109-118`).

None of this is a defect of the kernel. It is an engine built to be
*compared to itself* — which it was, byte-exactly, for five phases — now
needed as an engine that *reports to someone else*. The difference is one
object, and [S2.1.2](s2.1.2_feedback_object.md) builds it.

## Tasks

### Task T2.1.1.1 — Re-take the table with citations

One row per A-item, the phase README's columns, every claim a `file:line` in
`ein.rs/crates/` or a page under `docs/kernel/`. The rows above are the
expected result; the task is to confirm them at the M1a close commit and to
find what the reading missed. Two things to look for specifically: whether
any field of the summary's `root` block (`facts_by_relation`, `plans`, the
`hypgen` counters) already answers a question the ladder will ask — *which
relations have no facts* is `unused_fact` / `dead_relation` halfway done —
and whether the `--dump-states` `summary.json` (`ein-render/src/dump/state.rs:5-16`),
which is a *different* file, carries anything the run summary does not.

### Task T2.1.1.2 — Two measurements the next stages depend on

1. **What the engine already knows about rules that never fire and
   relations never used.** The `fire` events say which rule applications
   happened; the set of compiled `(rule × activator)` units is what `compile`
   events (`ein-infer/src/engine.rs:196`) name; the difference is the dead
   set, computable from one `--events` run with no new kernel code. Check
   whether the `--relevant` backtrack's seed (`trace/relevance.rs:27-55`)
   can be read as *the relations the goal touches* — it is the nearest thing
   to A4's `unreachable_query`. Fixture: a program with one rule whose body
   mentions a relation with no facts; expected: the rule appears in `compile`
   and in no `fire`.
2. **Whether the parser accepts an unknown keyword on a fact.** The loader
   layers a fact by its keywords — `:source` → fact, `:rule` / `:using` →
   reasoning, else ontology — and [S2.2.1](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.1_contract.md)
   has to mark four more provenance categories on forms. Whether
   `(left-of A B :origin generated)` parses, loads, and survives the
   canonical dump is the difference between a keyword and a sidecar. Where to
   look: [`00_ebnf.md`](../../../docs/kernel/ir/03-ein-lang/00_ebnf.md),
   [`01_grammar.md`](../../../docs/kernel/ir/03-ein-lang/01_grammar.md),
   `ein-ir/src/parse.rs` and `from_ir.rs`; the fixture is the one-line file
   and its `ein saturate --dump`.

### Task T2.1.1.3 — The six outcomes and the six invariants, as defined behaviour

A new section of [`defined_behaviour.md`](../../../docs/kernel/defined_behaviour.md),
after § 6, in the page's own form — a claim, the engine's operational test,
the test that holds it. The outcomes, on the summary's fields:

| outcome | operational test | held by |
|---|---|---|
| invalid program | exit 1 with a parse / load / compile diagnostic | the `parse-negative`, `load-negative`, `compile-negative` corpus groups; `examples/broken/*.expected` |
| inconsistent theory | `Contradiction ∧ (exhausted ∨ unsat_core ≠ ∅)` | the `solve -e` cells; `ein-bugs/zebra2-bad.ein` |
| satisfiable, incomplete | `k ≥ 1` and no model binds the goal — the query axis, not the model axis; M1d's *incomplete* (a witness still owed) is the second source once the engine can say it | the summary's `solutions[].goal_bindings`; **no fixture today** — one is written |
| multiple complete models | `k ≥ 2` — two distinct sorted fact lists is a proof, exhausted or not; `exhausted` says whether `k` is the total | `solve -n 3` and `solve -e` cells; `zebra2-minus-15` (32) |
| unique complete model | `k = 1 ∧ exhausted` — the plan's *found AND exhausted* | `-e`; `summary_properties.rs`, where `k = solution_nodes` is held or joins the thirteen |
| unknown | `Aborted`, **or** `k = 1 ∧ ¬exhausted` (a model, uniqueness not established), **or** `Contradiction ∧ ¬exhausted ∧ core = ∅` | **M1d's** ([Q-M1d.6](../../m1d_satisfiability/open_questions.md#q-m1d6--may-contradiction-be-said-with-exhausted--false), [Q-M1d.1](../../m1d_satisfiability/open_questions.md#q-m1d1--may-the-search-stop-before-the-lattice-is-exhausted)); until then the derivation is M2's, in [S2.1.2](s2.1.2_feedback_object.md) T2.1.2.2, and the section says so |

The invariants, A2's six: *every derived fact is justified* (O6 — each
`fire` names its `premises`; the test to name or write: every `derived` in a
`fire` has premises that are earlier `load` or `fire` facts, over the
corpus's event streams); *every contradiction has a recorded derivation*
(`Contradiction ∧ exhausted ⇒ unsat_core ≠ ∅` — the core is the smallest
frontier over recorded derivations, [`glossary.md`](../../../docs/kernel/glossary.md);
the `¬exhausted` case is exactly where it fails today, and the section cites
Q-M1d.6 rather than hiding it); *every reported solution satisfies the
active constraints* (no `(false)` and no `(X, ¬X)` pair in `solutions[].facts`;
`-y` / `--lattice-sanity-check`; externally, [M1c](../../../docs/history/m1c_external_validation/README.md)'s
`:expect`); *unique = one ∧ exhausted*, *ambiguous = two distinct*,
*unsat = zero by exhaustion* — the three rows above. Where a test does not
exist, the section names the one-line consumer-side test to add under
`ein-cli/tests/`; it adds no kernel code.

## Notes

The stage deliberately does not ask whether the *semantics* in A1's list are
right — whether NAF at the world boundary is the right negation, whether the
lattice is the right search. Those are [F1b](../../followups/f1b_logical_formulation.md)'s
and [M1d](../../m1d_satisfiability/README.md)'s questions, and the plan's
instruction is to *freeze* the boundary, not to improve it. A census that
finds a semantic gap files it with the owner and moves on.
