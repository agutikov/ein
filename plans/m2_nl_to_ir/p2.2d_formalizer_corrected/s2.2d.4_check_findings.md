## Context

The contract says a statement's relation is its sentence's verb in its own voice, and nothing enforces it. A
throwaway script of the analysis, over the recorded step 1 of the sixteen texts, refused 8 of the model's 57
statements, and the five on the Zebra text are exactly the misread ones (`drinks` for *is drunk in*, `lives-in`
for *lives next to*); the other three are two of an inflection and one invented word. The script was kept nowhere.

## Acceptance

Each a finding of `einaf::check`, measured with no model on P2.2's records and held to none on the steps by hand,
the three prompt examples and the entry programs:

- **A statement's relation is not made of words of its own sentence, in their order**, a verb's forms allowed
  (*sits* for *does not sit*). The same on an answer query: its relations are the statements', or words of its
  question, which is where `gap`'s `sum-of` begins.
- **A rule that concludes the relation it matches with nothing that stops it**, reported where the answer query's
  class holds several rows; a note, since a transitive closure is such a rule
  ([E-124](../evaluation/answer/E-124-one-state-may-answer-a-question-of-one-value-with-a-row-a-pass.md): `bob1`'s
  1, 3 and 5).
- **An answer's row that is a statement of the text read back, while the program has rules**: a note, which is
  `contra`'s *Bob has 5 apples.* and, rightly, `bystander`'s 9.
- **A relation's `:why` that names an arrow past its declaration's last label**: the model saying the relation has
  more places than it declared.

For each: how many of the recorded programs carry it, which of them were scored right, and what it would have
said to a loop.
