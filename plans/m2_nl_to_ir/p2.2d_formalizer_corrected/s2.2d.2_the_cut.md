## Context

The steps write a *partial* ontology in step 1 and a *partial* theory in step 2. On S2.2.4's runs those two parts
are where the slips between steps are
([L-M2.25](../findings/einaf/L-M2.25-the-steps-partial-parts-are-written-twice-never-revised-and-a-step-early.md)),
and with no grammar the steps load fewer programs than the one call. What the partial parts buy is real and small:
a step 1 that loads alone, and the kernel's one judgement in the middle, which nobody reads in a one shot. **A
strict cut was not investigated**; the first re-plan's four passes were one, in another order, replaced by the
notes' steps before any run. What the records support is narrower than a cut: *each kind of form is written once,
by one step, and no step writes a list for a later one*.

## Acceptance

| the cut | step 1 | step 2 | step 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| today's | statements, the answer queries, a partial ontology | the ontology whole, imports, selection | the rest of the selection, rules, the mechanism |
| strict | statements and the queries | the ontology: declarations, memberships, kinds | the theory: imports, selection, rules, the mechanism |
| two steps | statements and the queries | the ontology and the theory, one call | |
| the one call | everything | | |

**With no model**, in EinAf's gate step:

- the six steps by hand cut strictly, as files a test judges: each cut loads or is refused for the reason the
  test states, and each complete program is the same forms as today's and so the same answer;
- the step grammars of the strict and the two-step cut, each accepting its part by hand. A strict step 2's is
  rendered on step 1's facts: one declaration for each relation in use, at the count it is used at, and one
  membership for each name, so that `undeclared` and `wrong-count` cannot be written and nothing is written twice;
- a later step may not restate an earlier one's fact.

**With the model**, a plan committed first: the four cuts on the sixteen texts, each step under its grammar; and
**the oracle form** on the six texts with steps by hand, step 2 given the hand's step 1 and step 3 the hand's
steps 1 and 2, which separates a step's own difficulty from what it inherits. Compared: programs that load,
outcomes, the check's findings by kind, the Zebra and seating programs against the hand's step by step, calls
stopped on a loop, tokens.

**The stage's report** is what the user rules Q-M2.36 on; the contract's § 3 says which cut is the formalizer's
once ruled.

## Tasks

### Task T2.2d.2.1 — The cuts by hand

Each of the six complete programs dealt into three strict parts, and into two. The analysis did four of them in
scratch files: at each cut the program loads, but `youngest`'s first, whose goal names a relation no statement
uses, and the kernel has nothing to say of facts alone or of an ontology alone
([E-114](../evaluation/hypotheses/E-114-a-partial-program-s-h-is-set-by-its-relations-count-of-places.md)). So
the stage states how a strict step 1 is judged (the parse and the check; the kernel from the step that brings the
theory), and what happens to a relation only a query reads (declared by step 2, concluded by step 3, never
imported under the same name).

### Task T2.2d.2.2 — The modes and their grammars

A mode beside `--mode steps|whole` in `einaf::formalize`, the grammars in `einaf::grammar::steps`, a template a
step of each cut. If a judgement in the middle is wanted under the strict cut, step 3 is two calls, the selection
and then the rules a marker asks for, and the kernel is asked between them: today's step 2, with nothing written
twice.

### Task T2.2d.2.3 — The runs, and the report

The plan, the runs, the tables written from the records by a script beside them. As a development number: the
sixteen texts are the ones the prompts were written on.

### Task T2.2d.2.4 — What the stage found

For the user, Q-M2.36 with the measurement beside it. For [P2.2b](../p2.2b_guided_thinking/README.md): which
step asks which of its questions under each cut. For [S2.6.4](../p2.6_ablations/s2.6.4_representation_ablations.md):
the oracle arms' first numbers, on six texts. Of Ein, to [the evaluation](evaluation/README.md): what the
kernel said of each part of a program judged alone.
