# S2.4.5 — Alternatives as hypotheses

## Context

A sentence the formalizer cannot resolve has two ways through the loop. It may be **committed** one way, and the
loop catches it when the kernel answers *several* or *inconsistent* and the feedback names the sentence. Or it
may be written as **both readings**, each a hypothesis, and the kernel keeps the readings that survive. These
are two strategies for one sentence, and which the loop should prefer is measured, not decided.

**The form is settled, and it is nothing new** (the user on Q8: *"there is no `hrule` anymore, rules emitting
open facts replaced them"*). The old stage chose between a `hypothesis-parse` form and `hrule`, wrote its
exclusion by a `(not h)` written back into root, and waited for obligations to say *at least one*. None of that
is left to do ([`audit_p2.4.md`](../analysis/audit_p2.4.md) § A.3, items 18 to 23):

- a reading is **a hypothesis**, `(open F)`, which a rule concludes or the program writes
  ([Q-M1f.567](../../../docs/decisions/hypotheses/Q-M1f.567-a-hypothesis-rule-is-a-rule-that-concludes-open.md));
  a program that writes `hrule` is refused at the parse;
- *at most one* is **a rule that concludes `(false)`**;
- *at least one* is **an obligation**, a rule whose whole `:assert` is `(open R)`
  ([Q-M1d.2](../../../docs/decisions/hypotheses/Q-M1d.2-an-obligation-is-a-rule-that-concludes-open-r.md)), for
  any number of readings;
- no route writes into root: a reading that dies is a dead node with a core, and nothing is written back.

**The corpus has the pattern**, for the readings of a word in a theory
([Q-M1f.332](../../../docs/decisions/corpus/Q-M1f.332-a-word-with-two-readings-always-has-one-chosen.md): *a word
with two readings always has one chosen*): [`readings.ein`](../../../examples/word_problems/readings.ein) holds
the readings of *need* as facts, proposes each by the hypothesis rule `read-need`, owes one by `reading-owed`
and refuses two by `one-reading`; [`boxes12.ein`](../../../examples/word_problems/boxes12.ein) is two states and
one answer, [`boxes13.ein`](../../../examples/word_problems/boxes13.ein) two states and two answers, and each
asks which reading a state rests on by a query of its own.

**The same pattern for one sentence of a text**, as a program that loads today (run at this re-plan; the
monotonicity check passes it):

```lisp
(relation reading Sentence Reading :why "{?4} is a reading of {?3}")
(relation reads Sentence Reading   :why "{?3} is read as {?4}")
(relation at Thing Place           :why "{?3} is at {?4}")

(reading s7 left   :source "sentence 7, read as: to the left of")
(reading s7 right  :source "sentence 7, read as: to the right of")
(at fox h2         :source "The fox is in the second house.")

(rule read-s7 ()                                   ; each reading is a hypothesis
  :match  (reading s7 ?m)
  :assert (open (reads s7 ?m))
  :why    "suppose sentence 7 is read as {?m}")
(rule one-reading ()                               ; at most one
  :match  (and (reads ?s ?a) (reads ?s ?b) (neq ?a ?b))
  :assert (false)
  :why    "{?s} is read one way: not both {?a} and {?b}")
(rule reading-owed ()                              ; at least one
  :match  (and (reading s7 ?any) (absent (and (reading s7 ?m) (reads s7 ?m))))
  :assert (open reads)
  :why    "a reading of sentence 7 is owed")
(rule left-impossible ()                           ; what one reading leads to
  :match  (and (reads s7 left) (at fox h2))
  :assert (false)
  :why    "read as left, sentence 7 puts the fox elsewhere than h2")

(query :goal (reads s7 ?m) :goal-text "sentence 7 is read as {?m}")
```

What the kernel says of it, and of two edits of it:

| the program | `--single` over all of R | over the maximal line |
|---|---|---|
| as above: one reading dies, one is owed | `Solution`, the state `(reads s7 right)`, *“sentence 7 is read as right”* | the same |
| without `reading-owed` | `Ambiguity`: ∅ is in R beside the surviving reading | `Solution` |
| without `left-impossible`: both readings alive | `Ambiguity`, the two readings | `Ambiguity` |

So **the obligation is what makes a pruned reading *one* over all of R**, and without it the answer depends on
the reading of *one answer* the user has not ruled on
([Q-M2.10](../open_questions.md#q-m210--what-does-ein-certifies-a-unique-model-mean-on-r)). The template writes
the obligation.

**What is left is the experiment**, and what it needs built: a way for the formalizer to write the pattern, a
way to choose the sentences, and a parameter.

## Tasks

- **T2.4.5.1 — The template.** The pattern as the facts pass instantiates it for a sentence: the reading facts,
  each quoting the sentence as its `:source`; the hypothesis rule, the refutation and the obligation; each
  reading's own facts or rules, written to hold under that reading (a rule that matches `(reads s k)`, as
  `need-whole-only` and `need-pack-all` do); a query that says which reading a state rests on. Two fixtures on
  seed texts, a *next to* sentence and an *or* sentence, each written by hand first and held to the kernel: the
  outcome of each is the outcome of the hand encoding that commits to the surviving reading. Whether the three
  shared rules are a module the benchmark's programs import (as `readings` is in the corpus) or are written into
  each program is decided here; a module lives with EinAf's benchmark and not in `stdlib/`.
- **T2.4.5.2 — Emission.** Which sentences get alternatives is the model's call by default: the facts-pass
  prompt says that a sentence with more than one plausible reading is written as readings, with the template.
  A lexical trigger (*or*, *either … or*, *maybe*, a trailing question mark, the old heuristic) is the
  parameter's other value. Both readings quote the sentence, and their category under
  [B2](../EinAf.md#b2-separate-source-facts-from-generated-knowledge) is *source-derived*; the three shared
  rules are *generic theory* where they are a module and *generated rules* where they are written in, which
  [S2.4.4](s2.4.4_faithfulness.md) lists and does not condemn.
- **T2.4.5.3 — The parameter.** `readings` (`commit`, `alternatives`) is a parameter of a run, beside `strategy`
  and independent of it: it changes what GENERATE and REPAIR may write, and no edge of the machine. In the old
  stage it was a third value of `strategy`, though its own task gave REPAIR the *repair* behaviour. Under
  `alternatives` an episode's record says which sentences were written as readings and which readings each
  final state rests on, read off the states' facts and the reading query's classes. The pre-registered reading
  for P2.6: `alternatives` wins only where it reaches the same outcome in fewer iterations **and** with no
  higher hallucination rate.
- **T2.4.5.4 — What it does to the answer, measured.** On the seed's texts under the replayer, then by hand with
  the model: how often alternatives are written for a text with no sentence of two readings (on the canonical
  Zebra text the expected count is none, and the count is recorded, not asserted); how often a final answer is
  *several* by readings that agree on the query (the `boxes12` case: two states, one class) against readings
  that do not (the `boxes13` case); and how often a program with readings is flagged.
- **T2.4.5.5 — The evaluation.** Into [the evaluation of Ein](../ein_evaluation.md): the form needed no change
  to Ein (E-6 holds or does not on sentences as it does on words); that *at least one* has to be written, and
  what the kernel says where it is not (∅ in R, with no word that a reading was left unchosen); what a state
  says of the reading it rests on, and what a dead reading's core names.

## Acceptance

- Under the replayer, `readings = alternatives` runs end to end on a fixture whose one sentence has two
  readings of which one dies: both are written, the outcome is *one* over all of R, and the record names the
  surviving reading from the state's facts and from the reading query's row.
- The same fixture with both readings alive ends at a fixed point in *several*, and the record says whether the
  answer query's classes agree.
- The template uses a hypothesis rule, a refutation and an obligation and nothing else: no new form and no
  change to the kernel, and the fixtures are monotone.
- `readings` is a field of every transition record, and `commit` runs the same fixtures to their outcomes by the
  loop's own repair.
- T2.4.5.4's counts are in the stage's record. Q8 stays open in the ledger until P2.6 compares the two; this
  stage writes there what the form and the measurement showed.

## Risks

- **A reading's rules can flag the program.** The static check's sites include a hypothesis rule that reads a
  relation a commitment can change, a rule that reads one under an `absent`, and an obligation whose trigger
  reads one ([`05-events.md`](../../../docs/ein/ein.rs/05-events.md), the `monotonicity` line), and a flagged
  program is never certified ([Q-M2.11](../open_questions.md#q-m211--what-is-a-flagged-program-to-the-loop)).
  The template is held monotone on its fixtures; what a model does to it is T2.4.5.4's third count.
- **Alternatives multiply H.** A reading is a candidate beside the puzzle's own: n sentences of two readings are
  up to 2ⁿ combinations on top of the search the puzzle needs, and a description of them may not finish in its
  budget. The loop's question is `--single`, which does not describe; the cost is measured on the seed set
  before the parameter is offered to P2.6.
- **A reading is an assumption with a quote.** Both readings cite the sentence, so the mechanical judge passes
  both, and a model can launder an invented constraint as *a reading*. The canonical judge sees it on synthetic
  instances; on the rest it is the judged sample's.
- **Two readings that agree are *several* to the loop.** The machine repairs on *several*, and a faithful
  formalizer returns the program unchanged; the episode ends at a fixed point with the outcome *several* and an
  answer query of one class. What that is scored as is the answer rule's
  ([S2.5.3](../p2.5_harness/s2.5.3_metrics.md)), which reads the classes over a complete set from the final
  description; the machine's own stop on such a program is `stop_on`'s third value
  ([S2.4.1](s2.4.1_state_machine.md)).
