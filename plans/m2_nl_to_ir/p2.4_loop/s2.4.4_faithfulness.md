## Context

The failure the research plan singles out: *Ein: theory is ambiguous → LLM invents an additional constraint →
Ein: unique.* The program that results is valid by every test the kernel has: it loads, it is not flagged, its
outcome is *one* under any reading. It is also false, because the English never said the thing that made it so.
Answer accuracy sees it only when the invented constraint happens to pick the wrong state, and on an instance
whose gold class is ambiguous there is no right state to be wrong about. **Faithfulness is therefore a metric of
its own, judged against the source on every program and every repair**, in both arrangements
([the frame](../README.md#the-arms)): the filter's `Tᵢ`, and each program a model passes to the tool.

**The kernel has no part in the judgement, and the judge has no part in the loop.** Nothing the kernel computes
says whether a fact was in the text. And no finding of the judge is rendered into a prompt or a function's
result: the model's only reviewer is the kernel, and a judge that fed back would be a second feedback channel
whose worth the ladder could not separate from Ein's.

### What a program gives the judge

A stated fact may carry its sentence, `:source "…"`, on any fact, a negation and an activator among them; it is
optional, and a rule and a declaration take a `:why` and no `:source`
([`02-syntax.md`](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/02-syntax.md);
[`audit_p2.4.md`](../analysis/audit_p2.4.md) § B.2). The contract makes the formalizer quote
([S2.2.1](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.1_contract.md)), and the corpus has the shape: the word problems of
`examples/word_problems/` carry each sentence as the `:source` of the facts it was encoded as. The Zebra programs
carry a label, `"condition (N)"`, and not the sentence. The rest of what the judge reads (which category of B2 a
form is in) is the contract's table of categories, which EinAf computes and keeps in the record beside the
program, since a fact takes no keyword of the formalizer's own
([the analysis](../analysis/README.md#p22--the-formalizer-audit)).

### The facts are judged at step 1

The formalizer makes a program in three steps, the facts first and in the text's own words
([the user's notes on P2.2](../p2.2_formalizer/README.md#formalization-in-steps-the-users-notes-on-the-whole-phase)),
and the record keeps each step's program ([S2.2.5](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.5_from_text.md)). So the facts are
judged once, where they are closest to the text: the mechanical judge's `:source` check and the categories of
step 1's program are the facts' judgement. Each later program, a later step's or a repair's, is judged for what it
adds, and for what it changed of step 1's statements: a step-1 statement it removed or changed is the note
`rewritten_fact`, counted, since the user's principle is that the facts change less than the theory and a repair
changes the theory first ([P2.4 § The theory before the facts](README.md#the-theory-before-the-facts)). The tool
has no steps: a program a call carries is judged whole, as before.

### Three judges, by what ground truth exists

| judge | its ground truth | what it finds | where it runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) mechanical** | the text itself, and B2's categories | a `:source` that is no sentence of the text; a *generated assumption*, which the contract makes an event the moment it appears: a fact stated as holding with no `:source`, and nothing else. What frames the program (a declaration, a query, a `(solve …)` form, a closure, a written `(open F)`) is never an event, by [the contract's table](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.1_contract.md); a source fact a repair **deleted**; and, noted and not condemned, each generated relation and rule. It cannot see a real sentence quoted for a fact the sentence does not state: (b) or (c) can | always, on every program and every diff, in both arrangements |
| **(b) canonical** | the instance's canonical program, which the generator wrote ([S2.3.2](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.2_generators.md) fixes the unit) | constraint **precision** (an invented constraint is a false positive) and **recall** (a dropped one a false negative), exactly | every synthetic instance |
| **(c) judged** | a person reading the text against the program | the invented constraint where there is a gold answer and no canonical program | a sample, sized in [P2.7](../p2.7_failure_scaling_generalization/README.md); and a model-assisted arm beside it (below) |

**The default answer to Q-M2.3**, the ledger's recommendation: (a) always; (b) wherever a canonical program
exists; (c) by a person, on a sample, as the ground truth where it does not; and a model-assisted judge only as a
second column, **trusted only with its agreement with the sample reported**, and used beyond the sample only if
that agreement reaches a threshold stated before it is measured (Cohen's κ of 0.85 on the binary *faithful or
not*, to be revised only upward). The reason is the plan's own distinction: the gap between
`P(correct | certified)` and `P(faithful | certified)` is the result, and a gap measured by a model judging a
model has an error bar nobody took. The model of the plan is local and small
([Q-M2.5](../open_questions/model/Q-M2.5-local-model-choice.md)); a judge is another value of the record's model
field, and whether one is worth building is decided when (c)'s sample exists.

### The canonical judge compares answers, not fact sets

The old task asked whether a generated rule changed *the closure*, by a digest of root's facts with and without
it. Root's fixpoint holds every activator as a fact of its rule's name, so adding any activator changes it
whether or not it derives anything ([`audit_p2.4.md`](../analysis/audit_p2.4.md) § B.2). The comparison is
S2.4.1's semantic identity: a form is **redundant** where the canonical program with it answers as the canonical
program does (the same outcome, the same states over the candidates' relations, the same classes of each query),
and **invented** where it does not. A fact or an activator is compared by its canonical text, `:source` and
`:why` aside; a rule cannot be, since two texts can be one rule, and is compared by what it does to the answer.

### Faithfulness events, and notes

Each finding is one entry of the transition record's `faithfulness` list, or of a tool call's: the judge (`a`,
`b`, `c`), a kind, the form, a detail. **An event counts against the program**: `unfound_source`,
`generated_assumption`, `deleted_source_fact` (a); `invented_constraint`, `dropped_constraint` (b);
`judged_unfaithful` (c). **A note does not**: `generated_relation`, `generated_rule`, `redundant_form`, and
`rewritten_fact`, a step-1 statement a later program removed or changed, counted per run and per level beside the
hallucination rate; where a sentence is left with no fact at all, the event is `deleted_source_fact`. The line
between the two is the contract's table of categories ([S2.2.1](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.1_contract.md)), and a
final program is *faithful* in the metrics where it has no event
([S2.5.3](../p2.5_harness/s2.5.3_metrics.md)). A reader of a transcript sees which repair invented what.

### What S2.5.3 reads

The metrics are defined once, in [S2.5.3](../p2.5_harness/s2.5.3_metrics.md), and computed from records; this
stage hands them their inputs and says what each can and cannot carry:

- **faithfulness** (H2), of a final program: no event. Beside it (b)'s precision and recall where a canonical
  program exists, and (c)'s judgement where one does not, each column naming its judge: an event of (a) alone
  can make a program unfaithful, and no event of (a) does not make it faithful.
- **the hallucination rate** (H3), of the repairs of a run, by feedback level, because G5 expects it to rise
  with F4: what a repair added, with its category and its events, is in the transition record.
- **`P(faithful | certified)`**, under each reading the record carries; a flagged program is certified under
  none ([Q-M2.10](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.10-what-does-ein-certifies-a-unique-model-mean-on-r.md),
  [Q-M2.11](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.11-what-is-a-flagged-program-to-the-loop.md)).
- **For the tool**: the same over the last program the model ran, which is the episode's final program, and the
  count of episodes in which it ran none, where there is nothing to judge. Whether the final answer is what that
  program printed is [S2.4.6](s2.4.6_ein_as_a_tool.md)'s field and is not faithfulness to the source.

## Tasks

- **T2.4.4.1 — The mechanical judge.** The `:source` check over the program's stated facts, read from the parsed
  program: present; found in the text exactly, then with whitespace and case normalised; a quote that matches
  nothing is an event. It is the first finding of the contract's check
  ([S2.2.4](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.4_passes.md), T2.2.4.6), computed once and read by both, and for the facts it
  is taken on step 1's program. The categories, by the contract's table, and the generated assumptions among them.
  The deleted-source check, over S2.4.2's diff; and the rewritten-fact check, each program's statements against
  step 1's by canonical text and `:source`, the note `rewritten_fact` for each step-1 statement removed or changed.
  A `:why` and a `:goal-text` are the model's own words and in no category; the contract leaves to this stage
  whether a judge reads them. The default: (a) notes a `:goal-text` whose words, outside its references, hold a
  name the program gives an object, and (c)'s reader is shown the texts whole. It
  runs after CHECK on every program of the filter and on every program a tool call carries, including one the
  kernel refused, as far as it parses.
- **T2.4.4.2 — The canonical judge.** The instance's canonical forms against the program's, by S2.3.2's unit;
  precision, recall, and the false positives and negatives as events; for a rule and for any form the text
  comparison cannot place, the comparison of answers above, through the bounded kernel call
  ([S2.4.1](s2.4.1_state_machine.md)). No canonical program of the benchmark is flagged
  ([Q-M2.11](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.11-what-is-a-flagged-program-to-the-loop.md)); a judged program that is,
  with or without the form, is reported as flagged and the form is not classified.
- **T2.4.4.3 — The judged arm.** The form a person fills for an instance of the sample (faithful or not, the
  offending form, one line why), and what the person is shown: the text, the program, and the program's own
  sentences, which is where the reasoning as a program
  ([the note](../README.md#the-reasoning-as-a-program-the-users-idea-2026-11-11)) meets this stage. The
  model-assisted judge behind one interface, a direct judgement of text against program, or a back-translation
  and an entailment check ([F16](../../followups/f16_autoformalization/ideas.md) cites the practice); the κ
  against the sample; the threshold and the rule, written at the head of the module before any agreement is
  taken.
- **T2.4.4.4 — Events and fixtures.** The event's type in the transition record and in a tool call's record.
  Fixtures, over a seed instance and its canonical program: the canonical program (no event of (b), precision
  and recall 1); the same with an activator its own activators already bring (redundant, not invented); the same
  with one fact that quotes a sentence the text does not hold (`unfound_source`, and a false positive); the same
  with one fact that quotes nothing and the catalogue does not list (`generated_assumption`); the same with one
  clue's fact removed (`dropped_constraint`); the same with one step-1 fact changed by a repair while its
  sentence keeps a fact (`rewritten_fact`, a note, and no event of (a)); and, on an instance whose gold class is
  ambiguous, a
  canned loop at F2 whose repair adds a constraint and ends in *one* (the acceptance case). Which activator the
  redundant fixture adds is fixed against the seed's canonical program: `zebra2.ein` writes
  `(bijective drink-loc)` and no `(functional drink-loc)`
  ([`audit_p2.4.md`](../analysis/audit_p2.4.md) § A.5, item 34).
- **T2.4.4.5 — One interface, for both arrangements.** A judge is a function of a text and a program, and
  where they exist of the program before it and of the canonical program; it knows nothing of the machine that
  produced them. The filter calls it at CHECK and at every REPAIR. The tool calls it on every program a call
  carries, which [S2.4.6](s2.4.6_ein_as_a_tool.md) wires: there no contract is enforced, so how many of a
  program's stated facts carry a `:source` at all is reported first, beside the events.
- **T2.4.4.6 — Q-M2.3 decided, and the evaluation.** The decision is written beside the question in
  [the ledger](../open_questions/README.md), with what the fixtures and the first sample showed. Into
  [the evaluation of Ein](../evaluation/README.md): what Ein gives a judge and what it does not (a sentence on a
  fact and none on a rule; no mark of where a form came from, so that EinAf keeps a table beside the program; a
  written `:rule` or `:using` that the loader stores and checks none of,
  [F24](../../followups/f24_written_provenance.md)); and the corpus's own instance of a right answer by an
  undetermined reading, `examples/word_problems/boxes12.ein`, whose two states give one number.

## Acceptance

- The mechanical judge runs on every program and every diff of every run, in both arrangements, and its events
  and notes are in the transcript. On the benchmark's canonical programs it reports no event (see the first
  risk).
- On a synthetic instance whose gold class is ambiguous, a loop at F2 whose final program is *one* carries at
  least one `invented_constraint` event: **the E3 failure is detected by construction**, on the benchmark's own
  ground truth, before any table is drawn.
- (b)'s precision and recall are 1 for the canonical program itself, fall by exactly one false positive when one
  invented constraint is added by hand, and do not move for the redundant fixture: the fixtures that pin the
  unit.
- No prompt and no function result holds a judge's finding: a test over the rendered texts of the fixtures.
- The model-assisted arm, if built, has its agreement with the sample written in the stage's record before any
  table uses it; below the threshold it stays out of the tables.
- Q-M2.3 is answered beside its entry.

## Risks

- **A `:source` is a quote in a generated program and a label in the hand encodings.** `zebra2.ein` writes
  `:source "condition (1)"`. The span check is for programs the formalizer wrote; a canonical program of the
  benchmark either quotes the sentence or carries a map from label to sentence in its instance's metadata, and
  [S2.3.1](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.1_families_and_seed_set.md) is where that is chosen. Noted here so that the
  judge does not report an anchor for being older than the contract.
- **The judge is not a gate on the loop.** A run that ends in *one* with an `invented_constraint` event is
  recorded and counted. Stopping on a faithfulness event would hide the rate the milestone exists to measure.
- **A redundant form and an invented one are told apart by the kernel's answer**, so the comparison inherits
  S2.4.1's choice of identity and its blind spot, a rename; and it costs kernel runs per form. It runs on final
  programs and on fixtures, not on every repair, unless the count of forms it cannot place by text is small.
- **Precision against one canonical program punishes a second correct formalization.** Two programs can state a
  text faithfully with different relations. The unit is S2.3.2's; what this stage records is how often a program
  with the gold answer and the gold class scores below 1, which is the size of that error.
- **A person's reading is the ground truth and is one person's.** The sample's form keeps the offending form and
  the reason, so that a second reader can disagree with a line and not with a number.
