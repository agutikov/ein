## Context

The failure mode the plan singles out: *Ein: theory is ambiguous → LLM
invents an additional constraint → Ein: unique.* The resulting theory is
internally valid — the kernel certifies `k = 1 ∧ exhausted` and every
invariant holds — and semantically false, because the English never said
the thing that made it unique. No metric the kernel can compute sees this;
answer accuracy sees it only when the invented constraint happens to pick
the wrong model, and on a benchmark instance whose gold verdict is
*ambiguous* there is no "right model" to be wrong about. **Faithfulness is
therefore a first-class metric, judged against the source on every repair**,
and this stage builds the judge and decides
[Q-M2.3](../open_questions.md#q-m23--what-is-the-unit-of-faithfulness-without-a-gold-theory).

### Three judges, by what ground truth exists

| judge | ground truth | what it catches | where it runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) mechanical** | the text itself, and B2's categories | a fact whose `:source` span does not occur in the text; a form in a *generated* category (auxiliary relation, inference rule, assumption) — listed, not condemned; a source fact **deleted** by a repair; a `:source` reused for a fact the sentence does not state (the span check cannot see this one — (b) or (c) can) | always, on every program and every diff |
| **(b) canonical** | the generator's theory — the instance's facts and activators as canonical forms ([S2.3.2](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.2_generators.md) fixes the unit) | constraint **precision** (an invented constraint is a false positive) and **recall** (a dropped one a false negative), exactly | every synthetic instance |
| **(c) judged** | a human reading of the text against the theory | the invented constraint on an external instance, where there is an answer and no theory | a sample, sized in [P2.7](../p2.7_failure_scaling_generalization/README.md); and a model-assisted arm — an LLM-as-judge given the text and the theory, or back-translation of the theory to English and an NLI check, which the autoformalization literature uses ([F16](../../followups/f16_autoformalization/ideas.md): Lean Workbook) — **trusted only with its agreement to the human sample reported**, and used beyond the sample only if that agreement is at or above a threshold stated before it is measured (0.85 Cohen's κ on the binary *faithful / not*, to be revised only upward) |

**This stage decides Q-M2.3 as its recommendation says:** (a) and (b)
always; (c) human on the sample as ground truth; the model-assisted arm is
a second column, never the only one. The reason is the plan's own
distinction: the gap between `P(correct | certified)` and
`P(faithful | certified)` is *the* result, and a gap measured by a model
judging a model is a gap with an unknown error bar.

### Faithfulness events

Each judge's finding is one event in the transition record's
`faithfulness[]` — `{judge: a|b|c, kind, form, detail}` with `kind ∈
{unsourced_fact, deleted_source_fact, generated_relation, generated_rule,
generated_assumption, invented_constraint, dropped_constraint,
judged_unfaithful}` — so the metrics are counts over the transcript and a
reader can see *which* repair invented *what*. The events are also what
the demo shows when the loop goes wrong
([P2.10](../p2.10_result_artifact_demo/README.md)).

### The metrics handed to S2.5.3

Defined here, computed by [S2.5.3](../p2.5_harness/s2.5.3_metrics.md)'s
one module from the records:

- **semantic faithfulness** (H2) — per final theory: (b)'s precision and
  recall where a canonical theory exists; else (c)'s verdict; with (a)'s
  events as a third, always-present column. A theory with any
  `unsourced_fact` or `deleted_source_fact` event is *unfaithful* under (a)
  regardless of (b) / (c).
- **semantic hallucination rate** (H3) — per run: the fraction of REPAIR
  transitions that added at least one `invented_constraint` or
  `unsourced_fact`; reported per feedback level, because G5 expects it to
  rise with F4.
- **`P(faithful | Ein certifies unique)`** — over final theories with
  `unique ∧ search_complete`, the fraction faithful under (b) where
  available and (c) otherwise, with the denominator and the judge named.

## Acceptance

- The mechanical judge runs on every program and every diff in every run,
  and its events appear in the transcript; on the seed set's hand-written
  anchors it reports nothing (their `:source` spans are quotes — see Notes).
- On a synthetic `ambiguous` instance, a loop at F2 whose final theory is
  `unique` carries at least one `invented_constraint` event — **the E3
  failure is detected by construction** on the benchmark's own ground
  truth, before any table is drawn.
- (b)'s precision and recall on the synthetic anchors are 1.0 for the
  canonical program itself and drop by exactly one false positive when one
  invented activator is added by hand — the fixture that pins the unit.
- The model-assisted arm, if built, has its agreement to the human sample
  measured and written here before any table uses it; a κ below the
  threshold keeps it out of the tables and in this file's Notes.
- Q-M2.3 is marked decided beside its entry, with this stage as the
  verdict.

## Tasks

### Task T2.4.4.1 — The mechanical judge

`einaf/faith/mechanical.py`: the `:source` span check (exact, then
whitespace- and case-normalised; a quote that matches nothing is an event),
the category listing from [S2.2.1](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.1_contract.md)'s
sidecar, the deleted-source check over [S2.4.2](s2.4.2_repair.md)'s diff.
Runs in the machine after CHECK and after every REPAIR.

### Task T2.4.4.2 — The canonical judge

`einaf/faith/canonical.py`: the instance's canonical forms against the
theory's, by the unit S2.3.2 fixes (a fact form or an activator form, by
canonical text, `:source` and `:why` stripped); precision, recall, the
lists of false positives and negatives as events. A generated `rule` form
is compared by its closure, not its text: the harness saturates the
canonical program with and without the rule and asks whether the closure
changed (`state_digest` from [S2.4.1](s2.4.1_state_machine.md)) — a rule
that derives nothing new is *redundant*, not *invented*, and is an event of
its own kind.

### Task T2.4.4.3 — The judged arm

`einaf/faith/judged.py`: the sample reader's form (a YAML per instance:
faithful yes / no, the offending form, one line why) and the model-assisted
judge behind one interface with two implementations — direct judgement
with the text and the theory; back-translation plus NLI — each producing
the same binary plus a rationale; the κ computation against the sample;
the threshold and the rule written at the top of the file.

### Task T2.4.4.4 — Events and fixtures

The `faithfulness[]` schema in the transition record; fixtures under
`einaf/tests/fixtures/faith/`: the canonical Zebra program (no events), the
same with one `(functional drink-loc)` duplicated (redundant, not invented),
the same with an invented `(nation-loc Norwegian House-3)` quoting a
sentence that does not exist (unsourced), the `ambiguous` instance with the
F2 loop's invented constraint (the acceptance case).

## Notes

- **`:source` is a quote in generated programs and a label in the hand
  encodings.** `zebra2.ein` writes `:source "condition (1)"`; the contract
  ([S2.2.1](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.1_contract.md)) makes the formalizer
  quote the sentence. The mechanical judge's span check applies to
  generated programs; the benchmark's hand-written anchors
  ([S2.3.1](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.1_families_and_seed_set.md)) either
  quote the sentence or carry a label → sentence map in `meta.json`, and
  that stage is where the choice is made — noted here so the judge does not
  flag an anchor for being older than the contract.
- The judge is not a gate on the loop. A run that ends `unique` with an
  `invented_constraint` event is **recorded as such and counted**; stopping
  the loop on a faithfulness event would hide the rate the milestone
  exists to measure.
