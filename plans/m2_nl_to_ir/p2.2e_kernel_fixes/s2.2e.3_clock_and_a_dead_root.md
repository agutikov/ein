## Context

`ein solve program.ein --single -T 5` on the model's Zebra program returns after 72 s, *Contradiction*: one
saturation, root's. The same program with `(config :record-alternative-justifications false)` returns in 63 ms.
What runs between two reads of the clock is few firings, each recording every justification of a link several
rules derive. EinAf's bounded call returns as late, so a loop's budget of kernel time is not what it sets, and
P2.2's acceptance, *a program comes back not decided within the bound*, holds as worded and leaks beside it.

## Acceptance

- Where the 72 s go is measured (`-t`, a profile) and written down: which work, between which two reads of the
  clock.
- The clock is read inside that work, so that the run returns *not decided* within its bound and a stated
  tolerance; the abort is the value it is everywhere else.
- **No answer moves**: with no clock the program answers as before, in the same time within the measurement's
  noise; the corpus's saturations cost what they cost, through `bench_env.sh`.
- A fixture of the kind (a root that dies on a link several rules derive) under the bound, in the gate, small
  enough for it.
- The page of EinAf that lists what the bounds do not stop is re-read in the same batch.

### T2.2e.3.1 — Where the time goes. **Not root's saturation: root's core, which `--single` prints nowhere.**

E-126 read the 72 s as root's saturation recording every justification. It is not: `ein saturate` of the program
makes its 22 969 firings, 1 962 productive, in 0.1 s, every justification recorded. The `perf` events cannot be opened
on this host (`perf_event_paranoid` 2 and the sandbox), so the profile is `gdb`'s: eight stacks of
`ein solve program.ein --single -T 5` (a `--profile profiling` build), one every four seconds, every one in
`explain::contradiction_frontier`'s label propagation (`propagate` → `fold` → `union`, `minimise` and its sort)
called from `region::node_in_region` under `explore::Space::dead_root`. **Between which two reads**: the last read is
at root's saturation's final quiescence (`Saturator::step`), and the next would have been the route's `afford` before
another node, which a dead root never takes; root's core is searched between them, with no read at all.

The search, counted by a build that printed each round (not kept):

| round | facts in the wave | justifications folded | environments made | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | 599 to 1 152 | 13 646 to 19 388 | 745 to 19 880 | 0.4 to 2.4 ms |
| 5, 6 | 1 048, 1 145 | 18 884, 19 360 | 0.13 M, 1.2 M | 28 ms, 0.47 s |
| 7, 8 | 1 162, 1 164 | 19 395, 19 399 | 5.3 M, 11.3 M | 2.6 s, 5.7 s |
| 9 to 13 | 1 061 to 1 163 | 19 014 to 19 397 | 17.0 M to 24.0 M | 8.6 s to 11.9 s |
| 14, 15 | 633, 50 | 12 639, 951 | 17.2 M, 1.1 M | 8.5 s, 0.57 s |

The closure is 1 216 facts and 55 leaves; the search reaches its fixpoint in 15 of its 64 rounds, and from round 7 a
premise's fold is the cap's 64 × 64 = 4 096 unions; the slowest single fact of a wave is 48 ms. The core is two facts,
`(instance GreenHouse House)` and `(type Color Attribute)`, *cut at its budget of 64 environments per fact*, refuted
by `std.slots.slot-no-fill` (`ein solve program.ein`, 72 s). The budget bounds the search's size and not its time.

**And it is taken where no surface prints it.** Of the questions, a description and `--no-search` print root's core;
`--single`, `-n`, `--minimal`, `--maximal` and `--count` print none (their summaries write `region.core` `null`), and
take it all the same: the node function takes a dead node's core `if dead && (axes.cores || facts.is_empty())`. That
is against [Q-M1f.29](../../../docs/decisions/read-out/Q-M1f.29-a-question-that-prints-no-core-writes-a-dead.md) and
[Q-M1e2.31](../../../docs/decisions/answer/Q-M1e2.31-each-question-about-r-does-the-least-work-it.md), and it is what
the design page and `region::Cores`' note state: **filed as [E-130](evaluation/bounds/E-130-a-question-that-prints-no-core-takes-a-dead-roots-core.md)**,
not fixed here (below, Defaults 1).

**The smallest program of the shape** is `ein-cli/tests/bounded/dead_link.ein`: a line of thirty towns, two roads
between each two neighbours, each road a link, a link symmetric and transitive (`chain` with a `neq`), the two ends
apart, `(solve :no-search :expect (false))`. Root saturates in 25 290 firings, 872 productive, 30 ms (`ein saturate`);
its core's search takes 17.1 s under `ein solve --no-search`, and with the lever the run takes 29 ms. The family grows
smoothly with the line (on the release binary, `--no-search`, the search cut at the environments from seven towns
on): 10 towns 0.14 s, 12 0.33 s, 14 0.67 s, 16 1.2 s, 20 3.1 s, 30 17.1 s, 40 40.2 s; at ten towns a third road
0.28 s and a fourth 0.33 s.

### T2.2e.3.2 — The clock there

**The read.** The search takes a deadline (`explain::contradiction_frontier_by`) and counts its work in units, a fact
its closure reads, a justification folded, and each environment a fold or a minimisation makes; every
`CLOCK_EVERY` = 4 096 units it reads the clock, and past the deadline it returns `explain::Deadline`, which
`SolveError` takes as `SolveError::Deadline`, the clock's abort wherever it falls: one line on stderr, nothing on
stdout, exit 2, a summary with no answer whose one diagnostic is the deadline's; `ein test`'s `ERROR`; `ein oracle`'s
*before the cube was done*. The deadline is the one the node's saturation took (`Snapshot::bound`: the thread's
`saturator::bounded` switch, else the snapshot's), so the node function on a worker, `cores_after` after a walk, and
`Nodes::refutation` under the read-out's bound (`--trace`, `--dump-answer`) all read it. With no deadline, the
search is the search: `contradiction_frontier`, `explain` and `support_frontier` call it with none, and every reader
that takes no bound (`ein render h-space`, the shape golden, `ein render lattice`) is as it was.

**Nothing is left half-done.** The search reads the KB and writes nothing; what a stopped search had built is dropped
with it, and the run stops as the clock stops it anywhere. So the work can be interrupted, and E-126 closes.

**The tolerance.** An environment costs about half a microsecond where the labels are full (this program's round 13:
24.0 M in 11.9 s), so 4 096 units are about 2 ms, and one tick adds at most 4 096; a run stops within some
milliseconds of its clock. On the program, every question under `-T 5` returned in 5.01 s, exit 2, *aborted:
--max-time 5 s reached before the question was decided* (`--single`, describe, `--no-search`, `--count`, `-n 2`,
`--minimal`, `--maximal`); `dead_link.ein` under `-T 0.3` in 0.307 to 0.31 s on every question, `ein oracle`,
`ein test`, `--events`, `--dump-answer`, `--dump-lattice` and `--trace`, where `44e3bef1`'s binary answered its
description after 17.2 s. `ein render lattice`, which takes no clock, still takes 17.5 s, as it did.

**What the clock costs**, on AC, pinned to one P-core thread (`utils/bench_env.sh --cores PT:1`, cpu0; governor
`powersave`, epp `balance_performance`, turbo on), *before* the binary or probe built from `44e3bef1` and *after*
the fix, interleaved:

| what | before | after, no clock | after, a clock not reached |
|---|---|---|---|
| the model's program, `ein solve --single`, two rounds ([`single.sh`](probes/clock-and-a-dead-root/single.sh)) | 72.03 s, 72.01 s | 72.04 s, 72.85 s | 72.28 s, 72.26 s (`-T 1000`) |
| every corpus program that is not `slow`, 740, described by the library with every visited dead node's core taken (`Cores::ALL`; 261 hold a dead member of N), three rounds ([`core_clock_probe.rs`](probes/clock-and-a-dead-root/core_clock_probe.rs), [`corpus.sh`](probes/clock-and-a-dead-root/corpus.sh)) | 14.45 s, 14.40 s, 14.44 s | 14.51 s, 14.26 s, 14.26 s | 14.35 s, 14.28 s, 14.25 s (a day away) |

Within the noise in every column; the corpus's three runs answered alike, program for program (the same dead count
and outcome). The model's program's read-out is byte for byte the same in all six runs but for the header that echoes
`-T 1000`. The second *after, no clock* run of the program overlapped the runs that sized the fixture, on other cores.
The corpus's saturations are not touched: `Saturator::resume` reads the same bound through `Snapshot::bound`.

**The fixture**, in the gate: `the_clock_ends_a_dead_roots_core_search` (`ein-cli`) runs `dead_link.ein` under
`-T 0.3` on seven questions, `ein oracle` and `ein test`, each the clock's abort within the test's patience, the
library's `explore::run` under a 200 ms deadline under describe and `--single`, `SolveError::Deadline`; and the same
program with the lever (a scratch copy), which no clock stops, answering *Contradiction* under `--single` and its
claim held under `ein test`. `a_deadline_stops_a_core_search_and_one_not_reached_moves_nothing` (`ein-infer`) holds
the search itself: with no deadline and a deadline a day away the same explanation, on `SHARED_OPTIMUM`,
`core-search-cut.ein` and `zebra2-bad.ein`; a deadline already past stopping `zebra2-bad.ein`'s search, which does
more than 4 096 units, at its first read, and leaving the two smaller ones, which do fewer, to end; and
`dead_link.ein`'s saturated root, its search alone stopped 200 ms on. Not a corpus program: a description prints its
core, which no sweep waits 17 s for.

### Defaults taken

1. **E-130 is filed, not fixed.** The 72 s under `--single` are a core no surface prints, against Q-M1f.29; a fix
   would make the stage's own acceptance (*not decided* within the bound) moot for every question but a description
   and `--no-search`. It touches which cores each route takes (the modes' `Space`, `--count`'s delegation to
   describe's route, the design page's sentence), which is not the clock, so it is the coordinator's and the user's
   to place: a stage of this phase or the next that needs it.
2. **The clock's unit is the search's work, not its wall**: a fact read, a justification folded, an environment made,
   4 096 a read, so the cost of a read is a share of a few milliseconds' work and a run stops within as long; a
   power of two, as the saturator's `CLOCK_EVERY` is.
3. **No decision is written, and Q-M2.16's text is not edited**: it decides that a run the clock stops is no answer,
   wherever; where the clock is read is the pages'. The help of `-T` says it, and its golden is re-banked.
4. **The fixture lives with the clock's others** (`ein-cli/tests/bounded/`), not in the corpus, whose describing
   sweeps would wait 17 s for its core; its twin with the lever is written by the test into a scratch directory.
5. **EinAf's test reads Ein's fixture** by its path in the repository, rather than a copy under `einaf/tests/fixtures/`.
6. **The probes are kept** under [`probes/clock-and-a-dead-root/`](probes/clock-and-a-dead-root/): the corpus probe
   (an example of `ein-infer`, copied in to run, as `corpus.sh` does in a worktree of the commit before) and the
   program's timing script; what they write goes under `out/`, which git ignores. The measurement above built the
   *before* probe from `44e3bef1`'s sources in this checkout, the stage's changes set aside for the build.
