## Context

The page states `slot-spatial` as $S(a, b) \iff S(\operatorname{slot}(a), \operatorname{slot}(b))$, so with
`next-to` irreflexive two values `(next-to a b)` relates never share a slot. The two pruning rules exclude every
position that is not an `S`-neighbour of the partner's *except the partner's own*, by a guard `(neq ?p_o ?p1)`, so
a state that puts both in one house is not refuted. With sentence 15 dropped, `zebra`, `zebra3.1`, `zebra3.2` and
`zebra3.3` list 55 states and `zebra2` and `zebra3.4`, over `std.bijection` and their own rules, 32; of the 55, 23
put Chesterfields in the fox's house or Kools in the horse's. With the two guards taken out of a copy of the
library, the same files list 32, and the full puzzle still answers `Solution`. Not a row of the wrong-answer
register: the kernel answers the rules as written, and what is wrong is the rules against their page.

### T2.2e.2.2 — The guard

**What it was written for.** `std.slots` was written on 2026-08-17 (`26141452`) as the generalisation of
`zebra2.ein`'s own spatial rules, and its two pruning rules took `disjunctive-prune-*`'s guard as it then stood,
`(neq ?h_other ?h1)`, read as *a value cannot exclude its own house*. Three days later `a27c363c` took the guard out
of `zebra2.ein`'s rules: the `absent` after it decides that case, and decides it right where `?S` is irreflexive, so
the guard *"would be redundant where ?S is reflexive and wrong where it is not"* (the comment the file still
carries); `zebra2-minus-15.ein` went from 55 states to 32 and `examples/features/09_adjacent_via_same_house.ein`
became its fixture. `std.slots` kept its copy. No test of the module read the guard either way: taking it out of
each rule was a banked mutation survivor (`slot-prune-fwd::drop-neq`, `slot-prune-bwd::drop-neq`).

**Every `S`, with no condition; the rule knows by reading `S`.** The page states the congruence with no condition
on `S`, and the `absent` reads `S` at the placed end's position as it reads it at every other: where `S` relates the
position to itself the partner keeps it, where it does not the partner is excluded from it, each what the page's
statement says. So the page needs no condition and the rules read none; the fix is the two `neq` taken out.
`14_prune_placed_position.ein` holds the irreflexive case (`next-to`: the placed end's seat excluded, and nothing
else of the module activated), `15_prune_reflexive.ein` the reflexive one (`near`, each seat near itself: the seat
kept), each claiming every exclusion through `probe-excluded`; 14 fails on the library before the fix, 15 holds on
both and fails on a rule that excludes the seat whatever `S` is.
