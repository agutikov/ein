## Context

**The order.** Ein reads a file whole before it loads any form, and on S2.2.0a's programs an answer does not
depend on the order of the forms: the six Zebra encodings in synthesis order and in theory-first order answer
alike under every question. The formalizer assembles a program in the order its steps make it. No page of Ein
says an answer may not depend on that order, and no test of Ein writes a program's forms in another one.

**The table.** The parser's banked table of refused shapes has a row for a string glued to a name and none for a
string glued to a block comment, and none for a form whose closing paren a line comment takes. A second reader of
the syntax held to the table and the corpus alone would accept both.

### T2.2e.4.1 — The order's decision

**What *the same answer* is**, as S2.2.0a's probes compared it (E-112: every question's outcome under each reading,
the answer query's classes and rows, the states, H line for line), written into the decision as: R, its states and
its two counts, root's standing and what root owes, every question's outcome (the description, `--single`, `-n N`,
each line, `--count`, `--no-search`), every query's classes and rows over all of R, every claim's verdict, and H.
The subject is `language`: what a program is, its forms as a set of objects, rather than the answer's rules or the
engine's; the user's two reasons are the language's (a form its own object, a name one object, Q-M1f.380) and the
load's.

**What may move**, each checked on the binary before it was written (`ein.rs/target/gate/ein` at `e2b7c0c9`), and
the decision says each: the trace, the event stream, the order rules fire in and so a core (§ 1.3 already, Q-M1f.6);
**a listing kept in the written order**, root's owed instances (`likes owes Ann a Food.` before or after Carl's);
**which member of R a question that asks for some and not all meets first**: the obligation tree branches on the owed
instance written first among equals, so with two people, two foods and one food each, `-n 1` lists `(likes Ann Soup)
(likes Carl Stew)` in one order and `(likes Ann Stew) (likes Carl Soup)` in the other, every other question alike;
**the `:source` a fact stated twice keeps**, the first (Q-M1f.139); and **the `(config …)` a run takes of several**,
the last, whole (`06-config.md` § 1), which no flag's answer reads (Q-M1e2.30). § 1.3 gains a bullet with the first
three; the decision has all five.

**Where a run falls short of it today, found while writing it.** The ruling's premise, *"nothing depends on lines
order"*, holds of the load and not of a saturation's boundary, which admits the rules waiting on an `absent` in their
written order within each half of the agenda: where a rule's `absent` reads what another such rule concludes, the
one written first can decide the fixpoint. Ein's reasoning pages say so (`03-absent-and-naf.md` § What is not
provided, `08-monotonicity.md` § 8.3, and Proposition 2 of `03-transformations.md` proves the guarantee only for a
program order-free at the boundary), and the register has it at root as `W8/fifo-gap-root` (owner S1n.2.1) since
M1f's close. Probed again here ([`probes/order-of-forms/`](probes/order-of-forms/), `run.sh`): the reader written
first answers the query `(p ?x)` with `?x = k`, the producer first with no row, under every question but `--count`,
both read-outs saying `lattice monotone (the static check)`. The decision states the guarantee as ruled and says
this: *of a program the static check passes, such a run is a wrong answer by this decision, known and not fixed; of
a program it flags, nothing is claimed* (Defaults 1). Filed as
[L-M2.32](findings/wrong-answers/L-M2.32-the-guarantee-of-q-m2-24-has-a-known-wrong-answer-against-it.md), for
what it bears on outside the phase (the register row's readings, Q-M1f.313's *Why*, M1n).

**No test**, as ruled; the decision's *Held by* says *No test: the guarantee is stated, and a run that answers two
orders of one program differently is filed as a wrong answer*, and names the argument (Proposition 2) and the page.
§ 1.2's row says *stated, and held by no test*. The sweep is not written.

### Defaults taken

1. **The guarantee is stated for every program, as ruled, and the boundary's order is said as a known wrong answer**,
   rather than the guarantee scoped to programs order-free at the boundary (what Proposition 2 proves). The ruling
   says *different behaviour … would be considered a bug*, and the register already holds the case at root as a wrong
   answer; scoping would make the decision true of today's binary with no wrong answer, and is the user's to choose
   instead (L-M2.32). Of a flagged program nothing is claimed, by Q-M1f.564.
2. **The decision's subject is `language`**, not `answer` (where Q-M1e2.30's levers are) nor `engine` (determinism).
3. **What may move is listed whole**, the three things this stage saw move that § 1.3 did not name (a listing in the
   written order, `-n N`'s first member, a config written twice) and Q-M1f.139's `:source`, so that none of them is
   later filed as a breach of the guarantee.
4. **The two rows are last, under a heading of their own**, rather than among the names' rows, so that the rows after
   that place are not renumbered in the golden; no page or test cites a row's number.
5. **The register is not edited**: its row `W8/fifo-gap-root` and its owner are M1n's; what Q-M2.24 changes of its
   readings is L-M2.32's to carry to them.
6. **The probes are kept** under [`probes/order-of-forms/`](probes/order-of-forms/): the register's witness and twin
