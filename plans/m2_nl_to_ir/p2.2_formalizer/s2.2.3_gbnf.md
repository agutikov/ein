## Context

**The grammar is the filter's mechanism.** In the arms B2 to B5 the model's thinking is off and its whole output
is held to Ein: by the harness, which accepts nothing else, and by a grammar over the tokens
([the frame, the arms](../README.md#the-arms)). The tool, B6, writes Ein with none, and the filter with its
grammar off is an ablation ([P2.6](../p2.6_ablations/README.md)). So this stage builds a mechanism one
arrangement uses, and [S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)'s probe has already said how the model writes Ein
without it.

**The source is the two grammar files, not the page.** The plan of August generated the grammar from the EBNF
blocks of the grammar page. The page holds no grammar text: its regions are railroad diagrams and one table
([the audit, A.2 rows 16 to 18](../analysis/audit_p2.2.md#a2-the-grammar-page-the-corpus-the-tests)). The parser
is generated by grmtools from `ein.l` and `ein.y`, an LR(1) parser over the tokens of a separate lexer, and
[`ein-ir/tests/grammar_pages.rs`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-ir/tests/grammar_pages.rs) reads both through
grmtools' public readers (`lrlex`'s `LRNonStreamingLexerDef`, `cfgrammar`'s `YaccGrammar`) to write the page's
diagram sources, twenty files of ISO 14977 EBNF beside
[the grammar page](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/01-grammar.md), test-held to the two files
([Q-M1f.378](../../../docs/decisions/docs/Q-M1f.378-the-grammar-page-is-the-two-grammar-files-as-ebnf.md)).

**What is mechanical and what is not.** The phrase grammar translates rule by rule. The lexical grammar does
not, because GBNF is one grammar over characters and Ein's lexer is a pass of its own that takes the longest
match, and the earlier rule on a tie ([`01-grammar.md` § 1](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/01-grammar.md#1-lexical-grammar)).
What is written by hand, and listed where a reader can check it:

- how a reserved word stays out of a name: `and`, `or`, `neq`, `rule`, `query`, `solve`, `config`, `trace`,
  `macro` and `import` are never a name, and `nota` and `neq-1` are names, being longer (`ein.l`'s head);
- how a position word is a name in one place and not in another: `ein.y` has a rule of names for each place
  (`Name`, `HeadName`, `DeclName`, `FactHead`, `ValueHead`, `OrdinaryHead` among them);
- how a token ends at a separator, which the lexer gets from its catch-all tokens and its `AFTER_WORD` start
  condition;
- the tokens no production uses (`_`, `?_`, a keyword the grammar does not know, the engine's writeback names),
  which the grammar leaves out;
- whitespace and comments between tokens.

**What a grammar buys, stated once.** A parseable program, and nothing the loader refuses
([`07-diagnostics.md` § 2](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/07-diagnostics.md#2-errors-and-exit-codes)), and
none of the faults that load in silence: a fact of an undeclared relation, a form at another count, an activator
under a name the file does not list ([the audit, A.1 row 10](../analysis/audit_p2.2.md#a1-the-language)). The one
place a grammar does more is a grammar **restricted to a program's own vocabulary**: with the declared relations
and the names substituted in, an undeclared one cannot be emitted. That closure is EinAf's. Nothing in the kernel
holds a fact to a declaration ([Q-M1f.447](../../../docs/decisions/language/Q-M1f.447-a-relation-is-overloaded-by-its-count-of.md)).

**A grammar a step.** The formalization is made in three steps
([the user's notes on the whole phase](README.md#formalization-in-steps-the-users-notes-on-the-whole-phase)), and
each writes a part of its own: step 1 the facts with their `:source`, the answer query, the declarations and the
memberships, and no import, rule or `(solve …)`; step 2 the complete ontology and the selection; step 3 the rules
still missing and the hypothesis mechanism. So the natural restriction is a grammar a step, which is how this stage
takes [Q-M2.6](../open_questions/formalizer/Q-M2.6-one-gbnf-or-many.md)'s working answer, *many*. **The closure
moves with the steps.** Step 1 makes the vocabulary, and writes its facts before their declarations, as the
synthesis order has it: no grammar holds a step-1 fact to a declaration, and the contract's check does
([S2.2.4](s2.2.4_passes.md), T2.2.4.6). Steps 2 and 3 are rendered with step 1's relations and names, and are
closed over them.

**A grammar and a thinking model do not compose as they stand.** In the host probe a grammar over the whole
output took the thinking channel too: an empty answer and thirteen characters of reasoning
([`host.md`](../analysis/host.md#what-the-model-does-through-llama-server-a-probe)). Where a grammar begins is
part of this stage's design.

## Acceptance

- The whole grammar is written by one command of `einaf` from `ein.l` and `ein.y`, and a check in EinAf's gate
  step fails when the file under `einaf/grammars/` is not what the command writes.
- **The grammar is held to the parser in both directions, offline.** (a) For every `.ein` file under the corpus
  roots (`examples/`, `stdlib/`, `tests/`), the grammar accepts the file exactly when `ein_ir::parse` does, the
  refusals no context-free grammar can state aside, each named. (b) Every text sampled from the grammar parses.
- **llama.cpp reads the grammar as EinAf does**: the files of (a) through llama.cpp's own grammar sampler, with
  the model's vocabulary, agree with EinAf's acceptor. By hand, with a record.
- The step grammars exist, one a step, with their inventory, and (b) holds of each: every sampled text parses, and
  is of the forms its grammar is for. Each accepts its step's programs written by hand
  ([S2.2.1](s2.2.1_contract.md), T2.2.1.8), rendered on their vocabulary.
- On the model: a grammar from the first token with thinking off, and a grammar that begins after the thinking,
  each shown on a recorded generation; an output ends.
- Generation speed with no grammar, under the whole grammar and under each restricted one, measured and recorded;
  [Q-M2.6](../open_questions/formalizer/Q-M2.6-one-gbnf-or-many.md) decided in the ledger on that and on the probe's errors.

## Tasks

### Task T2.2.3.1 — The generator

A command of `einaf` that reads `ein.l` and `ein.y` through grmtools' readers, as `grammar_pages.rs` does, and
writes the whole grammar as a file under `einaf/grammars/`.

- **The phrase part** is `ein.y`'s rules, each production an alternative; a list, which `ein.y` writes as a rule
  that names itself first, is the repetition it derives.
- **The lexical part** is `ein.l`'s rules, each regular expression translated node for node, then the hand work
  of § Context, each piece a named rule of the generator and a row of the inventory.
- **The root is narrower than `Program`.** What the model may write is what the contract admits
  ([S2.2.1](s2.2.1_contract.md)): `relation`, `rule`, `import`, `solve`, `query`, a fact, a stored negation, a
  written `(open F)`. Not `config`, `trace`, or the three wrappers of a saved file (`Saved`, in `ein.y`). That
  root is the whole program in one call ([S2.2.4](s2.2.4_passes.md)'s T2.2.4.9), and each step's grammar narrows
  it again (T2.2.3.3). The full `Program` is generated too, as what the acceptance test (a) runs.
- **A string** is the lexer's `TEXT`: any Unicode, with the escapes `\n \t \r \" \\ \u{…}`
  ([natural language](../../../docs/ein/user/03-syntax-and-semantics/14-natural-language.md#what-a-program-writes)).
  It is written only after `:why`, `:source` and `:goal-text`, which the phrase grammar already says.
- The generated file says that it is generated. Its regeneration is one command, and the command's `--check` is
  what the gate step runs.

The alternative source, the twenty `.puml` files, is the same content as text. The default is the two files,
since the readers give the regular expressions as trees and the diagram sources write a character class in
words.

### Task T2.2.3.2 — The grammar held to the parser

Three tests. The first two need no model and are in EinAf's gate step.

1. **The corpus through an acceptor.** EinAf's own acceptor of the GBNF it generates, over every `.ein` file of
   the three corpus roots: accepted exactly when `ein_ir::parse`, reached through `einaf::kernel`, returns no
   error. The corpus holds files that
   are meant not to parse (the `parse-negative` group of
   [`corpus/corpus.toml`](../../../corpus/README.md)), and the test also reads the shapes the grammar refuses,
   one row a shape, from the kernel's own banked table,
   `ein-ir/tests/golden/grammar_table.txt`. Two refusals are not a grammar's to state and are listed by name: a
   text nested past 1 000 parentheses
   ([§ 1.7](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/07-diagnostics.md#17-a-text-nested-past-1-000-parentheses-is-refused-before-it-is-parsed))
   and a byte that is not UTF-8.
2. **The grammar's own texts through the parser.** A sampler walks the grammar's rules and writes texts, a
   stated number from a stated seed, weighted toward the hand-written parts: a name beside a reserved word, a
   position word in each place, the end of a token before a paren and a comment. Every text parses. **This is
   the direction the filter needs**: an output the grammar allowed and the parser refuses is an *invalid*
   outcome the model could not have avoided.
3. **llama.cpp's reading**, by hand, with a record. The files of test 1 are tokenised with the model's
   vocabulary and fed token by token to llama.cpp's grammar sampler built from the same text; it accepts and
   rejects as EinAf's acceptor does. What constrains a generation is llama.cpp's reading of the text, so a
   difference here is a bug of the generator, whatever test 1 says.

### Task T2.2.3.3 — The restricted grammars: one a step

One grammar a step of [S2.2.4](s2.2.4_passes.md), and one for step 2's second call, each the whole grammar
restricted to the forms its step writes, in the order the step writes them, so that a step cannot write a form
that is not its own. A grammar that is parameterised is written at request time by a function of `einaf`, and its
text's hash is the envelope's.

| grammar | what it allows, in order | parameterised on |
|---|---|---|
| step 1: the facts and a partial ontology | facts and stored negations, `:source` required, their arguments names, numerals and facts; among them, where the text asks, the answer query, one `query` with `:goal` and `:goal-text`, its `:goal-text` references and separators only, and `query` forms with `:goal` alone; then `relation` forms, the `:why` required, and membership facts. No `import`, `rule` or `(solve …)` | nothing, or the input's sentences, so that a `:source` is one of them verbatim |
| step 2: the complete ontology and the selection | `relation` forms, the `:why` required, and membership facts, the ontology whole; `import` forms of the modules the catalogue lists, with the `:symbols` it lists; the facts that activate them, their arguments drawn from the program's names; the marker of a missing property. No `rule` | the catalogue; step 1's relations and names |
| step 2's second call, and step 3: the rules | in step 2's second call, which runs only on a marker, `rule` forms for the property it names; in step 3, a marker before each `rule` form, and the hypothesis mechanism: a `(solve …)` form, a rule that concludes `(open F)`, a written `(open F)` | the catalogue; the relations and names of the steps before |

- A `relation` form takes `:why` or the load refuses it, so the grammars of steps 1 and 2 require the keyword; a
  synthesised rule's `:why` likewise.
- A hypothesis rule is a `rule` whose `:assert` holds `(open F)`: the whole grammar already admits it, and there
  is no other form for it
  ([Q-M1f.567](../../../docs/decisions/hypotheses/Q-M1f.567-a-hypothesis-rule-is-a-rule-that-concludes-open.md)).
  An alternative reading of a sentence is `(open F)` too
  ([Q8](../open_questions/formalizer/Q8-ambiguous-parses.md)), so no grammar of alternatives is written.
- Step 1's grammar cannot hold a fact to a declaration: the facts come first, and their declarations after them
  (§ Context). Steps 2 and 3 can hold an activating fact and a goal's pattern to a declared count, since the
  declared relations are finitely many by then. None can hold an argument to a label: a label is no type, and
  reading one as a type would be EinAf's invention.
- Whether `:source` is restricted to the input's sentences is decided by its cost: the grammar grows with the
  text, and T2.2.3.5 measures it.

### Task T2.2.3.4 — Where a grammar begins, and how an output ends

- **Thinking off: from the first token.** This is the setting of the arms B2 to B5.
- **Thinking on: after the thinking.** The grammar starts at the model's end-of-thinking marker, by llama.cpp's
  lazy grammar (the host's header has it, [S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)) or, where the binding does not give
  it, by EinAf adding the grammar to the chain at the marker, which it can do because it owns the loop over
  tokens. Built here for the ablation of [P2.6](../p2.6_ablations/README.md), *the filter after a chain of
  thought*, and shown on the host probe's question.
- **The end.** A root that is a sequence of forms lets the model stop after any form, or never. The stage
  decides what the root requires before the end of the turn (by default a step's part in synthesis order, so that
  step 1 ends after its ontology with its one answer query written, and a whole program in the one-call mode after
  its hypothesis mechanism), bounds the whitespace between tokens,
  and decides whether a comment is
  allowed at all (by default only the marker of T2.2.3.3). Reaching the token limit is a `Stop` of the runtime,
  recorded as a truncated output and counted as its own kind, never as a refusal of the grammar. How often a
  constrained generation runs into the limit by repeating a form is measured.

### Task T2.2.3.5 — Speed, and Q-M2.6

Tokens a second for a generation of a stated length on the pinned model: with no grammar, under the whole
grammar, under each step's grammar rendered for the Zebra puzzle (steps 2 and 3 on the relations and names of its
hand-written step 1, [S2.2.1](s2.2.1_contract.md)'s T2.2.1.8) and for a word problem; and the time to build each
sampler. Through
[`utils/bench_env.sh`](../../../utils/bench_env.sh), on AC power. The reading is written before the run; no
target is inherited from the plan of August.

**Q-M2.6**, one grammar or many, is decided on three things, and the verdict goes to the ledger with them: the
probe's errors by what would have prevented each ([S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)); these speeds; and what the
vocabulary closure is worth, which only a restricted grammar has, and under the steps only steps 2 and 3. The
working answer is *many*, one a step. What would reverse it: the probe finding no error a step's grammar removes
and the whole one does not, or a step's grammar costing more to build a call than it saves. Step 1 with the model
([S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md), T2.2.2.8) says how often a step 1 writes a fact its own declarations do not
match, which is what no grammar of step 1 can close.

## Risks

- **Longest match is not context-free where it matters least and bites most.** A grammar that lets a name be
  followed by a name character reads `nota` two ways; the lexer reads it one. Test 2 finds a divergence only
  where its sampler goes, which is why the sampler is weighted toward the hand-written rules.
- **A grammar too tight forces nonsense.** A model held to a form it has nothing to say in still has to emit
  tokens: it repeats a fact or fills a `:why` with noise. The stop reasons and the repeats of T2.2.3.4 are how
  this is seen, and a low answer rate under a grammar is read beside the probe's rate without one.
- **A parameterised grammar grows with the problem**: with step 1's names, and with the input's sentences
  if `:source` is restricted. T2.2.3.5 measures the build time and the speed at Zebra's size.
- **Step 1 is the least held step and the one that matters most**: its facts are what the later steps and the
  loop change least, and no grammar holds them to their declarations. What that costs is the contract's check's
  count, by step.
- **llama.cpp's GBNF has its own limits** on a grammar's size and on a repetition's bounds. Test 3 is where they
  show.

### The generator and its hand-written rules (T2.2.3.1)

`ein.l` and `ein.y` are read through `lrlex` (each rule's regular expression by `regex-syntax`) and `cfgrammar`, at
`ein-ir`'s versions, the build embedding the two files. **Translated**: every lexer rule a production uses, node for
node (the 25 words and 20 keywords their text, `TEXT` and its six `\u{…}` ranges, the skip rule's three pieces); every
rule of `ein.y`, each production an alternative, a list its repetition, compiled into a rule a state of its Glushkov
automaton so that each token's separator is chosen by the kinds of the two tokens it stands between (101 rules into
337; a list and a rule of more than one start or end kind written into its users; `Template` written once a kind).
**Hand-written**, a row each of the inventory, with the rule of `ein.l` or `ein.y` it stands for:

| rule | stands for | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `tok-name` (175 rules) | `ein.l`'s `NAME`, and the 25 words, `UNDERSCORE` and `WRITEBACK` before it | a name is the run of its characters less the 29 words an earlier rule takes whole |
| `tok-var` (3) | `VAR`, and `VARUNDERSCORE` before it | a variable is `?` and the run, less `?_` |
| `gap`, `gap-word`, `gap-text`, `gap-after-text` | the skip rule, `AFTER_WORD`, `BAD` and `BADTEXT` | a token that is no paren ends at a separator or a paren; after a string a separator does not begin with `#` |
| `comment-line-ended`, `gap-end` | the skip rule's `;[^\n]*` | a line comment runs to its line's end, or ends the text |
| `program`, `root` | `Program`, `TopForm`, `Form` | a text is a separator, forms, a separator; `root` the contract's forms |

### Defaults taken

1. **The generator reads `ein.l` and `ein.y` through grmtools' readers**, `lrlex` (with `regex-syntax` for each
   rule's regular expression), `cfgrammar`, at the versions `ein-ir` builds against, already in EinAf's lock file, and
   the build embeds the two files (`include_str!`), so that `einaf grammar write` needs no checkout path and a change to
   either rebuilds it. The twenty `.puml` diagram sources are not read.
2. **The check that the file is what the command writes is a test of EinAf's**
   (`the_grammar_file_is_what_einaf_grammar_write_writes`, with the manifest), in EinAf's gate step, and
   `einaf grammar write --check` for a reader; not a step of `utils/check_generated.py`. The generator is a function of
   EinAf's library, not a script of the process, and the test reads the two files the build embeds, as Ein's
   `grammar_pages.rs` holds the grammar page's regions to them; so no `utils/` file, no step of the gate and no list of
   `check_gate_steps.py` changed.
3. **The whole grammar's two roots**: `program`, every form `Program` takes, for the acceptance test; `root`, the
   forms the contract admits, for a program in one call. A macro is not among `root`'s: the stage's list leaves it out.
4. **The whole grammar is exact**, a separator chosen by the kinds of the two tokens it stands between, so that the
   acceptance test can be *exactly*; the price is `ein.y`'s 101 rules compiled into 337 (each rule's Glushkov automaton,
   states that behave alike one rule) and a tree of 175 rules for a name.
5. **EinAf's acceptor follows llama.cpp's parser and sampler**, a set of stacks, a group and a repetition expanded
   into rules, left recursion refused, rather than a parser of its own kind, so that what it accepts is what llama.cpp
   reads the same text as; a stack is a node of one tree, two equal stacks one.
6. **Test 2 samples 2 000 texts from the seed 20 261 126**, half from each root, a rule named last in an alternative no
   deeper than the rule that names it, 24 rules deep and 1 200 characters at most before every rule takes its least
   alternative, an alternative that begins with `tok-name`, `tok-var`, `head-name` or `space` weighing three times the
   rest; and it reports what the texts hold of the hand-written rules, and fails where they hold too little.
7. **A step's grammar is written in the model's layout**: a paren hugs what it holds, a separator a run of up to 24
   spaces or a line break and an indentation as long, forms one or two line breaks apart, a comment a line of its own
   between forms, a name of letters, digits, `_` and `-` less Ein's words, a string with no `"`, `\` or line break.
8. **Step 1's `:source` is one of the input's sentences** where the input is given: 17 sentences make the Zebra
   puzzle's step-1 grammar 15 kB, its sampler made in 0.20 ms and the grammar written in 36 ms: the restriction
   costs nothing a call shows.
9. **Step 2's ontology is open**, its imports and activating facts the catalogue's, and it is rendered on step 1's
   program so that an import lists no name step 1 declared ([Q-M2.29](open_questions/formalizer/Q-M2.29-are-step-2-s-facts-held-to-the-relations-step-2-declares.md)).
10. **Step 3's grammar also allows activating facts** of the modules the program imports, over its names: the steps by
    hand write the read-back there (the Zebra puzzle's eighteen, the seating's two), which T2.2.3.3's table does not
    list.
11. **The marker is the comment line `;; missing: …`**, which steps 2b and 3 require directly above each rule and step
    2 writes as any comment; the steps by hand carry none, and the tests write one above each rule
    ([L-M2.13](findings/einaf/L-M2.13-the-steps-by-hand-carry-no-marker-of-the-grammars-shape.md)).
12. **The catalogue the grammars are rendered on is a stand-in read off the library**: every module of `std` but
    `std.core`, its declared names, its rules of a parameter or more as activators, the relations its rules read that
    no module declares, Ein's words aside ([L-M2.12](findings/einaf/L-M2.12-the-grammars-catalogue-is-every-module-and-every-rule.md)).
13. **A step's hand-written part** is the forms of its `stepN.ein` whose canonical text step N−1's program does not

14. **A grammar begins where the program begins**: with thinking off at the first token, inside the fence the prompt

25. **The steps keep their name class**, letters, digits, `_` and `-`, which is inside the root's; the root's `.`,
