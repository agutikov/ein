## Context

**The grammar is the filter's mechanism.** In the arms B2 to B5 the model's thinking is off and its whole output
is held to Ein: by the harness, which accepts nothing else, and by a grammar over the tokens
([the frame, the arms](../README.md#the-arms)). The tool, B6, writes Ein with none, and the filter with its
grammar off is an ablation ([P2.6](../p2.6_ablations/README.md)). So this stage builds a mechanism one
arrangement uses, and [S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)'s probe has already said how the model writes Ein
without it.

**The source is the two grammar files, not the page.** The plan of August generated the grammar from the EBNF
blocks of the grammar page. The page holds no grammar text: its regions are railroad diagrams and one table
([the audit, A.2 rows 16 to 18](../analysis/audit_p2.2.md#a2-the-grammar-page-the-corpus-the-tests)). The parser
is generated by grmtools from `ein.l` and `ein.y`, an LR(1) parser over the tokens of a separate lexer, and
[`ein-ir/tests/grammar_pages.rs`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-ir/tests/grammar_pages.rs) reads both through
grmtools' public readers (`lrlex`'s `LRNonStreamingLexerDef`, `cfgrammar`'s `YaccGrammar`) to write the page's
diagram sources, twenty files of ISO 14977 EBNF beside
[the grammar page](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/01-grammar.md), test-held to the two files
([Q-M1f.378](../../../docs/decisions/docs/Q-M1f.378-the-grammar-page-is-the-two-grammar-files-as-ebnf.md)).

**What is mechanical and what is not.** The phrase grammar translates rule by rule. The lexical grammar does
not, because GBNF is one grammar over characters and Ein's lexer is a pass of its own that takes the longest
match, and the earlier rule on a tie ([`01-grammar.md` § 1](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/01-grammar.md#1-lexical-grammar)).
What is written by hand, and listed where a reader can check it:

- how a reserved word stays out of a name: `and`, `or`, `neq`, `rule`, `query`, `solve`, `config`, `trace`,
  `macro` and `import` are never a name, and `nota` and `neq-1` are names, being longer (`ein.l`'s head);
- how a position word is a name in one place and not in another: `ein.y` has a rule of names for each place
  (`Name`, `HeadName`, `DeclName`, `FactHead`, `ValueHead`, `OrdinaryHead` among them);
- how a token ends at a separator, which the lexer gets from its catch-all tokens and its `AFTER_WORD` start
  condition;
- the tokens no production uses (`_`, `?_`, a keyword the grammar does not know, the engine's writeback names),
  which the grammar leaves out;
- whitespace and comments between tokens.

**What a grammar buys, stated once.** A parseable program, and nothing the loader refuses
([`07-diagnostics.md` § 2](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/07-diagnostics.md#2-errors-and-exit-codes)), and
none of the faults that load in silence: a fact of an undeclared relation, a form at another count, an activator
under a name the file does not list ([the audit, A.1 row 10](../analysis/audit_p2.2.md#a1-the-language)). The one
place a grammar does more is a grammar **restricted to a program's own vocabulary**: with the declared relations
and the names substituted in, an undeclared one cannot be emitted. That closure is EinAf's. Nothing in the kernel
holds a fact to a declaration ([Q-M1f.447](../../../docs/decisions/language/Q-M1f.447-a-relation-is-overloaded-by-its-count-of.md)).

**A grammar and a thinking model do not compose as they stand.** In the host probe a grammar over the whole
output took the thinking channel too: an empty answer and thirteen characters of reasoning
([`host.md`](../analysis/host.md#what-the-model-does-through-llama-server-a-probe)). Where a grammar begins is
part of this stage's design.

## Acceptance

- The whole grammar is written by one command of `einaf` from `ein.l` and `ein.y`, and a check in EinAf's gate
  step fails when the file under `einaf/grammars/` is not what the command writes.
- **The grammar is held to the parser in both directions, offline.** (a) For every `.ein` file under the corpus
  roots (`examples/`, `stdlib/`, `tests/`), the grammar accepts the file exactly when `ein_ir::parse` does, the
  refusals no context-free grammar can state aside, each named. (b) Every text sampled from the grammar parses.
- **llama.cpp reads the grammar as EinAf does**: the files of (a) through llama.cpp's own grammar sampler, with
  the model's vocabulary, agree with EinAf's acceptor. By hand, with a record.
- The restricted grammars exist with their inventory, and (b) holds of each: every sampled text parses, and is of
  the forms its grammar is for.
- On the model: a grammar from the first token with thinking off, and a grammar that begins after the thinking,
  each shown on a recorded generation; an output ends.
- Generation speed with no grammar, under the whole grammar and under each restricted one, measured and recorded;
  [Q-M2.6](../open_questions/formalizer/Q-M2.6-one-gbnf-or-many.md) decided in the ledger on that and on the probe's errors.

## Tasks

### Task T2.2.3.1 — The generator

A command of `einaf` that reads `ein.l` and `ein.y` through grmtools' readers, as `grammar_pages.rs` does, and
writes the whole grammar as a file under `einaf/grammars/`.

- **The phrase part** is `ein.y`'s rules, each production an alternative; a list, which `ein.y` writes as a rule
  that names itself first, is the repetition it derives.
- **The lexical part** is `ein.l`'s rules, each regular expression translated node for node, then the hand work
  of § Context, each piece a named rule of the generator and a row of the inventory.
- **The root is narrower than `Program`.** What the model may write is what the contract admits
  ([S2.2.1](s2.2.1_contract.md)): `relation`, `rule`, `import`, `solve`, `query`, a fact, a stored negation, a
  written `(open F)`. Not `config`, `trace`, or the three wrappers of a saved file (`Saved`, in `ein.y`). The
  full `Program` is generated too, as what the acceptance test (a) runs.
- **A string** is the lexer's `TEXT`: any Unicode, with the escapes `\n \t \r \" \\ \u{…}`
  ([natural language](../../../docs/ein/user/03-syntax-and-semantics/14-natural-language.md#what-a-program-writes)).
  It is written only after `:why`, `:source` and `:goal-text`, which the phrase grammar already says.
- The generated file says that it is generated. Its regeneration is one command, and the command's `--check` is
  what the gate step runs.

The alternative source, the twenty `.puml` files, is the same content as text. The default is the two files,
since the readers give the regular expressions as trees and the diagram sources write a character class in
words.

### Task T2.2.3.2 — The grammar held to the parser

Three tests. The first two need no model and are in EinAf's gate step.

1. **The corpus through an acceptor.** EinAf's own acceptor of the GBNF it generates, over every `.ein` file of
   the three corpus roots: accepted exactly when `ein_ir::parse`, reached through `einaf::kernel`, returns no
   error. The corpus holds files that
   are meant not to parse (the `parse-negative` group of
   [`corpus/corpus.toml`](../../../corpus/README.md)), and the test also reads the shapes the grammar refuses,
   one row a shape, from the kernel's own banked table,
   `ein-ir/tests/golden/grammar_table.txt`. Two refusals are not a grammar's to state and are listed by name: a
   text nested past 1 000 parentheses
   ([§ 1.7](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/07-diagnostics.md#17-a-text-nested-past-1-000-parentheses-is-refused-before-it-is-parsed))
   and a byte that is not UTF-8.
2. **The grammar's own texts through the parser.** A sampler walks the grammar's rules and writes texts, a
   stated number from a stated seed, weighted toward the hand-written parts: a name beside a reserved word, a
   position word in each place, the end of a token before a paren and a comment. Every text parses. **This is
   the direction the filter needs**: an output the grammar allowed and the parser refuses is an *invalid*
   outcome the model could not have avoided.
3. **llama.cpp's reading**, by hand, with a record. The files of test 1 are tokenised with the model's
   vocabulary and fed token by token to llama.cpp's grammar sampler built from the same text; it accepts and
   rejects as EinAf's acceptor does. What constrains a generation is llama.cpp's reading of the text, so a
   difference here is a bug of the generator, whatever test 1 says.

### Task T2.2.3.3 — The restricted grammars

One grammar a pass of [S2.2.4](s2.2.4_passes.md), each the whole grammar restricted to the forms its pass is
responsible for, so that a pass cannot write a form that is not its own. A restricted grammar that is
parameterised is written at request time by a function of `einaf`, and its text's hash is the envelope's.

| grammar | what it allows | parameterised on |
|---|---|---|
| ontology | `relation` forms, the `:why` required; membership facts | nothing, or the input's sentences |
| theory | `import` forms of the modules the catalogue lists, with the `:symbols` it lists; the facts that activate them, their arguments drawn from the ontology's names; a `(solve …)` form; the marker of a missing property; and, only when the theory pass has stated one, `rule` forms | the catalogue; the ontology's relations and names |
| instance | facts and stored negations of a declared relation, at a count it is declared at, their arguments the ontology's names, numerals, and facts of declared relations; `:source` required | the ontology; optionally the input's sentences, so that a `:source` is one of them verbatim |
| query | one `query` with `:goal` and `:goal-text`, the answer query, its `:goal-text` references and separators only; before it, `query` forms with `:goal` alone; the goal's relations declared ones | the ontology's relations; the goal's own variables, where the grammar can carry them |

- A `relation` form takes `:why` or the load refuses it, so the ontology grammar requires the keyword; a
  synthesised rule's `:why` likewise.
- A hypothesis rule is a `rule` whose `:assert` holds `(open F)`: the whole grammar already admits it, and there
  is no other form for it
  ([Q-M1f.567](../../../docs/decisions/hypotheses/Q-M1f.567-a-hypothesis-rule-is-a-rule-that-concludes-open.md)).
  An alternative reading of a sentence is `(open F)` too
  ([Q8](../open_questions/formalizer/Q8-ambiguous-parses.md)), so no grammar of alternatives is written.
- The instance grammar can hold a fact to a declared count, since the declared relations are finitely many. It
  cannot hold an argument to a label: a label is no type, and reading one as a type would be EinAf's invention.
- Whether `:source` is restricted to the input's sentences is decided by its cost: the grammar grows with the
  text, and T2.2.3.5 measures it.

### Task T2.2.3.4 — Where a grammar begins, and how an output ends

- **Thinking off: from the first token.** This is the setting of the arms B2 to B5.
- **Thinking on: after the thinking.** The grammar starts at the model's end-of-thinking marker, by llama.cpp's
  lazy grammar (the host's header has it, [S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)) or, where the binding does not give
  it, by EinAf adding the grammar to the chain at the marker, which it can do because it owns the loop over
  tokens. Built here for the ablation of [P2.6](../p2.6_ablations/README.md), *the filter after a chain of
  thought*, and shown on the host probe's question.
- **The end.** A root that is a sequence of forms lets the model stop after any form, or never. The stage
  decides what the root requires before the end of the turn (by default the passes' order, so that a whole
  program ends at its answer query, the one query with a `:goal-text`), bounds the whitespace between tokens,
  and decides whether a comment is
  allowed at all (by default only the marker of T2.2.3.3). Reaching the token limit is a `Stop` of the runtime,
  recorded as a truncated output and counted as its own kind, never as a refusal of the grammar. How often a
  constrained generation runs into the limit by repeating a form is measured.

### Task T2.2.3.5 — Speed, and Q-M2.6

Tokens a second for a generation of a stated length on the pinned model: with no grammar, under the whole
grammar, under each restricted grammar rendered for the Zebra ontology (its relations and its thirty names) and
for a word problem; and the time to build each sampler. Through
[`utils/bench_env.sh`](../../../utils/bench_env.sh), on AC power. The reading is written before the run; no
target is inherited from the plan of August.

**Q-M2.6**, one grammar or many, is decided on three things, and the verdict goes to the ledger with them: the
probe's errors by what would have prevented each ([S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)); these speeds; and what the
vocabulary closure is worth, which only a restricted grammar has. The working answer is *many*. What would
reverse it: the probe finding no error a restricted grammar removes and the whole one does not, or a restricted
grammar costing more to build a call than it saves.

### Task T2.2.3.6 — The inventory

`einaf/grammars/README.md`: one table, the grammars, what each allows, which pass uses it, what it is
parameterised on, and every hand-written rule of T2.2.3.1 with the rule of `ein.l` or `ein.y` it stands for. A
manifest of the generated files' digests beside it, as `stdlib/MANIFEST.sha256` is for the library: a record
names a grammar by digest, never by path.

### Task T2.2.3.7 — What the stage found of Ein

The stage reads the kernel's grammar more closely than anything outside the kernel has. What it finds goes to
[the evaluation of Ein](../evaluation/README.md): what of the lexer could not be said in a context-free grammar and
what that cost; any corpus file on which the acceptor and the parser part ways for a reason that is the
grammar files'; whether the banked table of refused shapes was enough to test a second reader of the language.

### Dropped as moot

- **Generating from the page's EBNF blocks, and `00_ebnf.md`**: the page holds pictures (the audit, A.2 rows 16
  to 18).
- **The old hand-written skeleton with `string` and `number` rules**: Ein has no number token
  ([Q-M1e2.24](../../../docs/decisions/language/Q-M1e2.24-ein-has-no-numbers-and-no-ranges.md)).
- **The echo round-trip through the model**: T2.2.3.2's three tests hold the grammar to the parser exactly, and
  need no generation.
- **`hrule` in the theory grammar and `ambiguity.gbnf`**: there is no `hrule`, and an alternative has no form of
  its own ([Q8](../open_questions/formalizer/Q8-ambiguous-parses.md)).
- **A target of tokens a second**, and the note on a hosted arm: the first was the old plan's guess, the second
  is not in the plan ([Q-M2.2](../open_questions/model/Q-M2.2-must-the-model-be-local.md)).

## Risks

- **Longest match is not context-free where it matters least and bites most.** A grammar that lets a name be
  followed by a name character reads `nota` two ways; the lexer reads it one. Test 2 finds a divergence only
  where its sampler goes, which is why the sampler is weighted toward the hand-written rules.
- **A grammar too tight forces nonsense.** A model held to a form it has nothing to say in still has to emit
  tokens: it repeats a fact or fills a `:why` with noise. The stop reasons and the repeats of T2.2.3.4 are how
  this is seen, and a low answer rate under a grammar is read beside the probe's rate without one.
- **A parameterised grammar grows with the problem**: with the ontology's names, and with the input's sentences
  if `:source` is restricted. T2.2.3.5 measures the build time and the speed at Zebra's size.
- **llama.cpp's GBNF has its own limits** on a grammar's size and on a repetition's bounds. Test 3 is where they
  show.
