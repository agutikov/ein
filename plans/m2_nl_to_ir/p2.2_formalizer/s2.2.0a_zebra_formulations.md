## Context

[`examples/README.md` § Zebra puzzle — two ontologies, one puzzle](../../../examples/README.md#zebra-puzzle--two-ontologies-one-puzzle)
has the two encodings side by side, and both are held cell by cell to the published answer
([Q-M1e.25](../../../docs/decisions/corpus/Q-M1e.25-both-zebra-encodings-are-held-to-the-published.md)):

| | [`zebra.ein`](../../../examples/zebra.ein) | [`zebra2.ein`](../../../examples/zebra2.ein) |
|---|---|---|
| a clue, *The Englishman lives in the red house* | `(co-located Englishman Red)` | `(co-located nation-loc Englishman color-loc Red)` |
| a spatial clue, *The green house is immediately to the right of the ivory house* | `(right-of Green Ivory)` | `(adjacent-via right-of color-loc Ivory color-loc Green)` |
| *Milk is drunk in the middle house* | `(co-located Milk House-3)` | `(drink-loc Milk House-3)` |
| membership | `(instance V T)` and `(type Sub Super)` | `is-a` |
| the theory | `std.slots`, selected by `(slot-partition co-located instance type Attribute House)` and a `(slot-spatial …)` per spatial relation; no rule of its own but `guess` | `std.bijection` per projection, and fourteen rules of the file's own besides `guess` |
| the hypotheses | the rule `guess`, five activators | the rule `guess`, five activators |

The phase's re-plan chose `zebra.ein` as the shape of the formalizer's output, because it has no rule of its own:
the theory is selected, not synthesised ([the README](README.md#the-target-shape-and-the-program-that-is-the-model-of-it)).
The user's reading is the other way: the generic vocabulary *looks* better and works less well than the literal
one, and a third encoding should go further towards the text. What *works* means is the stage's first question,
and it is answered by measurement, not by argument: closeness to the sentences, what the theory costs and who
writes it, what the kernel does and says back, and what happens when a formalizer gets one piece wrong.

## Tasks

### T2.2.0a.1 — The two encodings, side by side, on every axis that bears on a formalizer

Each axis measured on both files, with the command that takes it, into one table:

- **Distance from the text.** For each of the fifteen statements and the two questions of
  [`nl2smt/puzzles/zebra.txt`](../../../nl2smt/puzzles/zebra.txt): the forms that state it; which of the
  sentence's words survive in them (relation, arguments); how many facts a sentence takes; what a reader has to
  know to see the sentence in its fact (that `co-located` means *lives in* here and *is drunk in* there; that
  `House-3` is *the middle house*). And the forms that state no sentence: memberships, kinds, activators, the
  hypothesis rule.
- **What the theory is, and who writes it.** The forms beyond the facts a formalizer has to get right, counted;
  how each property is spelled to be active (`slot-partition`'s five arguments, `bijective` with its hierarchy
  facts); what comes from the library and what is the file's.
- **What the kernel does.** Under `--single`, `--maximal`, a description and `--count`: the verdict, saturations,
  facts, layers (`--json-summary`'s `work`); H's size and writer (`ein show hyps`); the time, through
  `utils/bench_env.sh`.
- **What the kernel says back.** The read-out of each run; how a derived fact reads through its `:why` (*the
  Englishman and Red are in the same house* against *the Englishman lives in House-1*); the answer's sentence;
  `ein render program`, `ein render kb` and `ein render h-space`, each drawn to SVG, side by side.
- **What a formalizer's mistake does**, which is where *works not so good* is most likely to show. On each
  encoding, the same small damages: a clue dropped (`zebra2-minus-15.ein` exists, and its twin is written for
  `zebra.ein`), a clue added that contradicts the others (`examples/ein-bugs/zebra2-bad.ein` exists, and its twin is
  written), two arguments of one clue swapped, one activating fact misspelt or left out, one membership fact
  left out. Per damage: the outcome, the core and the rules that refuted where there is one, what the feedback
  would say to a model, and whether the damage is visible at all.

The record says, axis by axis, where the user's reading holds and where it does not, with the run that shows
it.

### T2.2.0a.2 — `zebra3.ein`: the text's own words

A third encoding whose facts are as close to the sentences as Ein allows, with the user's two examples as the
start: `(same-house Englishman lives-in Red color-of)` and `(drinks Ukrainian Tea)`. The theory carries what the
facts leave out, by the library where the library has it and by rules of the file's own where it does not: **the
facts stay literal and the rules vary**. Each design choice is written down with the alternative and why:

- **one relation per verb** (`drinks`, `owns`, `smokes`, `lives-in`, `is-drunk-in`) **or a few relations that name
  the verb as an argument** (`same-house` with role words, as the user's first example does and as `zebra2`'s
  four-argument `co-located` already half does); what each costs the theory;
- **the passive sentences** (*Coffee is drunk in the green house*, *Kools are smoked in the yellow house*): the
  house named by its colour, as the text names it, and the theory connecting the colour to a house;
- **positions written as the text writes them**: *the first house*, *the middle house*, *immediately to the
  right of*, *next to*, as names and relations the theory reads, not as `House-1` and `House-3`;
- **the descriptions** (*the man who smokes Chesterfields*, *the man with the fox*, *the Old Gold smoker*,
  *the Lucky Strike smoker*): whether a description is an object of its own or a relation's argument;
- **the questions** as one answer query whose `:goal` is written in the facts' own relations (*Who drinks
  water?* as `(drinks ?who Water)`), with the `:goal-text` sentence the phase's acceptance asks for, so that the
  theory has to make the literal question answerable;
- **the hypothesis mechanism**: the library's obligations where the theory selected has them, or a rule that
  concludes `(open F)`.

Drafts that lose against the axes of T2.2.0a.1 are kept as probes, each with the reason it lost. The file that
stays is an example of Ein's: under `examples/`, with its corpus entry and its catalog line, written in Ein's
voice (nothing names a plan, a stage or EinAf), **monotone**, answered `Solution` under every question within the
default budget, and held to the published answer cell by cell as the other two are (the test of
[Q-M1e.25](../../../docs/decisions/corpus/Q-M1e.25-both-zebra-encodings-are-held-to-the-published.md) and its
decision extended to a third encoding, in the same commit). `examples/README.md`'s comparison gains a third
column.

### T2.2.0a.3 — The program in the order it is made

If a formalization is made in steps ([the user's notes](README.md#formalization-in-steps-the-users-notes-on-the-whole-phase)), its text can be the steps'
outputs one after another, so that **the final program reads in the order it was synthesised**: first the facts,
in the text's order, each with its sentence as `:source`, and the questions where the text asks them; then the
ontology, the declarations and the memberships; then the theory, the imports, the facts that select what they
bring, the rules and the hypothesis mechanism. Each of the three encodings is written again in that order, with
nothing else changed, and the stage checks three things.

- **That it loads.** Each form that Ein may need to read before another is tried after it: a fact before its
  relation's declaration; a short name written before the import that lists it in `:symbols`; an activating fact
  before the rule or the import it activates; a macro's call before its definition; a query before what it names; a
  `(solve …)` list before the relations it names. Each one that is refused, or read differently, is listed with the
  kernel's message and the page that says it should be (or the absence of one).
- **That it means the same, held by invariance and not by inspection**: against the program in its conventional
  order, the same R, the same outcome under every question and every reading, the same classes and rows of the
  answer query and the same answer sentence, the same H (`ein show hyps`). Ein's contract says that a monotone
  program's fixpoint, and so R, do not depend on the order its rules fire in
  ([`01-determinism-and-order.md` § 1.1](../../../docs/ein/ein.rs/03-design/01-determinism-and-order.md#11-what-a-runs-output-depends-on)),
  and lists the inputs a run may not depend on, each with the test that holds it (§ 1.2): **the written order of a
  program's forms is not among them**, and no test of Ein writes a program's forms in another order (`agenda.rs`
  holds the agenda against the rules' written order, which is another thing). On three programs this is a check of
  what Ein holds by argument.
- **What does differ, as Ein allows** (§ 1.3, § 4): a core and the rules that refuted it (each encoding's
  contradictory twin, in synthesis order), the trace and the event stream, the order of the read-out's listings,
  the blind enumerator's ties, the `work` counts. Each is measured and said, so that a record can tell which of
  its differences are the order's.

What follows from it:

- If the order changes nothing that § 1.3 does not allow, **whether Ein should hold it over the corpus** (a test
  that writes every corpus program's forms in another order and holds every answer to the original's, beside
  `import_invariance.rs`) is a finding with that proposal, for the user.
- If a form has to come first, the finding says which, what the loader would have to do to read the file whole
  first, and whether that leaves Ein's syntax and semantics as they are
  ([Q-M2.16](../open_questions/kernel/Q-M2.16-what-of-the-kernel-may-change-inside-m2.md)); the stage changes no kernel.
  If an answer moves, it is a bug of Ein's, filed in the evaluation and in
  [the wrong-answer register](../../wrong_answers/README.md).
- **`zebra3.ein` is written in synthesis order** where the check allows it, so that the example reads in the order
  a formalization is made; the programs in both orders are kept as probes beside the analysis.

## Risks

- ***Works better* has more than one meaning.** A literal encoding may be closer to the text and cost the kernel
  more, or explain itself better and need more rules. The stage reports each axis and does not fold them into one
  verdict; which axis weighs most is the user's.
- **A literal fact can need a rule the library does not have.** That moves work from selection to synthesis,
  which [F12](../../followups/f12_rules_and_relations/ideas.md) prices high. The cost is recorded, and the
  question whether the library should grow is the user's, not the stage's.
- **A rule that reads a relation's name as an argument** (`(same-house Englishman lives-in Red color-of)`) may
  need an `absent` or a pattern the static check flags. A flagged `zebra3` is not an answer; the stage looks for a
  monotone shape, and records the flagged drafts as findings.
- **The third encoding moves a decision** ([Q-M1e.25](../../../docs/decisions/corpus/Q-M1e.25-both-zebra-encodings-are-held-to-the-published.md)):
  it is extended in Ein's words, and nothing of Ein names why the third was written.

- **The synthesis order may be one Ein does not read.** If a short name must follow its import, or a call its
  macro, the steps' outputs cannot simply be put one after another, and the formalizer has to reorder what it
  wrote. T2.2.0a.3 says which forms, and whether the loader could read a file whole first without a change to the
  language.
