## Context

The plan of August split extraction into passes because one pass over a whole puzzle was too much load and
because an ontology written first keeps the facts from naming things that do not exist. The first reason holds.
The second is the one the user's steps turn round: **the facts first**, in the text's own words, because they are
the part closest to the text and the part that should change least, and the ontology and the theory are what is
searched for around them
([the user's notes](README.md#formalization-in-steps-the-users-notes-on-the-whole-phase)). What keeps a fact from
naming a thing that does not exist is then the step after it, which completes the ontology, and the contract's
check, which reads every step's program.

```text
text ─► step 1 ─────────────────────► step 2 ──────────────────────────► step 3 ──────────────► assembly ─► the check
        the facts with :source,      the ontology whole; imports and   the rules still        synthesis     on each step's
        the answer query; the        activating facts (selected);      missing, each after    order         program, and
        declarations and the         a marker per missing property,    its marker; the                      the complete one;
        memberships seen (partial)   and its rules on a second call    hypothesis mechanism                 the categories
           │                            │                                 │
           └── judged at root ──────────┴── judged at root ───────────────┴── the complete program, asked
```

Steps 2 and 3 are where the user's three neural actions
([F13](../../followups/f13_puzzles_beyond_zebra/ideas.md)) become three priced moves, and step 1 is the first of
them: **reinterpretation** is a new step 1 or a new ontology in step 2; **theory selection** is a fact from the
catalogue, cheap and checked, the default; **theory synthesis** is a `rule` form, permitted only after the step
has stated which property it needed and did not find, so that every synthesised rule carries the reason it exists.
[F12](../../followups/f12_rules_and_relations/ideas.md)'s *select, do not invent* is held by that order, inside
steps 2 and 3.

What the stage is built on that the plan of August did not have
([the audit, B.0 and B.4](../analysis/audit_p2.2.md#b0-the-four-passes-in-todays-language)):

- **A program with no theory does not sit idle**: the blind enumerator answers it. So step 3 states the
  hypothesis mechanism, the check reads which writer the kernel reported, and a step's program before step 3 is
  judged at root (`--no-search`'s question), never solved.
- **Selecting is spelling.** An activating fact works only under a name the file can write: one it lists in
  `:symbols` from the module that declares it, the full name, or an alias. A relation a module reads and does not
  declare (`bijective`, `slot-partition`, `slot-spatial`) is written as it is
  ([`04-stdlib/` § What to list](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/04-stdlib/README.md#what-to-list)). Several
  selections take more than a relation: `(slot-partition co-located instance type Attribute House)` names the
  membership relations and two kinds.
- **The target exists in more than one formulation.** For an attribute puzzle the first re-plan took `std.slots`,
  whose obligations write H, and [S2.2.0a](s2.2.0a_zebra_formulations.md) measures it against the projections of
  `zebra2.ein` and the text's own words of `zebra3.1.ein` to `zebra3.4.ein`
  ([the phase README](README.md#the-target-shape-and-the-program-that-is-the-model-of-it)). Literal facts need a
  theory that reads them onto what the library's theory reads, which may be rules of the program's own.
- **The steps are Rust and read the kernel through `einaf::kernel`**, the one module that calls the engine's
  crates ([S2.1.3](../../../docs/einaf/README.md#what-it-depends-on)): a reply is parsed by
  `ein_ir::parse`, a form's canonical text is `ein_ir::dump_canonical`'s, and a program is run by the bounded
  kernel call. Where this file names a function of the crates, it names what `einaf::kernel` wraps; this stage
  adds to that module what it does not yet give. No binary is called.

## Acceptance

With the model, one shot, each run a record. On the Zebra text
([`nl2smt/puzzles/zebra.txt`](../../../nl2smt/puzzles/zebra.txt)) and on the sixteen word problems:

- **step 1**: its output parses and holds only its own forms; every statement's `:source` is a sentence of the
  input, and the report says which sentences have no fact; every relation carries a `:why`; one answer query, every
  reference of which the goal binds and which is written in the facts' own relations; and, where
  [S2.2.1](s2.2.1_contract.md)'s T2.2.1.8 has the text by hand, the coreferences and the kinds against it;
- **step 2**: every import and activating fact is an entry of the catalogue; the check finds every selection
  active; there is no `rule` form without the marker of a missing property before it; what it changed of step 1's
  ontology is recorded;
- **step 3**: the writer of H the kernel reports is the one the entry promises or the program states, and is not
  `blind` unless the program says so; there is no `rule` form without its marker;
- **each step's program** is judged at root and its feedback kept; a fact a later step writes for a sentence step
  1 already stated otherwise is reported and counted;
- **the assembled program**, in synthesis order, loads, and its outcome among the seven and the findings of the
  contract's check are reported. The answer is the acceptance of [S2.2.5](s2.2.5_from_text.md) and of
  [the phase](README.md#acceptance), not of this stage.

Without the model, in EinAf's gate step:

- **Every entry of the catalogue is run by the kernel**, and the test fails on an entry whose selection is not
  active or whose stated effect the kernel does not show.
- Recorded generations, one a step, replay through parsing, assembly, the check and the table of categories; the
  hand-written steps of S2.2.1's T2.2.1.8 replay the same way, as one generation a step.
- [Q9](../open_questions/formalizer/Q9-ontology-provenance.md) is decided in the ledger.

## Tasks

### Task T2.2.4.1 — The prompts

`einaf/prompts/`, one template a step, one for step 2's second call, and one for the whole program (T2.2.4.9).
Each cites the section of [the contract](s2.2.1_contract.md) it implements, its step's among them, and holds only
what B3 lists: the excerpt for its step, the catalogue (steps 2 and 3 only), the examples for its step, the text,
and the programs of the steps before it. An example of a step is a text with the program each step wrote for it,
cut to the steps up to this one, so that the model sees what it is handed and what it hands on. A template is
rendered by filling named slots; the template's hash and the rendered prompt's are the record's.

**The examples are never the item's own, and each follows the contract.** For the Zebra run no example quotes
a Zebra clue: the examples of an attribute puzzle are a smaller puzzle over the same theory, written by the stage
with its text in three steps, kept under `einaf/prompts/` and answered by the kernel in a test. For a word problem
the examples are word problems written over the library alone, the calculator's requests and rules of the
program's own, since the corpus's sixteen import their family's modules, which the main arms do not show
(T2.2.4.8); no item is shown an example of its own text family. Every program a prompt quotes is listed
([S2.2.1](s2.2.1_contract.md), T2.2.1.3). The user's first experiment, which shows the Zebra puzzle its own
program, is [S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)'s and no prompt of this stage.

### Task T2.2.4.2 — Step 1: the facts, the answer query and a partial ontology; and Q9

One call over the whole text, thinking off, under step 1's grammar where grammars are per step. Output, in
synthesis order: the facts, in the text's order, each statement as a fact or several in the text's own words, a
stored negation where the sentence denies, each with its sentence as `:source`; the answer query where the text
asks its question; then the declarations of the relations the facts and the query use, each with a `:why` that
reads its declaration; then the memberships the facts need as far as they are seen (`instance`, `type`, `is-a`,
*whatever else*), each with the sentence that names the thing as its `:source` where there is one. No import, no
rule, no `(solve …)`. What it decides is the user's list: which words name the same object, what is an object and
what is a relation, one fact a sentence or several, and whether the objects are of one kind or of several.

- **One call, the whole text in it**: which words name the same object is decided across sentences (*the Old Gold
  smoker* and *the man who smokes Chesterfields*; *He gave 2 to Alice.*). The first re-plan's instance pass, a call
  a sentence with the whole text in the prompt and the sentence marked, so that the `:source` is the marked
  sentence by construction, is kept as a mode of step 1, for the question of whether it helps; its split of the
  text into sentences is recorded, and a sentence may state no fact. A call a sentence repeats the prompt; with a
  kept context ([S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)) the shared prefix is evaluated once. The budget counts
  generated tokens (the frame's default 6), and the prompt's are recorded beside.
- **Membership is a relation the program chooses.** The kernel reads no name of it
  ([Q-M1f.436](../../../docs/decisions/engine/Q-M1f.436-the-kernel-reads-no-theorys-name.md)), and the library
  takes it as an argument. The examples fix one convention so that step 2 knows what to complete.
- **A number.** A quantity the theory computes with is a numeral of the calculator, `(n z 5)`, 12 being
  `(n (n z 1) 2)`, and step 1 writes it as the examples do, since it writes the facts. EinAf rewrites nothing the
  model wrote: a number written as a name is counted by the check. If the probe and this stage's runs show the
  model cannot write numerals, turning a decimal name into a numeral at assembly is the other direction of
  [S2.2.5](s2.2.5_from_text.md)'s formatter; taking it changes what *the model's program* means and is raised
  before it is done.
- **The answer query** (the first of the steps' design questions): the question translated as literally as a
  statement, over the facts' own relations, with its `:goal-text` sentence, T2.2.4.5.
- **A reply that does not parse** is a bug of the grammar where one is on ([S2.2.3](s2.2.3_gbnf.md), T2.2.3.2's
  second test), recorded as that; with no grammar it is the *invalid* outcome.
- **Q9**, the ontology's provenance, reframed by the ledger as theory selection. The three options in today's
  language: **B**, the model infers the vocabulary from the text, begun in step 1 and completed in step 2; **C**,
  the catalogue of the library, which step 2 selects from; **A**, a module given with the problem,
  `--module-dir DIR` on `einaf from-text`, which the program imports: a file beside a program is a module, and an
  import brings it whole ([`02-syntax.md` § Import](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/02-syntax.md#import)).
  Under A, step 1 is shown the module's declarations and still writes the facts, and step 2 imports the module
  for the ontology it holds. The working answer stands: B with C in the main arms, and A as the escape hatch,
  which is the widest condition of the library ablation
  ([S2.6.4](../p2.6_ablations/s2.6.4_representation_ablations.md)) and no part of the main arms. What the stage's
  runs can show: how far B gets on a family whose canonical vocabulary is a theory's own (the word problems), with
  A beside it. The verdict, with the runs it rests on, goes to the ledger.

### Task T2.2.4.3 — Step 2: the complete ontology and a partial theory

One call under step 2's grammar, `rule` forms off, with the text and step 1's program. The reply is **the ontology
whole**: every relation and membership the theory will read, the declarations' labels, the memberships and the
kinds step 1 left partial, which it may rename (the user: *"partial here means it is expected to change in next
step"*); and **the selection**: `import` forms with their `:symbols`, the facts that activate what was imported,
their arguments drawn from the program's names. It does not restate step 1's facts. Its ontology stands in the
assembled program in place of step 1's, and what it changed of it is recorded; a relation step 1's facts use and
step 2 no longer declares is a finding of the check.

**Synthesis is a second call with a cause.** If the reply carries the marker of a missing property (a line of a
fixed shape the prompt asks for and the grammar allows, naming the property and the relation), a second call
runs with `rule` forms on and the marker in its prompt: the user's *"some imported rules, some hand-written"*. The
rules it writes carry a `:why`, are categorised *generated inference rule* with the marker as their reason, and
their count per family is reported. On the families the library has no theory for (it has none for time and none
for tracking objects: [the audit, B.4](../analysis/audit_p2.2.md#b4-s224-the-passes)) the second call is
expected, and a literal vocabulary may need one to read its facts onto what the selected theory reads
([S2.2.0a](s2.2.0a_zebra_formulations.md)'s T2.2.0a.5).

### Task T2.2.4.4 — Step 3: the complete theory, and the hypothesis mechanism

One call under step 3's grammar, with the text and the program of steps 1 and 2. The reply completes the theory:
the rules still missing, each after the marker of the property it supplies, and **the hypothesis mechanism**:

- by the selection itself, where the catalogue's entry says the theory's obligations propose the candidates, and
  then nothing is written;
- `(solve :no-search)`, where the text states every fact there is and the answer is root's;
- a rule that concludes `(open F)`, or a written `(open F)`, where neither holds. That is synthesis, with its
  marker.

Where steps 2 and 3 divide the rules is what S2.2.1's hand-written steps show (T2.2.1.8), and the prompts follow
them. Whether the model is shown step 2's judgement at root before it writes step 3 is
[P2.6](../p2.6_ablations/README.md)'s arm; by default it is not.

**A synthesised rule may flag the program.** A rule that reads an `absent` over a relation a commitment can fill
makes the program not monotone, and the same rule under `(solve :no-search)` does not
([S2.2.1](s2.2.1_contract.md) has both runs). The steps forbid neither. The record holds the sites the static
check names, and the outcome is *flagged*, never certified
([Q-M2.11](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.11-what-is-a-flagged-program-to-the-loop.md)).

### Task T2.2.4.5 — The answer query, and the answer's form

Written in step 1 (T2.2.4.2). It is **the answer query**, the one query that carries a `:goal-text`
([the contract](s2.2.1_contract.md)): `(query :goal … :goal-text "…")`, the goal over the facts' own relations, a
relation only a rule concludes among them, so that the theory of steps 2 and 3 has to make it answerable; one
variable a thing asked for, named from the question; and a `:goal-text` that **is a sentence saying the answer in
the problem's words**, with a reference where each value stands, `"Bob has {?n} apples"`
([Q-M2.12](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.12-what-prints-the-answer.md)). The examples show a goal whose variables
bind names and numerals, so that the answer needs no formatter the family does not have
([S2.2.5](s2.2.5_from_text.md)). Any other query step 1 writes carries no `:goal-text` and is a check.

**The answer line** is given to step 1 with the text. It is [S2.5.2](../p2.5_harness/s2.5.2_baselines.md)'s
to write, one a family, the same words for every arm, and that stage comes after this one. So this stage writes
the two its own runs need (each asks for a sentence: how many, for the word problems; who drinks water and who
owns the zebra, for Zebra), each a prompt by hash, and S2.5.2 takes them over or rewords them, which is a new
experiment id.

- A reference in a `:goal-text` that the goal does not bind prints as written, `{?zz}`
  ([natural language, § 1](../../../docs/ein/user/03-syntax-and-semantics/14-natural-language.md#1-the-four-texts)):
  the kernel loads it, and the check counts it.
- The answer query writes no `:over`. Which part of R an answer is read over is
  [Q-M2.10](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.10-what-does-ein-certifies-a-unique-model-mean-on-r.md)'s, open, and the
  record carries each reading.
- For an external instance whose answer is a letter or *true*, *false*, *unknown*, the goal is the one the
  adapter of [S2.3.3](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.3_external_benchmarks.md) expects.

### Task T2.2.4.6 — Assembly in synthesis order, each step judged, and the contract's check

**Assembly.** The program's text is the steps' outputs one after another, the order they were made in (the steps'
third design question): step 1's facts and answer query, step 2's ontology in place of step 1's partial one, then
the theory of steps 2 and 3 with the hypothesis mechanism, each form as the model wrote it, each step under a
comment that names it and the record: the record keeps a program as written
([S2.5.1](../p2.5_harness/s2.5.1_experiment_record.md)). If [S2.2.0a](s2.2.0a_zebra_formulations.md)'s T2.2.0a.3
finds a form Ein reads only after another, the assembly moves that form and the record says so. A form's canonical
text (`ein_ir::dump_canonical`) is what the sidecar and the program's digest are keyed by, so that a form has one
key however it was spaced. Nothing the kernel does not read is added.

**Each step's program is judged** (the steps' second design question): the program assembled up to that step is
loaded and asked at root through the bounded call, and its feedback object and the check's findings that apply
to it are kept in the record. Only the complete program's judgement is the loop's.

**The check** is EinAf's, over each step's program and the complete one, the input and the catalogue. It reports,
and refuses nothing:

1. a `:source` that is no sentence of the input;
2. **a selection that is not active**: an activating fact whose name the file does not list from the module that
   declares its rule, or, read off root's saturation, a selected rule the events name no firing of and a
   selected obligation they name no `owe` of ([`05-events.md`](../../../docs/ein/ein.rs/05-events.md)). Which of
   the two readings the check uses is the stage's; the second is the kernel's own evidence;
3. **the mechanism**: the writer of H the kernel reports, and whether the program stated one;
4. a fact of a relation the program does not declare and no imported module has, and a fact or a goal's
   pattern of a relation, the program's or a module's, at a count it is not declared at. At step 1 this is the one
   hold on its facts, which no grammar has (S2.2.3). This re-plan's own first word problem over the calculator
   asked `(diff-of X Y ?n)`, three arguments where the answer has four: it loaded, the outcome was *one*, and the
   answer query had no row ([S2.2.5](s2.2.5_from_text.md));
5. a number written as a name where the relation's theory computes;
6. of the answer query: no query with a `:goal-text`, or more than one; a reference nothing binds; a template
   that holds more than its references, which is an answer written without the kernel;
7. **a step-1 statement written again otherwise**: a fact a later step writes with the `:source` of a sentence step
   1 stated, and differing from step 1's (the steps' fourth design question); and what step 2 changed of step 1's
   ontology, which is expected and recorded, not a finding.

A finding applies to a step's program from the step that could have avoided it on: the first, fourth, fifth and
sixth from step 1, the second and seventh from step 2, the third at step 3 alone, since a program before step 3
states no mechanism on purpose. Then the table of categories ([S2.2.1](s2.2.1_contract.md), T2.2.1.2), with the step of each form.
Findings and categories are the sidecar, kept in the record apart from the kernel's diagnostics. **The check is
built once, here**, because the one shot has to know what it produced. The lints of the feedback ladder
([S2.4.3](../p2.4_loop/s2.4.3_feedback_ladder.md)) are these findings given a sentence each; which of them a
level shows to the model is that stage's.

### Task T2.2.4.8 — The catalogue

Built first: steps 2 and 3 have nothing to select from without it.

- **Its generated part.** From the library the kernel resolved (`ein_ir::stdlib`: the modules and their text),
  each module parsed with `ein_ir::parse`: every rule's name, its parameters and its own `:why`. The library's
  manifest SHA stands at the top, which is `ein --version`'s `stdlib` line.
- **Its written part.** For each theory a program may select, an **entry program**: a few lines of Ein with the
  import line, the activating facts with their arguments, a fact or two and a query. The lines the prompt shows
  are cut from the entry program, so the model is shown text the kernel ran. An entry says what the selection
  gives the hypothesis mechanism.
- **Its test.** Each entry program is loaded and asked, through the bounded kernel call, and the test holds it to
  its stated effect: the rule fired or the obligation owes, the writer of H is the one stated, the query's answer
  is the one stated.
  [`tests/stdlib/`](../../../tests/README.md) already holds a program for each rule of the library, written to
  break and not to teach; an entry is written fresh and may be checked against it.
- **It holds the library's modules and no family's own.** The word problems' theory is four modules of
  `examples/word_problems/`, examples and no part of the library
  ([Q-M1f.334](../../../docs/decisions/corpus/Q-M1f.334-the-word-theory-is-examples-not-a-module.md)): it is the
  canonical program's, and the main arms do not show it to the model. Two families have no theory in the library
  at all, and that is where a formalizer's synthesis is exercised
  ([P2.3](../p2.3_benchmark/README.md)); a catalogue that offered a family's module would take the measurement
  away. Offering one is the widest condition of the library ablation
  ([S2.6.4](../p2.6_ablations/s2.6.4_representation_ablations.md)), reached by `--module-dir`; an entry for
  such a module, where that condition is run, is built as the library's are and names the directory the program
  is loaded from.
- **What a literal vocabulary needs of it**: [S2.2.0a](s2.2.0a_zebra_formulations.md)'s T2.2.0a.5 says what
  the literal vocabularies need of the library that it does not have
  ([E-110](evaluation/stdlib/E-110-the-library-has-no-reading-of-a-literal-vocabulary.md),
  [Q-M2.23](open_questions/kernel/Q-M2.23-does-the-library-grow-a-reading-of-a-literal-vocabulary.md)); an entry is written for what the library has,
  and the rest is the finding S2.2.0a files, whose remedy (a module in `stdlib/` or not) is the user's.
- **Its sizes.** The whole catalogue, and the *minimal* one of G9 ([P2.6](../p2.6_ablations/README.md)); each has
  its hash and its length in the model's tokens.

Where the stage reads how a theory is written:
[`04-stdlib/`](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/04-stdlib/README.md), a page a family, with
[the relation algebra](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/04-stdlib/02-relation-algebra.md),
[the bijections](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/04-stdlib/04-bijections.md),
[the slots](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/04-stdlib/05-slots.md) and
[the calculator](../../../docs/ein/user/04-reference/04-stdlib/07-calculator.md); and

### Task T2.2.4.9 — The whole program in one call

The prompt of [S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)'s probe, kept as a mode of the formalizer: one call, the whole
grammar or none, the program written in synthesis order, the same assembly and the same check, on the complete
program alone. It is what the tool arm writes and what research plan B1 says not to assume worse than a
decomposition; the steps are a decomposition the model may not need. Both modes run on the phase's material and
the record says which a program came from. The comparison on the benchmark is G8's
([P2.6](../p2.6_ablations/README.md)). Which mode baseline B2 runs with is decided here: the steps, unless this
stage's runs show the one call no worse on both materials, in which case the simpler is taken and the ledger says
so.

### Task T2.2.4.10 — What the stage found of Ein

The stage is where a model first selects from the library. To [the evaluation of Ein](../evaluation/README.md), each
entry with its program and command: which selections the model misspelt and whether anything the kernel said

## Risks

- **A selection misspelt is silent.** It is the commonest mistake a model can make in steps 2 and 3, and the
  kernel loads it and answers something else. The check's second finding is the only thing that sees it, which is
  why the catalogue's entries are run and not written from memory.
- **Three steps compound.** A coreference step 1 got wrong is wrong in every later step, and the facts are the
  part the one shot does not change. The per-step reports say where a failed program first went wrong, and
  T2.2.4.9's one call is the comparison.
- **A step's program with no theory is not idle**: solved, it is answered by the blind enumerator
  ([E-13](../evaluation/hypotheses/E-13-a-program-that-names-no-hypothesis-mechanism-is-answered-by.md)). A step
  before step 3 is judged at root for that reason, and the check reads no mechanism of it.
- **Literal facts may need rules no library has.** Step 2's second call and step 3 carry that cost, which
  [S2.2.0a](s2.2.0a_zebra_formulations.md) measures on the Zebra puzzle; a high rate of synthesis on a family the
  library covers is a finding about the facts' vocabulary, not about the model alone.
- **One call over the whole text may lose a sentence.** The report of which sentences have no fact is how that
  shows, and the sentence-by-sentence mode is the comparison.
- **The prompt has to fit.** The excerpt, the catalogue and the examples together are cut to the context the
  device holds ([S2.2.2](s2.2.2_model_runtime.md)); the minimal catalogue may be what the default has to be. Step
  1 needs no catalogue, which is one gain of the steps.
