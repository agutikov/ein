## Context

*Do not reduce evaluation to answer accuracy.* An answer can be right for the wrong program, a program can be
valid and unfaithful, a repair can fix one error and add two, and a system can be accurate and unaffordable. Four
layers, defined **once**, in one module of `einaf/src/`, computed from records only by `einaf evaluate`, each
with a fixture whose value was worked out by hand before the code existed.

Everything is reported **per family and aggregate**, and the aggregate is a **macro-average over families**: the
claim is about reasoning families, the families differ in size, and a micro-average would let the largest decide
the number. The gold class (one answer, ambiguous, unsatisfiable:
[README § The defaults](../README.md#the-defaults-the-re-plan-took), 8) is always beside the family figure.

**An answer is a short text, for every arm** (the same list, 7). The record holds the answer, where it came from
and the gold short answer; [S2.5.2](s2.5.2_baselines.md)'s reader and normaliser are shared. **The kernel's word
is not the instance's class**: `examples/word_problems/gap.ein`, whose right answer is *not enough information*,
answers `Solution` under `--single` (the gap is a fact the program derives), and
`examples/word_problems/boxes12.ein`, two states that give one answer, answers `Ambiguity`. So detection is
scored on the answer, and what the kernel said is shown against the class in a table of its own.

### The answer of an arm with a program

[P2.3](../p2.3_benchmark/README.md) leaves *how a class is scored* to this stage, and
[P2.4](../p2.4_loop/README.md) leaves *what short text an outcome is scored as*. Both are one rule, **the answer
rule**: from what the kernel found of an episode's final program to the short text that is scored. It reads the
shapes the benchmark states a class in, on the model's program in place of the canonical one: the outcome, and
the classes of the program's answer query (the contract says which query that is:
[S2.2.1](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.1_contract.md)) over the part of R the query's own `:over` names.

| the kernel found | scored as |
|---|---|
| a state in R, and the answer query has one class, with a row, over a complete set | the rows, each through the query's `:goal-text` and EinAf's formatters: *one answer* |
| a state in R, and two classes or more | the ambiguous text, with the classes' sentences kept beside it as its alternatives |
| a state in R, and one class with no row | the ambiguous text: the program derives nothing that answers the question |
| a state in R, and one class with a row over the states one question listed, not a complete set | what the wider question of the final program says (below); no answer where that is not decided either |
| *inconsistent* | the unsatisfiable text |
| *incomplete* | **no answer**, counted apart: the kernel's reply does not tell an unsatisfiable problem from a formalization that is not finished. Scored as the unsatisfiable text under the rule's other value, and both are reported |
| *invalid*, *not decided*, *flagged* | no answer |

The two fixed texts are EinAf's ([S2.2.5](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.5_from_text.md) words them) and are the ones
[S2.5.2](s2.5.2_baselines.md)'s answer line gives every arm. Where the gold lists the alternatives of an
ambiguous instance and the answer has some, their agreement is a column of its own and no part of accuracy.

**The rule is a parameter of `einaf evaluate`, named in `metrics.json`**, and is this stage's default, not a
ruling: a record keeps the outcome, the answer query's classes with their rows and whether they are over a
complete set, under every question asked of the final program, so another rule scores the same records with no
new run. What the rule calls *one answer* is next door to what *certified* means, which is the user's.

**Beside the rule, the class each reading's word says.** The kernel's word alone also names a class: `Solution`
one answer, `Ambiguity` (or `k_s` of two or more) ambiguous, `Contradiction` unsatisfiable, `Open` incomplete.
Detection is reported that way too, a column a reading of the next section, beside the rule's column. The two
differ exactly where the word is not the class, and how often they differ, per family and shape, is part of
what [Q-M2.10](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.10-what-does-ein-certifies-a-unique-model-mean-on-r.md) is decided on.
[S2.3.4](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.4_splits.md)'s Level B run has the same columns.

### Certified, under each reading

What *Ein certifies a unique model* means on R is open, and the user decides it after experiments
([Q-M2.10](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.10-what-does-ein-certifies-a-unique-model-mean-on-r.md)). The old definition,
*one model and an exhausted search*, has no one translation
([`audit_p2.5_p2.6.md`](../analysis/audit_p2.5_p2.6.md) § B.4). So no metric fixes it. Three questions are asked
of every episode's final program (the filter's last; for the tool the program its answer rests on, which
[S2.4.6](../p2.4_loop/s2.4.6_ein_as_a_tool.md) names; an episode with no program has none), and each is a
reading:

| reading | the question | certified when |
|---|---|---|
| **single** | `--single`, over all of R | the word is `Solution` |
| **maximal** | `--maximal --single`, over the maximal line | the word is `Solution` |
| **described** | a bare solve: R described | `k_s = 1` and the run exhausted; `k_c` is recorded beside it |

They differ on real programs: `examples/lattice/03_state_hash_collision.ein` is `Ambiguity` under the first,
`Solution` under the second, and five states in eight commitments under the third (run the three on it). The
description is also where the answer query's classes are over the set the query names, which the answer rule
reads. A reading whose run stopped before it decided is *not decided*, counted in a column of its own and
certified under nothing. **A flagged program is certified under no reading**
([Q-M2.11](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.11-what-is-a-flagged-program-to-the-loop.md)): the kernel prints a word for it
and claims nothing of that word.

```text
for each reading r:
P(correct  | certified_r)  =  #{certified_r ∧ answer correct}   / #{certified_r}
P(faithful | certified_r)  =  #{certified_r ∧ program faithful} / #{certified_r}
```

Both are computed under each reading, **per family and per shape**, with numerator and denominator printed. The
shape is what the user's answer names (*"it depends on problem shape"*): from the benchmark, the family, the
gold class and its finer split; from the kernel, of the final program, the size of the universe
(`region.universe.candidates`: nothing is hypothesised in a program such as `examples/word_problems/bob1.ein`),
whether root owes (`root.owes`), and which writers H was taken from (`ein_infer::oblgen::Mode`, the word
`ein show hyps` prints). *Faithful* is [S2.4.4](../p2.4_loop/s2.4.4_faithfulness.md)'s: on an instance with a
canonical program it is arithmetic against that program; elsewhere it is the judge
[Q-M2.3](../open_questions/answer/Q-M2.3-what-is-the-unit-of-faithfulness-without-a-gold-theory.md) settles, and the
number names the judge. The gap between the two probabilities is the research plan's distinction
between faithfulness and satisfiability as a number; every table that shows either shows both. **That table,
reading by family by shape, is what the user decides Q-M2.10 on.**

## Acceptance

- `einaf/src/` defines every metric above exactly once, as a function from a record to a value with its
  numerator and denominator; `metrics.json` is `einaf-metrics/1` and carries all three for every metric, and the
  answer rule's name.
- A fixture record, twelve instances across three families and the three classes with a value worked by hand for
  every metric, evaluates to those values. Among its programs is one whose three readings differ, one that is
  flagged, and one for each row of the answer rule.
- `einaf evaluate` over one record twice is byte-identical, and reads no file outside the record.
- Both conditional probabilities are reported under each reading, with their denominators, per family and shape,
  and on external instances with the judge's flag. No reading is selected.
- The one-shot numbers of [S2.2.5](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.5_from_text.md)'s record evaluate again under this
  module with no change of meaning.
- The kernel's cost and limits are numbers in the record and entries of
  [the evaluation of Ein](../evaluation/README.md).

## Tasks

### Task T2.5.3.1 — The module and `einaf-metrics/1`

One function a metric, with the three-part return; a registry that names them; `metrics.json`'s schema under the
record's versioning rule. A metric that cannot be computed on an instance (constraint recall on an external
instance) gives `n/a` with the reason, never a zero: a zero in a table is a claim.

### Task T2.5.3.2 — H1 and H2

The answer's comparison, by the shared normaliser, strict and lenient, and the answer rule above. The lenient
form reads an answer domain; the benchmark's instance has no such field today, so the stage asks it of
[S2.3.1](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.1_families_and_seed_set.md) for the families that have one (the entities a
*who* question ranges over), and where an instance has none the lenient form is containment alone and the table
says so. Detection both ways, because an arm that says *ambiguous* of everything has perfect recall.

**The gold claim**, for an arm with a program: the instance's `:expect` claim is put to the final program and
the kernel checks it, as `ein test` checks a claim. It needs the program to name what the claim names. Where it
names a relation the program lacks the kernel refuses the pair at the load and names the relation; where it
names an atom the program lacks the kernel reports the claim refuted, as it reports a wrong answer, so EinAf
tells the two apart by the program's own names. Both are **not comparable**, counted apart and never as wrong.
How a program's names are held to the text's is the contract's
([S2.2.1](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.1_contract.md)). The claim's verdict and the reader's are two instruments on
one answer: their two-by-two table is reported, and a disagreement is read by hand.

H2's rates from the feedback object's outcome and its diagnostics' kinds; faithfulness as S2.4.4 hands it
over; constraint precision and recall by the unit [S2.3.2](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.2_generators.md) defines. **The
key of a form is settled with S2.3.2**, which leaves it between the library's canonical dump
(`ein_ir::dump_canonical`, which no command prints and which prints a form as written) and the loaded fact. The
default is the form under full names, and the test that decides is an invariance: one program written under its
three import forms gives one set of keys, as it gives one answer.

### Task T2.5.3.3 — H3 and H4

H3 is a fold over `transitions.jsonl`: the diff of each repair, the diagnostics and the faithfulness events of
each iteration, the condition that ended the episode; and for the tool, its calls, their refusals, and whether
the last message is what the last run printed. H4 from the envelopes (tokens, calls), EinAf's own clock, and
the work each kernel line carries. Cost per correctly solved instance is the one number of H4 that divides by a
number of H1, and its denominator is printed. The kernel's work is never added to the tokens.

### Task T2.5.3.4 — The readings and the two conditional probabilities

`einaf readings <record>`: for each episode's final program, the three questions under the default budget, the
shape fields, the answer query's classes, and the gold claim's verdict, written to `readings.jsonl`, a derived
file ([S2.5.1](s2.5.1_experiment_record.md)). Both arrangements ask the readings of the final program at an
episode's end ([S2.4.1](../p2.4_loop/s2.4.1_state_machine.md), [S2.4.6](../p2.4_loop/s2.4.6_ein_as_a_tool.md)),
and a question the episode asked is not asked again: its feedback object is in the record. `einaf run` calls
this at the end of a run; it can be called again, or a reading added, on the record's commit with no call of the
model. Then both probabilities under
each reading, with numerator, denominator and the external-instance flag, and a third line, the gap, which is
what § 9.3 of the paper is about ([M5](../../m5_presentation/README.md)).

**The readings of one program are held to each other.** Where two of the three decided, they must agree as the
kernel promises its commands agree
([Q-M1e2.32](../../../docs/decisions/answer/Q-M1e2.32-no-two-commands-disagree-and-the-oracle-is.md)): `Solution`
over all of R with `k_s = 1`, `Ambiguity` with `k_s` of two or more, `Contradiction` or `Open` with `k_s = 0`.
A disagreement is a wrong answer of the kernel's: it is filed in
[the wrong-answer register](../../wrong_answers/README.md) with the program as its witness, and the instance is
marked in the table.

### Task T2.5.3.5 — Fixtures and tests

The twelve-instance fixture record under `einaf/tests/`, worked by hand in a page beside it that shows the
arithmetic; one test a metric; the determinism test; a test that removes each file of the record in turn and
checks that the evaluator names what it could not compute. All in EinAf's gate step.

### Task T2.5.3.6 — The kernel's cost and limits, as numbers

The second main idea's share of this stage
([README § The two main ideas](../README.md#the-two-main-ideas-the-user-2026-11-25)): the records say what Ein
cost and where it stopped on programs a model wrote, and this task turns that into numbers for
[the evaluation of Ein](../evaluation/README.md).

**The replay.** `einaf kernel-cost <record>` asks each recorded question again, of the recorded program, in a
process that holds no model: one program a process, no event stream, the cores and the power source recorded,
refused on battery. It takes the wall of the load and of the question apart, and the process's peak resident
memory, into `kernel_cost.jsonl`. It **holds the replay's work equal to the record's**: the kernel is
deterministic, so a difference in `saturations` or `facts` is a finding, and the file is not written over it. A
time taken during a run, with the model on the same machine, is the episode's latency; a time of the replay is
the kernel's cost. The matcher's own counters are a feature of the engine's crates that the shipped binary
compiles out ([`04-api.md`](../../../docs/ein/ein.rs/04-api.md) § 5); whether a build of EinAf with them on is a
second instrument of the replay is the stage's to decide.

**The numbers**, per arm and per family, each from records:

- the work and the cost of each question on one program, side by side: what `--single`, the maximal line and a
  description each cost, which says which question a loop can afford at every iteration;
- how often each bound was met: the default `--region-budget` reached, a flagged program past `--region-limit`,
  a call the harness stopped from outside ([S2.4.1](../p2.4_loop/s2.4.1_state_machine.md));
- the outcomes' distribution, and the flagged rate with the sites the kernel named;
- the refusals by diagnostic kind, most frequent first, and for each kind the fraction of repairs that cleared
  it at each feedback level: whether the kernel's message is one a model can act on;
- what loaded in silence. By EinAf's lints of the contract, which say what the kernel does not
  ([S2.4.3](../p2.4_loop/s2.4.3_feedback_ladder.md)): a theory selected under a name the file does not list, a
  relation misspelt or written at another count. By the kernel's own values: a program with no writer of H that
  the blind enumerator answered (`ein_infer::oblgen::Mode`), and a state alive with a fact beside its stored
  negation because the program imports no contradiction theory;
- the load's share of the kernel's wall.

**The limits**, as cases: the largest program each family's instances produced against the budget; every *not
decided*; every disagreement of T2.5.3.4; every refusal whose message misled the repair that followed. Each is
an entry of the evaluation with its record id and its program.

## Notes

- *Answer accuracy* is the score and is over what a sentence states; *exact match* and *containment* stand
  beside it because each is what a sceptical reader computes first, and because the distance between them and the
  score says how much of an arm's accuracy rests on the reading of its sentences.
- **How a free sentence is read for what it states is this stage's first design task**, since the user ruled the
  answer a sentence after the re-plan was written: the gold carries its values by the thing each is bound to
  (the answer query's rows, [S2.3.1](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.1_families_and_seed_set.md)); an arm that runs Ein
  is read off its rows; an arm that answers in text (B0, B1, and B6 where it did not use its last run) is read by
  one reader for all, the same on every arm, whose rule is written down and whose errors are counted on the
  hand-read sample of [S2.5.2](s2.5.2_baselines.md). The paragraphs below that say *strict* and *lenient* were
  written to the earlier default and are read as *exact match* and *containment*.
- The plan's `stats` block, its enterings and the counters named after them are gone with the walk
  ([`audit_p2.5_p2.6.md`](../analysis/audit_p2.5_p2.6.md) § A.1, § B.2): the unit a route counts is a saturation,
  and a set a licence decides costs none.
- The description is the costly reading: on an under-determined program of Zebra's size it runs to the default
  budget ([`analysis/` § 2](../analysis/README.md#2-what-the-kernel-says-today-where-the-plan-expects-the-walk)).
  It is asked once an episode, of the final program, under the budget
  [S2.4.1](../p2.4_loop/s2.4.1_state_machine.md) gives that question; a description the budget stops is
  recorded as stopped and its reading as not established, never as a count; and what it cost is one of
  T2.5.3.6's numbers.
