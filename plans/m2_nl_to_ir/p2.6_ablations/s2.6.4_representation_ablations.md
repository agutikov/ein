## What P2.2 hands this stage

- **G8: the steps' cut was not varied by P2.2.** S2.2.4's conditions vary the grammar and the steps against one

  have their first numbers there, on development texts.
- **G9: how a library is described was not varied either.** The condition that offers a family's own modules shows
  them by their forms with no description, and thirteen runs imported none

  [S2.2d.3](../p2.2d_formalizer_corrected/s2.2d.3_entries_described.md) describes an entry for its use and measures

- **The grammar off was measured on sixteen texts** by S2.2.4: with no grammar the steps load 11 programs against
  14, a selection misspelt 11 times and a `:source` that is no sentence 7 times; the one call under the whole

## Context

Four ablations share one shape, *what the model is given to write from, and what it is allowed to write*, and
they are one stage because the arms compose: a decomposition (G8) under a small library (G9) with a parse in the
prompt (the link-grammar arm) and no grammar over the tokens is one config, and the record names all four axes.

**The link-grammar arm is the old plan's fifth phase, carried whole.** The user's open question, *does feeding
link-grammar output to the LLM enrich the input usefully?*, had a runner, a serialiser, an A/B and a
pre-registered rule. What changed is the metric (constraint precision and recall on the synthetic families,
where the canonical program is exact, with answer accuracy beside it) and the place (an arm here, since G8 asks
the same question of the output side). The hypothesis is as plausible as it was: a linkage exposes the structure
of heads and dependents a model might use to disambiguate, and it is as plausible that an instruction-tuned model
does that internally and the pre-pass is noise. The point is to **measure, not assume**, and the arm is off by
default unless it measures a win.

**The parser is installed and its driver is written.** The old stage's first act was to register a submodule
and build a Python binding. Neither is needed
([`audit_p2.5_p2.6.md`](../analysis/audit_p2.5_p2.6.md) § B.5): [`nl2smt/`](../../../nl2smt/README.md) drives
the system's `link-parser` by its command line and reads the linkages as it prints them, the next linkage of a
sentence for each empty line after it, with tests under each installed parser. `nl2smt/` is external to Ein and
to the process and its content does not change; the arm's runner is EinAf's own, does what `nl2smt/nl2smt.py`'s
`linkages` does, and `nl2smt/` is the reference for how the parser is driven and what it prints.

**The fragment is not Ein, and is not made to be.** The old serialiser's
`(link-grammar :sentence "…" :linkages (… (det "the" Norwegian) …))` is refused at the parse: a form takes a
closed list of keywords, which `:sentence` and `:links` are not in, and a string is written only after `:why`,
`:source` or `:goal-text`. It was *context for the prompt, never a constraint*; so it is plain text in the
prompt, and nothing of it is a form a model could copy into its program as a fact.

**G8 asks whether an explicit intermediate helps.** The research plan's B1 allows a decomposition (*entities,
relations, constraints, query*) before the program and says *evaluate whether this intermediate representation
helps rather than assuming that it does*. [S2.2.4](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.4_passes.md)'s three steps are already a
decomposition *in Ein*; the G8 arm is a pass in front of them that writes a plain JSON semantic model the steps
are then conditioned on. The user's four oracle ablations
([F13](../../followups/f13_puzzles_beyond_zebra/ideas.md)) are the same axis cut the other way, what the model
is *given* of the canonical program, and they are the most informative arms of the stage, because each switches
off exactly one thing a failure could be blamed on:

| arm | what the model receives | what it writes | what it isolates |
|---|---|---|---|
| **text + known theory** | the text and the canonical program's *theory* part | the ontology, the instance, the query | semantic parsing, with the selection of a theory taken out |
| **program + unknown theory** | the canonical program's ontology, instance and query, and **no text** | the theory part only | the selection or the writing of a theory, with parsing taken out |
| **program + known theory** | the whole canonical program | nothing: the kernel alone | the kernel; right by construction, since it is the benchmark's own check, and the number that matters is its cost |
| **text + unknown theory** | the text | everything | the whole pipeline: B2 one shot, B5 with the filter |

*Where the 18 % goes* is the question these four answer, in the user's phrase, and the table is per family,
because the families whose theory the catalogue does not offer are expected to fail on the second row and
nowhere else. **The theory part of a program is more than its imports** today: the import lines with the names
they list, the activators, the rules that conclude a hypothesis `(open F)`, a `(solve …)` form's lists, and the
import of the contradiction ([`audit_p2.5_p2.6.md`](../analysis/audit_p2.5_p2.6.md) § B.5). Which forms of a
canonical program are its theory is the benchmark's to mark ([P2.3](../p2.3_benchmark/README.md)).

**G9 asks how much is the library.** The research plan's three conditions, *no stdlib, minimal, full*, mean
something else than when the stage was written (the same audit, § B.6):

- **`std.core` is in every program**, with no import line, and a library without it is refused
  ([Q-M1f.551](../../../docs/decisions/stdlib/Q-M1f.551-std-core-is-loaded-into-every-program.md)). So *no
  stdlib* cannot be an empty library: the smallest library a program can be given is `std.core` alone.
- **An import brings its module whole, with its own imports**
  ([Q-M1f.247](../../../docs/decisions/imports/Q-M1f.247-every-import-brings-its-module-whole.md)). So a list
  of permitted imports is a list of closures: `std.algebra` alone is `std.algebra`, `std.macro` and
  `std.contradiction`.
- **The contradiction is a theory a program imports**
  ([Q-M1f.557](../../../docs/decisions/stdlib/Q-M1f.557-the-contradiction-is-a-theory-a-program-imports.md)).
  With no import, a state that holds a fact beside its stored negation is alive unless the model writes the
  rule that kills it. That moves contradiction detection under the smallest condition for a reason that is not
  *reusable theory*, and the reading has to keep the two apart.
- **The obligations a program usually owes by are four rules of the library.** With none imported a state owes
  only what a rule of the program's own makes it owe, and H is what the program's hypothesis rules and written
  `(open F)` facts give, or else what the blind enumerator guesses.

So the three conditions are lists of what a program may import:

| condition | a program may import | what it then has |
|---|---|---|
| **core** | nothing | `std.core`: a held `(false)` ends a state, and nothing else. The model writes every rule, its contradiction among them |
| **minimal** | `std.algebra` | its closure: the relation algebra's properties with their two obligations, `std.macro`, and the contradiction |
| **full** | any module of the library, and a family's own modules where the main table's catalogue offers them | the library as [`stdlib/README.md`](../../../stdlib/README.md) lists it, the catalogue as [S2.2.4](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.4_passes.md) builds it |

Which instances a condition takes theory away from is read off the instance's own metadata, which names the
library modules and the family's modules its canonical program imports
([S2.3.1](../p2.3_benchmark/s2.3.1_families_and_seed_set.md)).

The condition is enforced where it cannot be argued with: by the harness in every arm, which reads the imports
off the parsed program and rejects one outside the list as *outside the condition*, a refusal of EinAf's and
never the kernel's *invalid*; and by the grammar where one is on, which lists the permitted import targets, so
that a forbidden module cannot be written. The catalogue in the prompt lists the same set. The library on disk
is the same in all three, so the manifest's SHA in the record is the same, and a record names its condition by
the list and the closure it gives. This is also the ablation that reads on
[F8](../../followups/f8_FCA_RCA_odis_tptp/ideas.md)'s `C(n)`: how many distinct rule shapes the model writes
under **core**, across families, is the first point of that curve taken by a machine.

**The grammar off** is [the phase's](README.md#the-three-the-rulings-added): the filter as it is against the
filter with no grammar over the tokens. It is here because it changes what the model is allowed to write, and
because G9's enforcement is the grammar's wherever a grammar is on.

### The pre-registered readings

Written here, before any run, in the old rule's form:

- **Link grammar**: on the synthetic families at B2, three seeds, `F1_augmented − F1_baseline` over constraint
  precision and recall: above `0.03`, on by default; below `0.0`, deprecated, and the code path removed;
  between, an option, `--link-grammar`, off by default. Answer accuracy is reported beside it and is not part of
  the rule; neither are the prompt tokens the linkages add, which are reported.
- **G8**: the decomposition must beat direct generation on constraint recall *and* not lose on faithfulness (the
  extra pass is one more place to invent). If it wins on recall and loses on faithfulness the reading is *the
  intermediate helps coverage and hurts grounding*, and the arm stays an option, not the default.
- **The oracle arms**: the gap between *text + known theory* and *text + unknown theory* is the cost of
  selecting a theory; the gap between *program + unknown theory* and all right is the cost of writing one on the
  families the library has no module for. The reading is the pair of gaps per family, with no threshold: the
  numbers are the result.
- **G9**: if **full** is near **minimal**, the reusable theory beyond the relation algebra is not where the
  performance comes from. If **core** is near **full** on the families whose theory the library holds, the model
  is deriving the library again and the library's value is cost, reported as generated tokens per solved
  instance. Either is a finding. Under **core**, contradiction detection is read apart, with the count of final
  programs that were alive holding a fact beside its stored negation.
- **The grammar off**: read first on parse success, then on accuracy at a matched budget, on B2 and B5. Within
  the main table's tolerance of the filter with its grammar: the grammar is not what makes the filter work, and
  stays an option. The grammar ahead: it stays on. The grammar behind: constrained decoding costs content, and
  that is the finding.

## Acceptance

- The arm's runner produces linkages on the seed set's anchors from the installed `link-parser`; its version
  and its dictionary's are fields of the record; a sentence the parser gives several linkages yields several in
  the prompt. No submodule is added and no binding is built.
- The four oracle arms, the three library conditions and the grammar off are configs in
  [S2.5.1](../p2.5_harness/s2.5.1_experiment_record.md)'s schema; the library condition is enforced by the
  harness in every arm and by the grammar where one is on, and is in the record as its list and the closure.
- Every arm is run on `val`, three seeds, under the budget rule; tables from records; each read by its
  pre-registered rule.
- [Q11](../open_questions/formalizer/Q11-link-grammar-value.md) is resolved beside its entry, with the numbers per family,
  and the code matches the verdict: on by default, an option, or removed. If removed, EinAf's runner goes and
  `nl2smt/` stays as it is.
- What the stage found of Ein is in [the evaluation of Ein](../evaluation/README.md).

## Tasks

### Task T2.6.4.1 — The parser

The old task, *register the submodule and build the binding*, is moot: the installed command is enough, and
`nl2smt/` shows it (audit § B.5). What is left: EinAf's check that `link-parser` is there and which version it
is, at the start of a run of this arm, into the record; and the decision of the submodule closed as *not added*,
with the one command that would add it kept in [`nl2smt/README.md`](../../../nl2smt/README.md), where it is.
Tests of the arm skip where the parser is missing, as `nl2smt/`'s do: EinAf's gate step installs nothing for it.

### Task T2.6.4.2 — Runner and serialiser

In `einaf/src/`: a sentence in, its first few linkages out, by the command line `nl2smt/nl2smt.py` uses; and a
serialiser from a linkage to the lines of the prompt. **The form of those lines is the stage's to fix on
`dev`** between the parser's own print and a list of labelled links, a link a line; the default is the list. The
steps' prompts with the linkages added are a second template each, by hash like every other. Tests, the old three
re-aimed: a Zebra sentence parses; the serialiser is a function of the parser's output, the same text twice; a
sentence with more than one linkage yields more than one.

### Task T2.6.4.3 — The decomposition

The pass in front: the text to `semantic.json` (`entities`, `relations` with their arguments, `constraints` as
spans of the text paired with mentions of relations, `query`), under a grammar or a schema of its own, recorded
as a generation; the steps conditioned on it by the prompt. One flag of the config.

### Task T2.6.4.4 — The oracle arms

Four configs, each defined by which parts of the canonical program the prompt supplies and which the model
writes. The harness assembles the program from the supplied parts and the generated ones, asks the kernel as
every arm does, and records which parts were canonical. *Program + known theory* is the benchmark's check under
another name and runs for its cost: the kernel on programs a person or a generator wrote, per family, which is a
baseline of [the evaluation of Ein](../evaluation/README.md) beside the kernel on programs a model wrote.

### Task T2.6.4.5 — The library conditions

The three lists; the harness's check of a program's imports against the list; the grammar and the catalogue
generated per condition (the whole one and G9's minimal one are
[S2.2.4](../p2.2_formalizer/s2.2.4_passes.md)'s; **core** has none); the list and its closure in the record.
**The word problems run under full only**, and are marked *not run* under the other two with the reason: their
theory is the calculator, the modules under `stdlib/math/`, and the family's four modules, which no smaller
condition holds and no model is asked to write. The `C(n)` count under
**core**: distinct rule shapes per family and cumulative, **at more than one reading of the same shape, each
named**. The census of the corpus's rules reports several readings and chooses none, and which is kept is
[M12](../../m12_rules_as_data/README.md)'s open question and the user's; this stage chooses none either.

### Task T2.6.4.6 — Run, read, resolve

Three seeds, the arms, `val`; the tables; the readings applied; Q11 written beside its entry; the code made to
match.

### Task T2.6.4.7 — The grammar off

B2 and B5 with no grammar in the sampler chain, everything else as the main table's records name it: the
harness still accepts a program and nothing else, and a text the kernel refuses at the parse is an iteration
spent, with the refusal fed back at the arm's level. Parse success is read from the feedback object's
diagnostics, so the arm is also the measurement of which of the parser's refusals a model meets when nothing
holds its syntax, and whether the parser's repairs help it.

### Task T2.6.4.8 — What the stage found of Ein

To [the evaluation of Ein](../evaluation/README.md): under **core**, how often a model's program was alive with a
fact beside its negation, and how often the blind enumerator answered because nothing wrote H; under
**minimal** and **full**, how often a theory was selected under a name the file did not list and sat inert;
with the grammar off, the parse refusals by kind and the repairs that followed; from the oracle arms, the
kernel's cost on canonical programs against its cost on a model's. Each with its record.

## Notes
