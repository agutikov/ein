## The gap this closes

*(Closed 2026-08-17 — kept as written so the defect the stage removes stays
legible.)*

Before S1.21.8 `(absent P)` was evaluated three times, all *inside* the closure and
all against a transient mid-saturation KB: at enqueue-time matching
([`match._run_steps`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/match.py)),
re-evaluated at fire time to close the enqueue/fire race
([`match.absents_still_pass`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/match.py),
`Saturator.naf_dropped`), and implicitly by the semi-naive enqueue, which
must **full-match** any plan whose delta relation lands inside an
`AbsentGuard`
([`saturator._absent_relations`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/saturator.py)).
Soundness rests on the monotone-growth argument plus priority-band
discipline, with [`naf_deps`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/naf_deps.py)
as a static map of which rules depend on the fire-time re-eval. The world a
guard queries is therefore *"the KB as of this dequeue"* — not the
saturated world `W` that
[`absent_semantics.md`](../../../docs/kernel/inference/absent_semantics.md)'s
`W ⊭ ∃x̄.Pθ` reading names. This placement (leak **L1** of the seam
census) is the common root of the retired-`unconditional_facts` unsoundness
(no negative provenance — corollary C2), the NAF-unsound deletion-MUS
(C3), and the absent-flip semi-naive split (C5).

## Shape (from R6 §2 — recorded, not executed)

1. **Compile split** — lift `AbsentGuard`s out of `JoinPlan.steps`
   ([`compile.py`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/compile.py)) into a
   separate `plan.naf_guards`; the residue is a purely positive
   Scan/Join/Guard plan; `match._run_steps` drops its AbsentGuard arm.
2. **Alternating (two-phase) saturation** — inner loop fires only positive
   plans to quiescence; *at quiescence* evaluate every parked NAF-guarded
   binding against the stalled KB (= the world `W`); admissions re-enter
   the inner loop; outer loop to fixpoint. Retires the enqueue-time NAF
   eval, the fire-time re-check, and the absent-flip full-match split in
   one move; guards are only ever judged against a positive fixpoint,
   making the `W ⊭ ∃x̄.Pθ` reading literal and demoting priority-band
   protection from load-bearing to advisory.
3. **A boundary contract type** — a `World` view (saturated fork KB + its
   commitment `C`) exposing `holds(pattern)` / `absent(pattern, bindings)`;
   guard evaluation goes through it exclusively;
   [`absent_semantics.md`](../../../docs/kernel/inference/absent_semantics.md)
   (worlds model, E1–E3, C1–C7) is this contract's spec.
4. **Negative provenance** — firings admitted through the boundary record
   their guards as negative premises (`Provenance` grows an
   `absent_premises` field: `Deps(Y) = PositiveDeps(Y) ∪ NegativeDeps(Y)`,
   the review §2 formula). This is the missing object that made
   `unconditional_facts` unsound (retired, P1.21 R2) and deletion-MUS
   unsound ([`frontier.py`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/frontier.py),
   P1.21 R3); with it both become revisitable. Composes with
   [S1.21.7](s1.21.7_multi_justification_provenance.md) (multi-justification
   provenance — same seam, orthogonal extension).
5. **Re-eval points at world transitions** — each root writeback
   (`(not h)` singleton death, forced-positive promotion, lookahead
   kill-cache — the `<monotonic-unconditional>` / `<forced-positive>` /
   `<lookahead-dies-immediately>` writebacks in
   [`monotonic/_helpers.py`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/monotonic/_helpers.py)
   / [`hypgen.py`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/hypgen.py)) is a
   world transition; the boundary re-evaluates parked guards there,
   replacing the ad-hoc re-saturations. Fork entry already re-evaluates
   trivially (fresh saturator per fork,
   [`commitment.py`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/commitment.py)).
6. **Measurement gates** — `Saturator.naf_dropped` → 0 by construction;
   the `naf_deps` derived-NAF warning retires; the determinism/parity
   pins (`tests/inference/lattice/test_shuffle_invariance.py`,
   `tests/inference/test_saturator_fork_parity.py`) and the acceptance
   fixtures byte-identical before/after.

## Implemented (2026-08-17)

**Compile split.** `compile.split_naf` lifts every top-level `(absent …)`
out of a plan's steps; what remains is a purely positive Scan/Join/Guard
plan. Guards land in `JoinPlan.naf_guards`, aligned per disjunct, and
`JoinPlan.disjuncts()` pairs each disjunct's steps with its own guards.
Each lifted `NafGuard` carries three things beyond the guard itself:

- `scope` — the vars bound by the positive premises that *preceded* it.
  Boundary evaluation projects the completed bindings back down to it, so
  lifting is exactly as strong as evaluating in place. This is the subtlety
  the stage did not name: `(and (absent (P ?x)) (Q ?x))` asks "no P at
  all?" while `(and (Q ?x) (absent (P ?x)))` asks "no P for *this* x?", and
  moving a guard to the end without projection would silently convert the
  first into the second.
- `watched` — every relation the query reads. The boundary's invalidation
  key: if none grew, the verdict cannot have moved and the query is not
  re-run. (Same relation set the retired `_absent_relations` computed,
  repurposed from "force a full re-match" to "decide whether to re-ask".)
- `monotone` — whether the query is anti-monotone (no nested absent).

**Boundary.** New `inference/world.py`. `World(kb, commitment)` exposes
`holds` / `absent` / `admits` / `first_failing` / `negative_premises`; it is
a read-only view taken at a quiescence point, not a snapshot.

**Two-phase loop.** `Saturator.step()` alternates a purely positive closure
to quiescence with a boundary round; `Engine.step()` (the queue-less
pre-saturator loop) does the same directly. Observables: `naf_dropped`
(structurally 0), `naf_rounds`, `naf_admitted`, `naf_retired`.

**Faster, not slower.** Exhaustive `zebra2` solve **10.4 s → 8.5 s**;
acceptance gate **130 s → 91 s**. Deleting the absent-flip full-match split
more than pays for the boundary evaluations — but only after two
optimisations that the naive design needed: retire a candidate whose
anti-monotone guard has failed (it can never pass again), and skip
re-judging when no watched relation grew. Without them the same design ran
**80 % slower**, at 233 k guard queries per solve.

## Deviations from the § Shape

Three, all deliberate:

- **Admission is one candidate per boundary round, not the batch.** The
  shape says "evaluate every parked NAF-guarded binding against the stalled
  KB; admissions re-enter the inner loop". Taken literally that derives
  **both** `p` and `q` from `p ← absent q; q ← absent p` — both guards pass
  against the world where neither holds — and `{p, q}` is not a model of
  that program under any reading. Admitting one closes the window entirely:
  the queue is empty at quiescence, so the admitted candidate fires against
  exactly the world its guard was judged against. On a stratified program
  the two policies agree.
- **The `naf_deps` derived-NAF warning was re-grounded, not retired.** Its
  stated rationale ("soundness leans on the fire-time re-eval") is dead, so
  the *claim* retired — but its subject became the main remaining hazard:
  NAF over a derived relation is exactly the shape that can make a rule set
  non-stratified. Deleting the only diagnostic for the one thing that can
  still go wrong would have been a regression in observability.
- **`match._run_steps` keeps its `AbsentGuard` arm.** The shape says the
  arm drops. It cannot: a *nested* absent inside a guard's own sub-plan is
  what `forall` desugars to, and the boundary runs that sub-plan through
  the same driver. The requirement the shape was expressing — "closure
  plans are purely positive" — holds; the arm is now reachable only from
  inside a negative query, which is documented at the call site.

## Divergences introduced (enumerated, not absorbed)

The acceptance asked for these to be reviewed rather than silently
accepted. The stage guessed P2/P4 would move; **P1 and P2 moved, P4 did
not**, and the direction is the opposite of a regression:

- **P1 — `test_fire_then_arrive_keeps_both` → `test_guard_is_judged_against_the_positive_fixpoint`.**
  The old KB held *both* `p` and `q`: `q` was in the closure, yet
  `(absent (q ?x))` had licensed `p`, because the gate popped before `q`
  was derived. That anomaly is what the stage exists to remove; the result
  is now `{q}`.
- **P2 — `test_priority_swap_changes_outcome` →
  `test_priority_swap_does_not_change_outcome`.** The same program with the
  priorities swapped used to yield a *different* model. It no longer does.
  This is the headline: priority-band discipline is demoted from
  load-bearing to advisory.
- **P4 — mutual NAF: unchanged** (`{p}`), by the one-per-round admission
  above. The *mechanism* changed — the loser is never admitted rather than
  dropped by a re-check — so `naf_dropped == 0` replaced `== 1`.
- **New, and stated plainly:** on a genuinely unstratifiable program the
  engine reports one model where several exist, and does not say so. That
  was true before too, but priority no longer even explains *which* one; it
  is boundary-admission order. A static stratification checker is the
  proper remedy and remains future work (P1.21 § Out of scope).

## Post-landing defects (found by adversarial probing, fixed 2026-08-17)

The stage shipped, then was attacked. Two defects it had introduced:

- **D-S8-1 — `scope` dropped the rule's activator parameters. Soundness.**
  `split_naf` started `bound = set()` and collected only vars bound by
  positive Scan/Join steps, so a rule *parameter* — bound from the
  activator, in scope for the whole match — was projected away before the
  boundary ran the guard. Invisible for relation slots (`_slot` substitutes
  a bound parameter to a literal at compile time), fatal for a predicate
  `Guard`, which is compiled from the **raw IR nodes**: an
  `(eq ?y ?PARAM)` under an `(absent …)` resolved `?PARAM` to `None`, the
  negative query found nothing, and the guard **passed when it had to
  fail**. Fixed by seeding `split_naf` from `bindings_seed`.

  Worth naming the lesson: the projection is what makes lifting a guard
  safe, and getting the projected set *too small* is silently unsound in
  exactly the direction that fires extra rules. The scope computation
  deserved the adversarial pass it got.
- **D-S8-2 — an alternative justification lost its negative premises.**
  `_apply` handed its guards to `fire()` on the productive path but called
  `_record_alternative` without them, so a re-derivation recorded under
  S1.21.7 carried `absent_premises=()` no matter how many guards admitted
  it. Provenance is per *derivation*; each derivation carries its own
  negative dependencies. Fixed.

`inference/explain.py` was fuzzed over randomised AND/OR graphs (cyclic and
acyclic, varying fan-out) and came back clean — no unsoundness, no
non-termination, no non-determinism.
