## The claim

`absent` is what splits Ein into *positive monotone reasoning* + *non-monotone
observations of absence*, and its consequences are already scattered through
the implementation as hard-won local decisions:

- fork-derived facts can't be back-propagated to root
  ([`solver.py:376-379`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/monotonic/solver.py),
  S1.21.2);
- positive-premise provenance is insufficient for dependence
  (S1.21.2's counterexample);
- deletion-based MUS minimisation is unsound
  ([`min_core.py:17-25`](../../../ein.py/src/ein/inference/min_core.py));
- saturation needs fire-time re-evaluation of `AbsentGuard`;
- semi-naive triggering must watch relation changes *inside* `AbsentGuard`.

The review: **formalise the semantics in its own document before extending
the language.** Candidate meanings of `KB ⊨ absent(P)`:

1. **Closed-world:** `P ∉ closure(KB) ⇒ absent(P)`.
2. **Stratified NAF:** requires a dependency stratification (none exists).
3. **Branch-relative epistemic:** `KB_C ⊭ P` where `C` is the commitment
   environment — `absent(P)` is a *query over the current saturated
   world/environment*, not a ground logical atom.

The review judges (3) closest to Ein's actual behaviour; that distinction
(query vs atom) is the important one.
