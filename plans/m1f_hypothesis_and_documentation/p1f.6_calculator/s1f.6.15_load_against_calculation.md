## The instruction

The user, 2026-11-11, after asking what the `.einb` cache does for a library:

> add examples with long numbers and many calculations so we can compare load time with calculation time

## What it is for

A small arithmetic program spends its run reading the tables, not calculating: one sum is about 0.1 ms of
saturation behind about 14 ms of loading `std.digits` and `std.arith`. The examples make the other end
visible, where the program's own requests are the load, and they give the phase three numbers per program,
**parse**, **load** and **saturate**, as `ein saturate` prints them.

**A first measurement, before any example was written** (the coordinator's, addition only, the stdlib at
`277cfc44`, best of three, `ein saturate`, 2026-11-11; random operands; re-taken by this stage with its
script):

| requests | digits each | parse ms | load ms | saturate ms | firings | program text |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 6 | 1.7 | 14.2 | 0.1 | 22 | under 1 KB |
| 1 | 100 | 2.1 | 14.1 | 0.3 | 210 | 1 KB |
| 1 | 900 | 11.5 | 14.4 | 2.3 | 1 810 | 11 KB |
| 100 | 9 | 8.3 | 14.3 | 3.2 | 2 701 | 12 KB |
| 1 000 | 9 | 71.9 | 16.6 | 31.3 | 27 001 | 115 KB |
| 10 000 | 9 | 718.2 | 39.3 | 452.1 | 270 001 | 1 152 KB |
| 1 000 | 100 | 730.8 | 39.9 | 324.5 | 209 001 | 1 182 KB |
| 100 | 900 | 650.7 | 36.3 | 287.2 | 180 901 | 1 056 KB |

Two things it shows already, each to be confirmed:
- **Up to about a hundred requests the tables are the cost**, a constant 14 ms, which a `.einb` of the whole
  program removes and nothing removes for a library alone ([`findings.md`](findings.md)).
- **Past that, parsing the program's own numbers costs more than calculating with them**: a megabyte of
  nested numbers takes about 0.7 s to parse and 0.3 to 0.45 s to add. The parser's cost is known
  ([Q-M1f.8](../open_questions.md#q-m1f8--the-parsers-remaining-cost-within-grmtools-or-past-it)); long
  nested numbers are its worst input so far.

## Tasks

1. **The examples**, under `examples/arith/`, each with an `:expect` on a few of its answers and a header
   that says what it measures:
   - **long numbers:** one request per operation on operands of some hundreds of digits (the nesting limit
     of 1 000 bounds a written number), e.g. a 300-digit product and a 600-digit by 300-digit division;
   - **many calculations:** a batch of some thousands of requests per operation on school-sized operands;
   - **a long chain,** where the numbers are derived and not written, so the parse is small and the
     calculation long: a factorial or a power by the program's own rules, its answer hundreds of digits.
   Generated by a script under `utils/` with `--check`, as the tables are, with a fixed seed.
2. **The corpus cost.** `slow = true` is a measured claim (an entry whose runs cost 1 s or more together).
   The examples are sized to stay under it where they can, and the stage says which are slow and why. A
   size that would make the gate slower by more than the examples are worth goes to a script, not the
   corpus.
3. **The measurement script,** `utils/arith_cost.py`, run through `utils/bench_env.sh`: parse, load,
   saturate, firings, facts and resident memory, for each example and for a sweep of requests against
   digits per operation; the same for the program saved as a `.einb`; and `ein solve` beside `ein saturate`,
   since the read-out does more than the fixpoint.
4. **The page:** the table in `stdlib/README.md`'s section for the module, with the command that re-takes
   it, and what it says about where the time goes: the tables, the parse, the calculation.
5. **What it finds** goes to [`findings.md`](findings.md): the library-load cost with no cache of its own,
   and the parser on long nested numbers, each with its number.

**The plan's sizes did not fit the corpus, and the reason is a finding**
([Q-M1f.166](../open_questions.md#q-m1f166--the-sizes-of-the-generated-arithmetic-examples), defaulted). The plan
asked for operands of some hundreds of digits, some thousands of requests and an answer of hundreds of digits. The
first probe of a 100-digit product: 81 ms under `ein saturate` and 4.0 s under `ein solve`; a quotient of 200 digits
by 100, 171 ms and 20.7 s. `ein solve` writes every fact of the state as text, and each step of a circuit holds its
whole state, so the text is the facts times the digits: E-24 of [`findings.md`](findings.md). Every corpus program is
solved by `corpus_cli`, dumped and reloaded by `answer_round_trip` and solved, dumped and saturated by
`arith_search_free`, per commit, and a state file reloads at 0.3 s a megabyte. So the shipped sizes are the ones every
per-commit surface affords, and the plan's sizes are the script's sweep, and the generator's arguments:

| file | `ein saturate` | `ein solve` | the state file | its reload |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `long_numbers.ein` | 34 ms | 73 ms | 4.0 MB | 1.2 s |
| `many_calculations.ein` | 47 ms | 92 ms | 4.9 MB | 1.3 s |

(`build/s1f.6.15/size.sh`, the release binary, 2026-11-11.) At the plan's sizes, `--digits 300 --short 60 --requests
2000 --factorial 120`, the three would be slow entries whose dumps run to gigabytes.

## T4 record, 2026-11-11: the page, and where the time goes

[`stdlib/README.md` § What a program pays](../../../stdlib/README.md#what-a-program-pays-the-tables-the-parse-the-calculation)
is the script's output of 2026-11-11 and what it says. The coordinator's first measurement, re-taken:

| | the first measurement, addition alone | re-taken, the whole theory |
|---|---|---|
| the load a program pays | 14.2 ms | 20.6 ms: 14.3 of it `std.digits`, the rest the 89 rules and 67 relations that are now the theory |
| 1 sum | parse 1.7, saturate 0.1 ms | parse 1.1, saturate 0.3 ms, 0.02 of it the sum |
| 1 sum of 900 digits | parse 11.5, saturate 2.3 ms | parse 7.9, saturate 2.7 ms |
| 10 000 nine-digit sums | parse 718, saturate 452 ms | parse 748, saturate 502 ms |

Both of its readings hold. **Up to about a hundred requests the tables are the cost,** and **past a thousand sums the
parse costs more than the calculation**; a product or a quotient reverses the second, 294 ms of parse against 4.3 s
of calculation at 4 000. Three things it did not show:

- **A `.einb` buys nothing back** (E-25): a saved arithmetic program opens in 21.9 ms where its text parses and loads
  in 21.8. E-15's 7.0 ms against 19.1 was taken while the theory read two tables, and is reproduced on that stdlib.
- **`ein solve` costs the state's text** (E-24), 30 times `ein saturate` on a 900-digit sum and 91 times on a
  quotient of 200 digits by 100.
- **Memory ends a batch before time does** (E-26): a kilobyte a stored fact, 2.1 GB for 4 000 products.
