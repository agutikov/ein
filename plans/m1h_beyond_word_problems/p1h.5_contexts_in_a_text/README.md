## Why M1h has a phase of it

A text is full of contexts: what a character says, believes, wants, is told, pretends, dreams; a lie, a mistake,
a fairy tale inside a story, a question asked of a reader about what *someone* thinks. P1h.10's fifty questions
about a school text will include *"what did X think happened"* beside *"what happened"*, and the second must not
follow from the first. So where M1g's P1g.12 decides what a context is in the engine, this phase asks what a
text needs of it.

## Questions it starts from

1. **Which attitudes a school text uses**: says, believes, knows, wants, pretends, is told, dreams, imagines; which
   imply truth (*knows*) and which do not (*believes*, *says*).
2. **Reported speech and lies**: a character says something false; the reader knows it; another character
   believes it.
3. **The reader's context**: what the reader is told, against what the story makes true (an unreliable narrator).
4. **Change over time**: a belief corrected later in the text, with [P1h.1](../p1h.1_temporal_and_causal_theories/README.md)'s
   timeline.
5. **Questions about contexts**: *"Why did X think Y?"* answered by the rules that put Y into X's context.
