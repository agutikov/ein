# S1h.10.2 — Fifty questions in five groups by complexity, each with its expected answer in words

**Phase:** [P1h.10](README.md) of [M1h](../README.md)
**Status:** planned 2026-11-13. Not started. **It stops for the user,** before any question is formalized:
the groups and the questions are the user's to accept, change or replace.
**Depends on:** the text the user chose at [S1h.10.1](s1h.10.1_the_text.md)'s stop.

## What it rests on

The instruction, 2026-11-12:

> Write 50 questions grouped into 5 groups by complexity from elementary (who did what) to very complex (idk
> maybe: feelings, motivation explanation, call of duty, moral dilemmas, what auther wanted to say, etc.)

The milestone README: *The upper groups are the user's guesses, marked as such in the instruction ("idk
maybe") … The phase proposes the five groups and the questions and stops for the user before formalizing
any.*

So one group is given, the first: *"who did what"*. The other four are not: the instruction offers five
examples of what *very complex* may be and marks them a guess.

## What it produces

`fifty_questions.md` in this directory: the text with its sentences numbered (in full if S1h.10.1 established
that it may be reproduced; by number against the named edition if not), the five groups each with its
criterion, and the fifty questions as one table. No program, and nothing outside `plans/`.

## Tasks

1. **T1h.10.2.1 — the text, numbered.** Every sentence a number, by the counting rule of S1h.10.1. The
   numbers are what every later stage cites: a fact's `:source`, a question's basis, a slice.
2. **T1h.10.2.2 — the five groups, proposed.** Each group with a **criterion a third person could apply** to
   sort a new question into it, stated as what answering needs, since that is what the theories will be
   measured on. The first group is the instruction's, *who did what*; its criterion is proposed with the
   others (a candidate: the answer is a fact one sentence states). For the other four the stage proposes
   criteria from the text it has in hand and from the user's five examples, and says of each example where
   it fell: in a group, across two, or nowhere in this text. The stage does not start from a scheme of its
   own: a group exists because the text has questions for it.
3. **T1h.10.2.3 — the fifty questions.** For each: the question as a pupil would be asked it; its group;
   **its expected answer, in words;** the sentences the answer rests on, by number; and **what the answer
   needs that no sentence states**, in a phrase (*that a person who cries is sad*; *that a promise binds*),
   or the word *nothing*. The last column is the phase's subject: the milestone README has *a question about
   a feeling or a motive needs facts no sentence states, and the theory that supplies them is the measured
   thing.*
4. **T1h.10.2.4 — the answers that are opinions, marked.** A question whose expected answer rests on no
   sentence and on no knowledge a reader would call common (most of *what the author wanted to say*) is
   marked, with whose answer was written down: a school's usual one, with its source, or the stage's. The
   user sees the mark before reading the list.
5. **T1h.10.2.5 — the checks before the stop.** Every question has one group and every group its criterion;
   no two questions ask the same thing in other words; each expected answer is checked against the numbered
   sentences by a second reading; the count a group is on the page.
6. **T1h.10.2.6 — the questions for the user,** on the page:
   [Q-M1h.19](../open_questions.md#q-m1h19--the-five-groups-and-the-fifty-questions): the five groups; the
   fifty questions; whether the groups are of ten each, which the instruction does not say; and whether a
   few questions of each group are held back from whoever writes that group's theory.
7. **T1h.10.2.7 — the stop, and the re-plan.** The user reads the page. What is accepted, changed and
   struck is recorded on it in the user's words. Then
   [S1h.10.3](s1h.10.3_the_text_and_the_questions_formalized.md),
   [S1h.10.4](s1h.10.4_five_theories_or_one_theory_and_five_ontologies.md) and
   [S1h.10.5](s1h.10.5_how_the_theories_grow.md) are given their tasks, to the text, the questions and the
   rulings.

## Acceptance

- The text is numbered, and every question cites the sentences it rests on by number.
- Each of the five groups has a criterion on the page, and each of the user's five examples is placed or
  said to be absent.
- There are fifty questions, each with a group, an expected answer in words and the column *what no sentence
  states*.
- Every answer that is an opinion is marked.
- The user has accepted the groups and the questions, or has changed them; the page records it.
- Nothing outside `plans/` moved.

## What it must not do

- **Not formalize a question,** and not design a relation: *before formalizing any*.
- **Not write a question to suit a theory.** No theory exists yet, and the questions are a pupil's.
- **Not drop one of the user's examples in silence.** If the text has no moral dilemma, the page says so.
- **Not present the stage's own answer to an opinion question as the text's.**

## The standing rules

Questions go to [`open_questions.md`](../open_questions.md) under the next free id; findings to this phase's
`findings.md`. A ruling is recorded as the user gave it. The link check is run before the commit, which
changes pages only.
