## Context

**The sub-question, as filed:**

> **(d) What is reported when R has holes?** Both lines no longer describe R.
> Is the answer then the list of R's maximal convex pieces, or a qualifier, or
> a refusal (Q-M1e2.2's *refuse what cannot be stratified*)? The follow-up's
> recommendation: the outer lines stay the answer, and the read-out adds the
> minimal holes, each with the `absent` guard of its core and its repair
> edges. A hole is never counted as a solution.

**What the general ruling changed.** On 2026-10-16 the user ruled that R is,
in general, its node set: *"In general non-monotonic case R is completely
defined only by full set of nodes. Is that correct? Let's go from it, and
simplify the results if our case allows or if it is reqested explicitely."*
So *what the answer is* when R has holes is settled: it is R. What (d) still
asks is how the read-out describes R's shape around its holes, which lines it
prints and under what name, what it says about each hole, and whether any
program is refused instead.

**What a hole is a property of.** A hole is a fact about the **status**, the
second arrow of `L ──state──▶ KB ──(dead, owes)──▶ {0,1}²`
([README § The words](README.md#the-words)). `dead` follows the state, so a
dead hole needs a state that is not monotone, which needs an `absent`. `owes`
does not: a trigger, or an activator that compiles an obligation, makes an
owing hole over a monotone, path-independent state. **A hole needs no path
dependence**
([`monotonicity.md` § 7.5](../../../docs/kernel/inference/monotonicity.md#75-the-status-dead-follows-the-state-owes-does-not)).

**What the read-out prints today**
([`read_out.md` § 6.3](../p1e2.1a_the_solution_region/read_out.md#63-holes-the-stub-never-silent)).
The `lattice` row carries the hole count and up to four minimal holes, each
with why it is out. The `(region …)` listing is the cube's R, so a hole needs
no form of its own. On `examples/lattice/04_obligation_trigger_hole.ein`,
`ein solve` (2026-11-02):

```
  maximal line      1 state — ⊤, the trivial line: no restriction bites
  lattice           not monotone: 1 site (the static check) — 1 hole; assumes a monotone lattice for what a node is (P1e2.1b)
    minimal hole    {(seats Ann C1)} — owes 1
  route             2, the cube — no licence: the static check flags the program
```

The phrase *assumes a monotone lattice for what a node is* is S1e2.1a.1's
stub, and its words are wrong twice over: the lattice is never monotone, and
what is assumed is that a node is built by fork from root. Rewording it is
[S1e2.1b.5](s1e2.1b.5_the_detector.md)'s.

**The attribution gap.** The site census names a site for every minimal hole
and every violation's witness on the programs it was written on: a dead node by
its `absent` premises, an owing node by the trigger sites of what it owes. On
`lattice/08` and `lattice/09` it names none. Its re-take at `6743dd17` prints

([`monotonicity.md` § 5.2](../../../docs/kernel/inference/monotonicity.md#52-on-the-cube)).
`lattice/08` owes because an `absent` took its witness away, and `lattice/09`
owes through an obligation a node compiled. The census's rule covers neither.

**What *forbid* leaves.** On 2026-11-02 the user ruled that `(absent P)` ⟹
`(false)` over a relation a commitment can still extend is refused
([S1e2.4.1 § 5](../p1e2.4_what_the_switch_left/s1e2.4.1_refutation_under_absent.md#5-the-ruling)).
S1e2.4.1's rewrites keep R and turn dead holes into **owing** holes
(`three_components_owe`, `trigger_hole_owe`, `naf_open_hrules`), and
`via_hole.ein` keeps a **dead** hole through a derived relation. So once
S1e2.2.5's refusal ships, a program that loads has owing holes, and dead holes
only through a derived relation or a compiled refutation, unless its F2 and
F3 take them. Until then, and P1e2.2 runs last, both kinds load.

## The options, for F21's D4

**Not asked in this stage.** The stage drafts each option on real programs and
writes down what each would change. The decision is F21's.

| option | what the read-out says | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) R, its lines, and its holes named** | the `(region …)` listing is R, as today. The two lines are printed as R's minimal and maximal elements, named as such and not as bounds. Every minimal hole is printed with its cause (the core and its `absent` guard, or the owed instances and their trigger) and its repair edges. A hole is never counted. This is the 2026-10-15 follow-up's recommendation, less what the general ruling made moot | one row per minimal hole, under the 100-line cap, the rest in `--dump-answer` |
| **(b) R as convex pieces** | R split at its holes into its maximal convex parts, each with its own two lines | a new notion on the page and in the read-out. Its size against (a) is measured |
| **(c) a qualifier** | today's stub, reworded: the lines are printed, with one line saying they do not describe R | no new rows, and the reader is told less than the cube knows |
| **(d) a refusal** | a program whose R can have a hole is a load error | it would refuse `06_friends_tables` and every conditional obligation written the way the user wrote it. That is F21's D2 (D) refusal extended from `absent` to triggers |

**Sub-questions, whichever option:**

- **(d1) Two kinds, one word or two.** A dead hole is a refutation that a
  commitment repairs. An owing hole is a debt that a commitment creates. Today
  both are *minimal hole* and differ only in their *why*.
- **(d2) A claim form.** Does `(solve :expect (region …))` state holes? The
  listing is R, so a claim of R already fixes its holes, and (a) needs no new
  form. If an option adds one, it is shown on real corpus programs with
  answers computed before anything is implemented (the user's rule of
  2026-10-16).
- **(d3) The stub's words.** What replaces *assumes a monotone lattice for
  what a node is*. S1e2.1b.5 rewords it as a warning in the meantime; (d3) is
  what the read-out says once D2 is decided.
- **(d4) `lattice/04`'s row.** It follows from D4 and D2 and is not asked
  separately: a row is filed if, under them, today's read-out on it differs
  from the right answer computed under them. Otherwise the README's inherited
  row is closed with that reason.

**A recommendation, mine, recorded for F21 and not a ruling:** (a), with dead
and owing holes named apart, and no new claim form. The general ruling already
made R the answer, and (a) is what ships with the stub's words removed and the
repair edges added. (b) and (c) each print less than the cube computes, and
(d) refuses the case S1e2.1b.6 calls the common one.
