## The case

[`solution_semantics.md` § 1.2](../../../docs/kernel/inference/solution_semantics.md)
says *a **name** is a constant the program mentions, in a fact or in a rule*.
After S1e2.2.4 the blind enumerator's objects are `kb.names()`, the arguments
of the KB's top-level facts, and `invariant::rule_constants`, the constants a
rule's `:assert` names. Two kinds of constant the program writes are in
neither, and never become a candidate:

1. **only in a rule's `:match`.** `match-only.ein` is W4's program with
   `spawn` replaced by `reach-z: :match (q ?a Z) → (good ?a)`. H is 2, both
   dead, R is {∅}. With `(seen Z)` added (the `-declared` control) H is 6,
   R is four states, the maximal `{(q A Z), (q B Z)}`. No corpus program has
   such a constant (the probe's `mentioned_names_probe.rs`, 213 programs).
2. **only inside a nested fact.** `Kb::names` does not index a nested fact's
   arguments (Q40), and the seed does not reach them. `negative-only.ein` is
   W4's program without `spawn`, plus `(not (q Z A))`: H 2, R {∅}; declared,
   H 5, R four states. **Seven corpus programs' R moves** when each such name
   is declared (`build/s1e2.2.4-probe/r3/corpus-twins/compare.txt`):

| program | today | with the names declared |
|---|---|---|
| `examples/saturation/hypothesis-contradiction/coloc-disproved.ein` | k_s 1, H empty | k_s 2, H 1 |
| `…/next-to-disproved.ein` | k_s 1 | k_s 2 |
| `…/right-of-disproved.ein` | k_s 1 | k_s 2 |
| `corpus/fuzz_findings/binding-key-layout.ein` (W7) | k_s 1 | k_s 2 |
| `corpus/fuzz_findings/binding-key-layout-swapped.ein` (W7) | k_s 1 | k_s 2 |
| `examples/syntax/equality.ein` | H 6, k_s 64 | H 12, k_s 4 096 |
| `examples/syntax/arg-kinds.ein` | H 19, k_s ≥ 169 767 (a lower bound) | H 41, k_s ≥ 112 793 (a lower bound) |

**The user ruled (a) on 2026-11-04**, *§ 1.2 literally*: every constant
written in a fact at any depth, or anywhere in a rule (`:match` and `:assert`),
is a name of the program, and so a candidate object. **A constant written
only in a `(query …)` is not**: R is the program's, and a query asks about
it. Two corpus programs carry such a constant (`syntax/query-full.ein`'s
`Water`, `ein-bugs/query-goal-free-head-unsat.ein`'s `b`), and their R stays as
it is.

This is W4's class, a name of the program missing from the candidates, which
ruling (h) of Q-M1e2.7 makes an engine bug. It is a new case, not a W4 row,
because W4's seed is the rule's `:assert` and its rows are fixed.

## The method

1. **Re-take and list first.** At the stage's HEAD, re-take the probe's
   witnesses and the seven programs, then run the full corpus sweep (every
   declared run, as S1e2.2.5 did) and keep it. **Measure the stdlib too**:
   a stdlib rule's `:match` constants are a rule's constants, and an
   imported rule's `:match` could name a constant in every program that
   imports it. Compute the widened set for every corpus program and list
   every program whose H moves. If any program beyond the seven moves, list
   it with its R before and after. Then write down every golden, answer,
   exit cell and register row the fix will move. A move not on that list is a
   finding, reported, not re-blessed.
2. **Bank the witnesses** under `examples/ein-bugs/wrong-answers/`:
   `match-only.ein` and `negative-only.ein`, each with its `-declared`
   control, each with a header saying what it shows and a `(solve … :expect
   …)` that states the right R once fixed (until then, the register row holds
   the wrong answer). Corpus entries for all four.
3. **File W15** in `corpus/wrong_answers.toml`, `open`, one row per witness:
   the two banked programs, and each of the seven corpus programs whose R is
   in reach. For `arg-kinds.ein`, where R is a lower bound both ways, the row
   states H (19 → 41) if the schema can say it; if not, it is listed as a
   moved golden with no row, and the stage file says so. Each row's `right`
   is its `-declared` twin's R, held by the oracle where it is in reach. This
   is the stage's first commit, with the list of moves, and nothing built.
4. **The fix.** One list of the program's names, computed from the AST: every
   constant in a fact at any depth, and every constant in a rule's `:match`
   and `:assert` (rules, hrules and obligations), in argument position. It
   replaces `invariant::rule_constants` as what `hypgen::candidate_objects`
   unions into `kb.names()`, and `Universe::of` counts the same list. Query
   forms are not read. The candidate list stays deduplicated and in the order
   `candidate_objects` already defines. A unit test in `hypgen` holds a
   `:match` constant and a nested-fact constant in the list, and a query
   constant out of it.
5. **W7's two rows.** If their `now`/`right` read R (not `ein saturate`),
   their `right` is restated to the R under the ruling in the same commit as
   the fix, and they stay `open` for W7's lost derivation. If they read
   `saturate`, they do not move and the stage says so.
6. **The pages.** `solution_semantics.md` § 1.2 says the ruling, with the
   date and the query exclusion; § 1.4 and § 6.6 if they restate the seed;
   `defined_behaviour.md` § 3.3; `inference/README.md`'s M1 invariant;
   `invariant.rs` and `hypgen.rs` docs; Q-M1e.21 and Q-M1e2.9; every header
   of the seven programs that states its R or its candidate count.

## Acceptance

- Every constant a fact names at any depth, or a rule's `:match` or `:assert`
  names, is a candidate object; a constant only a query names is not. Held by
  a unit test and by the two banked witnesses answering their controls' R.
- Every W15 row `fixed`; the seven programs' R equal to their twins' and to
  the oracle's.
- Nothing moves that step 1 did not list.
- No mode disagrees with the oracle on any moved program.
