## The case

The core promise, as `glossary.md` and five other pages now state it: the
reported core is the **smallest frontier** over the recorded derivations.
`Kb::record_justification` keeps at most `MAX_ALT_JUSTIFICATIONS = 32`
alternatives per fact (`kb.rs:95`), retained by **premise count**, while
`explain.rs` minimises **frontier size** over what survived. The two metrics
disagree. Premise count is local and frontier size is transitive, so a smaller
frontier can be refused for having more premises.

```
$ ein solve examples/ein-bugs/alt-cap-core.ein            # `narrow` primary
  unsat core (3 facts)   (p1 Y01) (p2 Y01) (p3 Y01)
$ ein solve examples/ein-bugs/alt-cap-core-reordered.ein  # one :priority apart
  unsat core (2 facts)   (w1 X) (w2 X)
```

The right answer for both is the 2-fact core: at
`MAX_ALT_JUSTIFICATIONS = 1_000_000` the first file reports it too. Pinned as a
defect by `explain_semantics.rs::the_alternatives_cap_can_enlarge_the_reported_core`.
**Reach:** only `zebra2-bad` hits the cap in the corpus, and its core is
unchanged at 32 and 10⁶.
**Re-taken 2026-11-04** (T1e2.2.11.1): that was the walk's measurement. Under
R's routes ten programs reach the cap, always on `(false)` in a dead node,
`zebra2` and `zebra2-minus-15` among them, and an uncapped store moves no core
on any of them but `alt-cap-core.ein`'s.

## The three fixes (Q-M1e.15, priced 2026-08-29)

1. **Retain by a frontier estimate** (e.g. the sum of premises' recorded
   depths). The right metric, but retention then depends on a quantity that
   changes as the KB grows, so the list must be re-sorted rather than
   inserted into.
2. **Raise the cap.** Cheap: the worst list would be 1 049 long. It turns a
   wrong answer into a less likely one.
3. **Record the minimum separately.** Beside the capped list, keep the single
   arrival with the smallest premise closure seen so far. Bounded and exact for
   what the search asks, and it needs a closure size the recorder does not have
   today.

**Recommendation: (3)**, and the stage's first task is to find out what the
closure size costs to maintain. If it is not affordable, (1) with a
re-sort is the fallback. (2) alone does not meet the milestone's bar.
**Corrected 2026-11-04** (T1e2.2.11.1): (3) is not *exact for what the
search asks*, and neither is (1). A saving between two facts behind a full
list is the per-fact worst derivation of its fact, so every per-fact metric
drops it, and no list of fixed length is exact
([§ Two findings](#two-findings-and-why-the-stage-stops-here)). Put to the
user as Q-M1e2.11.

#### Two findings, and why the stage stops here

**1. (3) is not exact, and no bounded retention is.** Q-M1e.15 calls (3)
*exact for what the search asks*. It is not: the search asks for the smallest
frontier of the *target*, and a fact's best derivation for its own sake need
not be the one the target needs, because a saving can lie *between* two
facts. `plans/m1e2_correctness/probes/alt-cap-shared.ein` (since T1e2.2.11.2 [`examples/ein-bugs/wrong-answers/alt-cap-shared.ein`](../../../examples/ein-bugs/wrong-answers/alt-cap-shared.ein)):
`(x K)` has 33 one-premise derivations, each a frontier of one given, and a
34th, `x-join`, over `(a K)` and `(b K)`; `(z K)` has only `z-join`, over the
same two; `(false)` needs both. Over every derivation the core is `{(a K),
(b K)}`, 2; but `x-join` is the per-fact worst of `(x K)`'s 34, so premise
count (today), a frontier estimate ((1)) and the smallest closure ((3)) all
rank it last, the full list refuses it, and the core is `{(a K), (b K), (c
C01)}`, 3. Measured: `ein-before` 3, `ein-proto3` 3, `ein-cap1e6` 2. The
argument is general: a list of fixed length must drop one of any larger set
of pairwise-incomparable derivations, and a program can be written whose
target needs the dropped one. So only an unbounded store is exact, or one that
re-derives what it dropped when the search asks. The search's own budget has
the same shape past 64 incomparable environments, but its cut is reported
(`Explanation::exhausted`), and the cap's is not.

**2. `:record-alternative-justifications false` decides the core, today.**
[`alt-cap-below.ein`](../probes/alt-cap-below.ein) is `alt-cap-core.ein` with
two witnesses, so no list is full, and
[`alt-cap-below-noalts.ein`](../probes/alt-cap-below-noalts.ein) the same with
the flag off: at HEAD, `unsat core (w1 X) (w2 X)` against `(p1 Y01) (p2 Y01)
(p3 Y01)`. Off, `wide`'s re-derivation of `(false)` is dropped and the core is
`narrow`'s primary chain. No corpus program separates the two settings, which
is why `lever_invariance.rs` holds at HEAD; every fix of W10, (3), (1) or the
uncapped store, makes `alt-cap-core.ein` separate them, 2 against 3, and that
test fails. `configuration.md` § 2.2 calls the flag *explanation only*, and §
2.5 and the test hold the read-out uncut, its core rows among them. And
`examples/zebra.ein` sets the flag off on purpose, for cost: with its

unpinned, `build/s1e2.2.11/bench.py`, 2026-11-04), and reports the same
