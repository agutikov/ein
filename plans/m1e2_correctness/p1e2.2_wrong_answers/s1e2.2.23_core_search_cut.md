## The case

The witness is [`F2_core_cut.ein`](../inventory_2026-11-05/findings/F2_core_cut.ein), re-taken on the inventory's snapshot build at `cdcc3994` (2026-11-05):
- `(A k)` has 2⁸ = 256 environments of eight facts, each `pᵢ` from `xᵢ` or `yᵢ`. `(B k)` rests on `(y1 k) … (y8 k)` alone, and `(A k) (B k)` derives `(false)`.
- `ein solve` prints a ten-fact core, `(x1 k) (x2 k) (y1 k) … (y8 k)`, with no qualifier.
- The eight `y` facts alone derive `(false)` ([`F2_core_cut_control.ein`](../inventory_2026-11-05/findings/F2_core_cut_control.ein)).
- With five pairs instead of eight ([`F2_core_cut_5.ein`](../inventory_2026-11-05/findings/F2_core_cut_5.ein)), the search stays under its budget and finds the all-`y` core.
- Re-taken at `3db6187f`, after S1e2.2.21 (2026-11-05): `ein solve` still prints `(x1 k) (x2 k)` among the core's facts, and the five-pair control still prints the all-`y` core.

The mechanism at `cdcc3994`:
- The core search keeps 64 environments per label (`ExplanationBudget`).
- `region.rs:1122` and `:1268` call `explain::smallest_contradiction_frontier`, which discards `Explanation::exhausted` (`explain.rs:618–623`).
- So a core from a cut search reaches the read-out, `--json-summary` and `nogoods.json` with nothing to say it was cut.

[S1e2.2.11](s1e2.2.11_alt_cap_core.md) says the cut *"is reported (`Explanation::exhausted`)"*, and F22 assumes the same. Both hold only for a library caller. It is not F22's case, because no fact in the witness has more than 2 of the 32 alternatives the cap allows.

The promise this breaks is [Q-M1e2.11](../open_questions.md#q-m1e211--which-derivations-is-the-unsat-core-the-smallest-over-and-may-a-config-flag-change-it) (i)(b): the smallest frontier over every derivation the program records.

## The method

1. **Re-take and list first.** S1e2.2.21's support makes environments larger, so the cut may now bite where it did not.
   - Bank the witness and its two controls under `examples/ein-bugs/wrong-answers/`.
   - Re-take them, and measure on S1e2.2.21's HEAD how many cores the corpus cuts at the budget: every dead node of every cube the oracle dumps, every member of N, and root. Give the count per program, with the command and the date.
   - File **W23** `open`. Its `right` is the same core, qualified as cut.
   - Prototype the qualifier and run the full sweep and `cargo test` under it. Write every move into this file before any edit.
2. **The fix.** Every surface that prints a core says when its search was cut, and at what budget: the read-out's `unsat core` lines and dead rows, `--json-summary`'s core, `nogoods.json`, `--trace` and `ein oracle --dump`. An example qualifier is *"a frontier, not the smallest: the core search stopped at its budget of 64 environments"*, and a field beside the core in each JSON. Raising the budget is not the default (*minimum work per question*). If step 1 finds no corpus core cut, report the cost of an uncut search beside the default, and keep the default.
3. **Tests that fail at the stage's HEAD in both profiles:**
   - the witness carries the qualifier on every surface, and the five-pair control does not;
   - a corpus test that no core is printed unqualified from a cut search.
4. **The pages:**
   - `glossary.md`'s *Smallest contradiction frontier*: the budget, and what a cut core says;
   - `inference/answer_dump.md`'s `nogoods.json` row;
   - `read_out.md` where it shows a core;
   - S1e2.2.11's sentence, with a dated note;
   - F22: the exact core past the budget becomes its residual too.
