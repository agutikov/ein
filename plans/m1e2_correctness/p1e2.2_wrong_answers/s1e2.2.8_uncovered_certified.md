what is **owed**, and `knows` is owed by nothing. The blind rung does not
enumerate arity ≥ 3 (`hypgen.rs`, `fill_slot`'s doc). In both cases the search
exhausts *its candidate set* and certifies the answer as if it had exhausted
*the program*.

**Why it is contested.** Q-M1e.6's second clarification quantifies a
solution over `alive₀`, the rung's own L1 set. Relative to that, `k = 3` is
correct by definition. As a statement about the program, the reading a user
has, there are at least six models. The M1d record already called it *"a
claim stated without the qualifier that licenses it"*. So the defect may be the
**certification's wording** rather than the count.

## Readings, and the fix each implies

| reading | fix |
|---|---|
| **the certification is scoped, and must say so** | `exhausted` stays, and the read-out qualifies it whenever the rung left relations or arities uncovered: *exhausted over the hypotheses the program's obligations / hrules propose; `knows` was not hypothesised.* The `uncovered` count is already in the rung event. This is S1d.3.3's lower-bound qualifier, applied to scope rather than to depth |
| **the rung must cover what it certifies** | `exhausted = true` only when every relation the goal or a rule reads is either closed, owed, or hypothesised. Otherwise `false`, a lower bound. This is strictly more honest and moves verdicts. Measure how many corpus entries lose their certification |
| **the program is under-specified** | `knows` with no obligation and no hrule is an open-world relation, and the answer is right. `not-wrong`, plus a sentence in `solution_semantics.md` § 5 |

Recommendation: **the first**. It is the one that makes the printed sentence
true without moving a verdict, and it reuses a number the engine already
computes. The arity-3 case belongs to it as well. *Unenumerated* is a scope,
and the read-out should name it.
