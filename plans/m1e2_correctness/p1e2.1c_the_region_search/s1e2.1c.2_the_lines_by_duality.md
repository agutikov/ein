## Context

**The order is ruled.** The user, 2026-10-16, on
[`solve_flags.md`](../p1e2.1a_the_solution_region/solve_flags.md)'s budget
question: *"(A) now, with (B) as the first step of P1e2.1c"*. (A) was route 1
over commitments under a budget of 2¹⁹ facts. (B) is the **state route**, and
the phase README calls it this stage's first half.

**Why states.** [`walk_and_region.md` § 6](../p1e2.1a_the_solution_region/walk_and_region.md#6-what-this-means-for-question-3):
zebra has 270 454 alive commitments and 27 alive states. A route that visits
each alive state once costs at most *alive states × n* saturations. Its upper
bound against route 1's visits, from that page:

| program | route 1 visits | alive states × n, an upper bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| `zebra.ein` | 270 912 | 1 512 |
| `zebra2.ein` | 270 579 | 1 120 |
| `square-*` | 1 048 576 | 551 180 |
| `features/02` | 32 768 | 10 920 |
| `type-exclusivity/pets` | 11 351 | 170 085 |
| `syntax/arg-kinds` | 524 288 | 9 961 472 |

It wins where commitments collapse into few states, and loses where every
commitment is its own state. So the stage builds it as a route beside route 1,
not in place of it.

**What the state route needs, from L3**
([`solve_interface.md` § 3.1](../p1e2.1a_the_solution_region/solve_interface.md#31-the-licences)):
on a monotone program a commitment's state is its closure's, so k_c is counted
over the states as `f(T) = 2^|T| − Σ f(S)` over the alive states S ⊊ T, and a
state's minimal generators are the minimal transversals of its complements
against the states below it. `explore::Licence` has L0, L1, L2, L4 and L7
today, so L3 is new.

**The second half, duality.** [Q-M1e2.7](../open_questions.md#q-m1e27--the-answer-is-a-region-of-the-lattice):
without holes, the minimal line is E's alive members, and the maximal line is
the discharged complements of the minimal sets that hit every member of N. The
oracle checks it on every cube (`region::Topology::duality`), and it held on
every monotone one
([region census § 5](../p1e2.1a_the_solution_region/region_census.md#5-holes-upward-closure-the-duality)).
The prior art is S1e2.1b.6's: MARCO-style MUS/MSS enumeration (Liffiton et al.,
*Constraints* 2016), incremental monotone dualisation (Fredman & Khachiyan,
*J. Algorithms* 1996), and formal concept analysis for closed sets and their
generators (Ganter & Wille 1999).

**The everything-alive cubes** are duality's plainest case: N is empty, R is
the one sub-cube [∅ … ⊤], and k_c = 2ⁿ. k_s still needs the states, 27 559 on
`square-bwd/floors.ein`
([`solve_interface.md` § 3.4](../p1e2.1a_the_solution_region/solve_interface.md#34-what-it-costs)).

**The target column.** [`solve_interface.md` § 3.2](../p1e2.1a_the_solution_region/solve_interface.md#32-a-monotone-program-each-modes-route)'s
*at P1e2.1c* column names this stage for `--minimal` (*"duality, S1e2.1c.2:
output-sized"*) and for describe (*"the state route, k_c by L3, the
generators by minimal transversals"*). The boundary with
[S1e2.1c.4](s1e2.1c.4_the_flags_at_scale.md) is drawn here: this stage builds
the routes and makes **describe** use them. S1e2.1c.4 routes every flag
through them, or through L5.

## The question it puts to the user

**Does the default's budget change, now that describe has a route over
states?** The user ruled on 2026-10-16 that *one budget, in facts, for every
mode, default 2¹⁹* holds
([`solve_interface.md` § 7](../p1e2.1a_the_solution_region/solve_interface.md#7-decided-here-unless-the-user-objects)),
and that zebra's R is described by `--region-budget`, not by default.

| option | what a bare `ein solve examples/zebra.ein` does |
|---|---|
| **(a) keep 2¹⁹ facts** | whatever the cheapest route reaches within it. If the state route fits zebra within 2¹⁹, the default describes it with no change to the budget |
| **(b) raise the default** | describes the zebra family by default, at the cost S1e2.1c.1 and this stage measure, paid on every bare run past it |
| **(c) wait for S1e2.1c.4** | decide once every mode's cost is measured |

**No recommendation before the measurement.** The cost is paid on every bare
`ein solve`, and (a) may already describe zebra.

## Tasks

### Task T1e2.1c.2.1 — The state route

**Done 2026-11-03.** [`ein_infer::states`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-infer/src/states.rs)
walks the alive states by size from root's, saturating each state's one-up
extensions, and nothing else. Three things keep it from saturating what it
knows: a dead extension `S ∪ {b}` prunes every later `S' ∪ {b}` with
`S ⊆ S'` (L1, sound because states are taken by size), an extension that is a
known state's closure is read and not saturated (L3), and a set two states
share is saturated once. It declines where a node contradicts L1–L3. Its
budget is route 1's, facts beyond root, checked before each layer.

- **On every monotone cube the oracle enumerates, 156 of them, it meets exactly
  the alive states, each with the cube's status**, and never saturates more
  than route 1: fewer on 13, the same on the rest
  (`the_state_route_is_the_oracle_on_every_monotone_program_it_reaches`).
- **Over the whole corpus, 181 monotone programs, it never saturates more or
  derives more facts than route 1**: fewer on 32, equal on 149. The `L1`
  pruning is what closes S1e2.1c.1's crossover: `pets`, `lattice/07` and
  `lattice/06_friends_tables_everyone_sits` cost exactly route 1's 11 351,
  177 and 345 saturations, where the unpruned count was 26 192, 1 073 and
  3 045.
- `zebra.ein`: **445 saturations, 106 829 facts, 0.28 s**, against route 1's
  270 912, 30 251 060 and 20.3 s. `zebra2`: 374, 42 705, 0.18 s.
- `zebra2-minus-15` **finishes**: 99 505 saturations, 9 481 774 facts, 4 709
  alive states, 32 of them in R, 12.2 s and a 66 MiB peak at `-j 16`.

Re-taken by `cargo run --release -p ein-infer --example state_reach --
--route1 --jobs 16 FILE…`, which prints `route1_reach`'s line for both routes.

### Task T1e2.1c.2.2 — k_c by L3, and the generators

**Done 2026-11-03.** [`states::describe`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-infer/src/states.rs)
reads `R` off the states, and L3 is `explore::Licence::Closure`, *"L3 a
commitment's state is its closure's"*.

- **A state's commitments, by L3.** The commitments that reach `S` are the
  subsets of `S` under no closed proper subset of `S`. Every closed set under
  `S` is alive and is met, and its lower covers are among the states one edge
  below it. So `f(S)` is the count of subsets of `S` that meet every
  complement `S \ T` against a state `T` one edge below. It is counted by
  branching ([`transversal::count_hitting`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-infer/src/transversal.rs)),
  and past 2¹⁶ branches by the phase's own form,
  `f(S) = 2^|S| − Σ f(T)` over the states `T ⊊ S`. No corpus program reaches
  the second form. `k_c` is the sum over `R`'s states.
- **A state's minimal generators** are the minimal transversals of the same
  complements, by MMCS. A state whose every complement is one candidate has
  one generator, those candidates, and `2^(|S| − |g|)` commitments, with no
  search: that is every state of `syntax/arg-kinds`.
- **The minimal line** is `{∅}` when root's state is in `R`. Otherwise it is
  the minimal sets among `R`'s generators, which are `E`'s alive members.
- **Held to the oracle** on the 156 monotone cubes: `k_s`, `k_c`, each
  state's commitments and minimal generators, and the minimal line, both as
  the oracle's minimal elements and as its `E`'s alive members.
- **Past the limit, against route 1's counts**: `zebra` gives k_c 261 982,
  k_s 1 and 63 generators; `branching/06` gives 18 622, 74 and 1 227; the
  `square-*` cubes give 1 048 576, 27 559 and 29 711, with 23 896 merged; and
  `arg-kinds` gives 2¹⁹ states and 2¹⁹ generators. `zebra2-minus-15`, which
  route 1 does not finish, gives **k_s = 32 and k_c = 241 462 963**, with
  12 068 minimal generators. Its commitments of R per size, enumerated from
  the generators (`state_reach --by-size 6`), are route 1's layer counts,
  0, 0, 221, 10 270, 107 963, 585 989 and 2 135 864, through the last layer
  route 1 finished.
- **What it costs**: 0.81 s on `arg-kinds` (2¹⁹ states, against 2.2 s of
  route), 0.05 s on each `square-*` cube, 0.08 s on `zebra2-minus-15`, and
  1 ms on `zebra`. Single-threaded, release, `wall_description` in
  `state_reach`'s line.

### Task T1e2.1c.2.3 — The lines by duality

**Done 2026-11-03.** `states::describe` reads the rest of `R` off the states:

- **The maximal line** is the states of `R` whose every one-up extension is
  dead (L4). Dropping *of `R`* gives the maximal alive states.
- **`N`, by duality**: the minimal transversals of the maximal alive states'
  complements, since a set is dead exactly when no maximal alive set holds
  it. The algorithm is MMCS (Murakami & Uno 2014). `zebra` has 9 maximal
  alive states and |N| = 458, and `zebra2-minus-15` has 467 and **27 804**,
  which is route 1's floor exactly. Route 1's N through size 6 was all of N.
- **`E`'s alive members** are the minimal line of T1e2.1c.2.2.
- **The components** are a union-find over `R`'s states, joined by their
  one-up edges inside `R`, each sized by its states' commitments. **The
  sub-cubes** are the pairs `[a … M]` with `a` on the minimal line, `M` on the
  maximal one and `a ⊆ M`. **The owing frontier** of an empty `R` is the
  maximal alive states, all owing.
- **The cube** is decided whenever ∅ and ⊤ are both in `R`, by L1 and L2,
  even on a run its budget stopped. That is the input to the everything-alive
  cubes' read-out in T1e2.1c.2.4.
- **Held to the oracle** on the 156 monotone cubes: the maximal line, the
  same line again as the discharged complements of `N`'s minimal
  transversals (`Topology::duality`), `N`, the components, the sub-cubes and
  the owing frontier.
- **The duality, the other way, past the limit** (`state_reach --duality`):
  the complements of `Tr(N)` are exactly the maximal alive states read off by
  L4 on `zebra`, `zebra2`, `zebra2-hints`, `branching/06` and
  `zebra2-minus-15`. On the last, that direction costs 16.5 s of MMCS alone,
  against 9.5 s for the whole state route, which is one reason the route reads
  the maximal line off the states and computes `N` from it, and not the
  reverse.

**Where N and E come from**, a decision taken without a ruling and written
beside the code (`states.rs`, § *Where N and E come from*): both are read
off the state route, and neither is enumerated separately. Describe needs
every state of `R` for `k_s`, `k_c` and the merged states, so a MARCO-style
enumeration of `N` and the maximal alive sets could only add saturations
beside it: at least one per member, 27 804 + 467 on `zebra2-minus-15`. An
enumeration without the states is where a *line-only* mode could win, which
is S1e2.1c.4's.

### Task T1e2.1c.2.4 — Describe takes the cheapest route

**Done 2026-11-03.** A bare `ein solve` on a monotone program takes the state
route ([`explore`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-infer/src/explore.rs)'s

test` and `ein solve` cannot disagree about which R a claim is checked
against. The read-out is `described::of_states`.

**The rule, a decision taken without a ruling** (`explore.rs`, § *Describe's
route*): it reads the static check's verdict and nothing else. Monotone, the
state route; flagged, the cube, or route 1 when root is dead (L0). No option

### Task T1e2.1c.2.6 — The budget question

The user's, on the measurements. **Open: the default stays 2¹⁹ facts until

**(a) Keep 2¹⁹ facts.** A bare `ein solve` describes the zebra family exactly,

**(b) Raise the default.** Each step buys one class of program and prices it
on every run that reaches it. 2²⁰ buys zebra's three modes, at about 6 s at

not 2²³ or 2²⁴ as a default. 2²⁰ is the one step that buys something a reader
asks first (is zebra unique?), and S1e2.1c.4 should make it free.*

**(c) Wait for S1e2.1c.4.** Keep 2¹⁹ and decide once every mode's route

cost about describe's 445 saturations, not 8 927. A budget chosen now for
them would be chosen again. *Mine: (a) and (c) are the same action today:
keep 2¹⁹. Prefer saying (c), so that S1e2.1c.4's page carries the question
