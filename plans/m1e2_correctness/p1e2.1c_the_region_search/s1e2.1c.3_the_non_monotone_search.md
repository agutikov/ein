column:

> no duality. **Split on the guards.** Fix the truth of every ground
> third-kind read a commitment can flip (g of them). Inside each cell the
> program is monotone and has lines, and R is the union over the cells whose

## The question it puts to the user

Asked when the phase starts, with P1e2.1b's rulings in.

> **Past the oracle's limit, how is a flagged program's R computed?**

| option | when it applies | what it gives |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) the guard split** | S1e2.1b.6's prototype agrees with the oracle on every flagged cube, and its cells fit the node S1e2.1b.2 ruled | R exact, at 2ᵍ × the monotone cost, g printed |
| **(b) the cube past the limit** | always. The user's ruling of 2026-10-15: *"we can safely limit n with 16 by default and warn user simply if asked for 16<=n<=20 and show intensive warning if user asks to go through lattice with n>=20"* | R exact when asked for by name, at 2ⁿ, which is what `--region-limit` does today |
| **(c) partial** | always | today's C3 answer |

The options are not exclusive: (a) where 2ᵍ fits the budget, and (c) where it
does not. **No recommendation until the prototype has run:** (a) is a route
only if its test holds on every cube, and S1e2.1b.6 is what says so.

## Acceptance

As far as it can be written before P1e2.1b rules:

- **Every flagged program the corpus holds** gets R exactly by the ruled
  route, or a read-out that says why not, naming the route and the bound (C3).
- **Held to the oracle.** On every flagged cube within the limit, the ruled
  search equals the oracle node for node: `every_mode_is_the_oracle`, extended
  to the new route. On the flagged programs at 17–19, at a raised limit where
  the cost allows, `06_friends_tables` among them, the same.
- **g measured and printed** per program wherever the split is the route, and
  the budget checked before the 2ᵍ cells start, as every route checks it
  before a step.
- **Learning only as S1e2.1b.4 rules**, each kind a named `explore::Licence`.
- **No lever changes R** (`lever_invariance.rs`), and no `-j` changes the
  read-out (C4).
- **The goldens it moves are named before they move**, `read_out_examples.md`'s
  X9 first if `06_friends_tables` becomes exact.
- **If nothing scales**, because the prototype disagrees with the oracle or no
  ruled node fits a split, the stage ships no route past the limit and writes
  why in `solve_interface.md` § 3.3. That is a result, and the eight programs
  keep their C3 answer.

## How it is measured

On the eight programs: g, the cells, the cells whose guess is consistent, and
the saturations, facts and wall, beside the cube's 2ⁿ. On the flagged cubes
within the limit: agreement with the oracle, node for node. The commands go on
the page with the date. The page is `alive_set_reach.md`'s flagged half,
which S1e2.1c.1 leaves open for the flagged programs whose node was not ruled
in time.

## Notes

- **The node decides whether the split fits.** The split guesses each
  flippable read and keeps a cell whose guess holds, which is a stable
  reading. Under the fork route, a fact root derived under an `absent` stays
  in every node whatever the guess, so a cell's guess can be contradicted by
  root itself. That is why the split waits on S1e2.1b.2, and why its
  prototype is re-checked over the ruled node.
- **Obligation triggers are guards too.** A trigger's growth adds a debt,
  which is the other non-monotone read
  ([P1e2.1b § What non-monotone means](../p1e2.1b_the_non_monotone_lattice/README.md#what-non-monotone-means-and-what-is-not)).
  Whether the split fixes trigger reads as it fixes `absent` reads, and so
  what g counts on `06_friends_tables`, is part of T1e2.1c.3.1's design.
