## Context

[Q-M1e.9](../../../docs/history/m1e_review_processing/open_questions.md#q-m1e9--is-dead-really-upward-closed-under-absent)
found that `dead` is not upward-closed under `absent`. Under the rule
`(and (p ?x) (absent (q ?x))) ⟹ (false)`, `{(p A)}` is dead and `{(p A), (q A)}`
is alive. M1e's
[S1e.2.3](../../../docs/history/m1e_review_processing/README.md#s1e23--q-m1e9s-containment-a-diagnostic-for-a-refutation-resting-on-an-absent)
shipped a warning (option B) and counted **9 rules over 7 corpus entries**.
Two languages would make the answer right, and the user chooses between them:

- **forbid**: a `(false)` or `(not …)` conclusion may not rest on an
  `(absent P)` whose relation is still extendable. The program is refused, and
  the error names the replacement.
- **respect**: the shape stays legal, and the engine never learns from a
  refutation whose derivation reads such an `absent`.

Since S1e2.1a.5's switch (2026-10-18) no CLI path runs the walk, which held all
three mechanisms that learned (the kill cache, the singleton writeback, the
width-1 no-good). W3's four register rows were flipped to `fixed` by route 2,
which learns from nothing. That flip assumed *respect*.

### 2.1 Under *forbid*

**The error.** S1e.2.3's warning is the error's text today, verbatim on
`naf-upward-closure.ein` with `--events`:

> rule 'bad': concludes (false) from (absent …) over q, which this program's
> hypothesis generator can still propose — so a state the search has not
> finished extending is refuted, and a commitment that would have discharged
> the guard is never reached (`dead` is not upward-closed under `absent`;
> Q-M1e.9). Write the constraint over a STORED negative, as std.algebra's
> `total` does — demand `(not (R ?a ?b))` for every candidate rather than
> concluding from its absence — or, if this is a requirement and not a
> refutation, state it as `(open ?R)` and let the verdict report what the
> state owes.

On `features/05` it reads the same for `typecheck-arg-0`, `typecheck-arg-1`
and `no-room-left`, each `[activator color-of is-a …]` and `over is-a`. Under
forbid it would exit as a load error does: 1 under `solve`, 2 under `test`.
The rewrites below show that the text has to name a **third** replacement,
because neither of its two fits a `std.elim` row: scope the relation
(`:no-hypothesis`).

**Each refused entry, and what replaces the rule.** These are measured with
`ein solve` on the probe, 2026-11-02.

**The W3 probe's three rewrites**, which are the ones the message names:

```lisp
;; today — R = {∅, {(q A)}, {(p A),(q A)}}, a dead hole at {(p A)}
(rule bad () :match (and (p ?x) (absent (q ?x))) :assert (false))

;; (1) a stored negative, total's form — naf_stored_negative.ein
;;     nothing stores (not (q A)), so it never fires: R = all 4 nodes, monotone
(rule bad () :match (and (p ?x) (not (q ?x))) :assert (false))

;; (2) a requirement — naf_open.ein
;;     `(open q)` over `(absent (q ?x))` is refused at load: "the obligation is
;;     ground". It needs a witness variable of its own, hence ?y and `eq`. With
;;     no hrule the rung is the obligations', which proposes only what a state
;;     owes, and root owes nothing: the universe is empty and R = {∅}
(rule bad-owed () :match (and (p ?x) (absent (and (q ?y) (eq ?y ?x))))
                  :assert (open q))

;; (3) = (2) + two hrules proposing (p ?x) and (q ?x) — naf_open_hrules.ein
;;     R = today's 3 states; {(p A)} owes instead of dying; still flagged
```

So the only rewrite that keeps the program's answer is (3), and it still has
the hole: an obligation's trigger is a non-monotone site too
([P1e2.1b § What non-monotone means](../p1e2.1b_non_monotone_programs/README.md#what-non-monotone-means-and-what-is-not)).

**Corrected 2026-11-02: a fourth rewrite keeps R and is monotone.** The
sentence above holds for rewrites over the **same candidates**, and there it
is a theorem: a monotone program's alive set is closed under subsets, and
W3's holds `{(p A), (q A)}` without `{(p A)}`. With more candidates it does
not hold. [`dual_rail.ein`](s1e2.4.1_probes/dual_rail.ein) makes q a decision, a
yes/no attribute that must be decided (`functional` + `total-owed`), and
refutes on the decided *no*:

```lisp
;; (4) q as a decision — dual_rail.ein
(functional q-val)
(total-owed q-val is-a)
(rule bad () :match (and (p ?x) (q-val ?x no)) :assert (false))
```

`ein solve` on it (2026-11-02): 3 states, *"monotone (the static check) — R
is convex: no hole"*, by *"route 1, the alive down-set — L1 dead is
upward-closed"*, and its own `(solve :expect (region …))` holds. R is W3's one
to one: `{(q-val A no)}` ↔ ∅, `{(q-val A yes)}` ↔ `{(q A)}`,
`{(p A), (q-val A yes)}` ↔ `{(p A), (q A)}`. A state that has not decided q
owes the decision; it is not dead. The rewrite is mechanical for a relation
the search guesses. A derived relation needs its negative derived
(completion), which is not measured here. It is the form zebra already uses:
every variable a functional, total attribute.

**The stdlib.** Two ways, and the census cannot choose between them:

- **No stdlib change.** `std.elim`'s three rules stay as written. The one
  corpus program that leaves `is-a` open, `features/05`, adds
  `(solve :no-hypothesis (is-a))`, the line its four `tests/stdlib/elim/`
  siblings already carry. The refusal names that line.
- **`std.elim` carries it.** The three rules are rewritten so that they cannot
  rest on an open membership relation. A stored-negative form,
  `(not (?isa ?a ?Dom))`, would make a typecheck that never fires, since
  nothing stores a negative membership. No form was found that keeps the
  check's meaning, and none is measured here.

**What else moves under forbid.** `refutation_under_absent.rs` changes from a
census to a refusal test, and the refused entries move to refusal fixtures
(`examples/broken/compile/` is where a check that runs after root's
saturation would put them) or are rewritten as above. `utils/region_synth.py`
can no longer write a dead hole: every hole it writes is owing. `(config
:warn-derived-naf)` loses its refutation half, which is a
[configuration reference](../../../docs/kernel/configuration.md) change, as
S1e2.2.5's T3 says.

### 2.2 Under *respect*

**What the engine must never learn from**: a node that is dead because its own
fixpoint derived `(false)` through a read that a commitment can flip. That read
is a `direct` row, or a `via` row behind it. It must never carry that
refutation to another node, as a skipped superset, a stored `(not h)`, or a
no-good clause.

**Which paths learn today.** The answer is checked per path, at `6e7dfc49`:

| path | learns from a dead node? | on a program with an exposed refutation? |
|---|---|---|
| route 1, [`ein_infer::downset`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-infer/src/downset.rs) and `explore`'s cuts: L1 (dead is upward-closed), L2, L4, the climb | yes: it never visits a superset of a dead node | **never**. Every `direct` row is a static-check site by construction, and every `via` row has one upstream, so each such program is flagged. A flagged program gets route 1 only when root is dead (`explore.rs:306`) |
| L0, *a fork only adds* (`explore.rs:306`, `claim.rs:151`) | root's death, given to every node | **one corpus entry**, `features/12`, whose root is dead by `no-room`. The fork node holds root's `(false)`, so the oracle says the same. Whether a node should inherit root's fixpoint at all is S1e2.1b.2's question (fork or scratch). Here no candidate could lift it anyway (§ 1) |
| route 2, `explore::flagged`: `cube_in_order` and `region::compute_prepared` | **no**. Every node is root's snapshot plus the commitment, saturated, and nothing is skipped or written back | — |
| `ein test`, `(solve :expect)` checks (`claim.rs`) | the two routes above, under the same switch | — |
| the walk's kill cache, singleton writeback, width-1 no-good and apriori filter | yes | **no CLI path** since S1e2.1a.5. The library is uncalled until P1e2.1c measures what of it the region search reuses. `solve_shape` still runs it inside tests for the shape digests |
| `summary::root_block` (`--json-summary`'s `root` block) | runs `hypgen::generate`, kill cache on, on a fork | a count in a report, not an answer: P1e2.3's S4 |
| `-L`, `-K`, `-y` | — | levers no route reads |

**So no shipped mode learns from such a refutation.** The test that holds
this is `ein-infer/tests/explore.rs::every_mode_is_the_oracle`. D4's matrix
gives one answer under every lever: `ein solve --maximal` on
`naf-upward-closure.ein` with no flag, `-K`, `-L` and `-L -K` prints
`(model (p A) (q A))` four times (2026-11-02).

**What respect binds.** No answer moves today. Respect is a requirement on
work not done yet. [P1e2.1c](../p1e2.1c_the_region_search/README.md)'s region
search may not bring back a learned clause, a kill-cache entry or a writeback
on a flagged program, or it must check each refutation's `Prov::absent`
([F18](../../followups/f18_world_aware_negatives.md)'s narrow form, D4's
option C). S1e2.1b.4 writes which. The cost is the one the corpus already
pays: a flagged program gets no licence, so past `--region-limit` 16 a question
that needs the whole cube is answered in part. `features/05` is that case
today, at 7 176 states out of 2⁵⁶.

## 4. The question

Explained from first principles, on the smallest programs that show each part
of it: [`s1e2.4.1_the_question_explained.md`](s1e2.4.1_the_question_explained.md)
(2026-11-02, at the user's request).

> **When a rule concludes `(false)` or `(not …)` and its `:match` reads
> `(absent P)` over a relation that a commitment can still extend, is the
> program refused before any search, with an error naming the replacement
> (*forbid*), or is it legal, with the engine never carrying that refutation
> past the node whose own fixpoint derived it (*respect*)?**

### What each answer leaves open

**If forbid:**

- **F1, what *extendable* means.** (a) The relation is eligible: S1e.2.3's

## 5. The ruling

**2026-11-02, the user: *forbid*.** The reading came first, after the
[explainer](s1e2.4.1_the_question_explained.md) and the fourth rewrite above:
*"That is exactly what I meant (absent q ?x) => false - this is incorrect form of obligation, isn't it?"*
Then the ruling: *"forbid, and bank dual_rail.ein in the docs"*.

What it says: over a relation a commitment can still extend, `(absent P)` ⟹
`(false)` or `(not …)` is an obligation written as a refutation. It judges a
state that has not yet decided P as if P were false, so it says *contradiction*
where the truth is *not yet*. A program that writes it is to be refused, and
the error names a form that says what was meant. Three were measured here:
`(open ?R)` with its hrules (§ 2.1 (3)), q as a decision ((4), above), and
the relation closed (`features/05`'s one line). Over a closed relation the
shape is ordinary closed-world reasoning and stays legal.

**Left to S1e2.2.5**, which builds the refusal: F1 (which reading of
*extendable*), F2 (the derived case), F3 (refusal or warning, and where), F4
(what the error names) and F5 (`std.elim` or `features/05`). This stage's
suggestion stays as written in § 4: F1 (b) and F2 with `via`.
