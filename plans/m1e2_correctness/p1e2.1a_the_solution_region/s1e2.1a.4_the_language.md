## Context

A program can state its own answer since M1c:
`:expect (model …)`, `(or (model …) …)` or `(false)`, where *naming a
relation closes it*
([`01_grammar.md` § Query](../../../docs/kernel/ir/03-ein-lang/01_grammar.md#query)).
Each form states **one model set**. Under
[Q-M1e2.7](../open_questions.md#q-m1e27--the-answer-is-a-region-of-the-lattice)
a model set is one line of R, and nothing says which. A query cannot ask for
the minimal line, the maximal line or both, and an expectation cannot state R
or say that it has holes.

The user asked for the language to *"fully cover R in all cases with holes
and without"*. The phase's line was **forms for R only**.

### The rulings (2026-10-16)

[Q-M1e2.7](../open_questions.md#the-rulings-and-the-questions-back-2026-10-16)
(b) and (c) move that line. Solving and asking come apart:

- **`(solve :hrules …)`** defines the problem, which is the lattice. `hrules`
  leave `(query …)`. Today 19 corpus files put `:hrules` on their query
  (outside comments, 2026-10-16).
- **`(solution …)`** states the **full expected region**, for example
  `(solution (or (model …) (model …)))`. The plan's notes:
  - a region shape (a sub-cube, or the two lines under a `:convex` claim the
    runner checks) alongside the explicit list, since `features/02` has 728
    states;
  - a `(model …)` names a **state**, as (e) is ruled.
- **`(query :goal … :expect …)`** answers a question about the region. Its
  `:expect` states **only goal facts**: the projection classes, deduplicated,
  because different models can give one goal row set. A goal can be
  undetermined in some solutions, ∅ among them, and the form has to say how
  that is stated.

`ein solve` studies the region first. Asking for one line is secondary. It
stays possible as a query's choice of subset.

**The third answer (2026-10-16)** sketched the region's own form: *"full region
info shows all valid commitments and all states that have multiple generating
commitments with minimal ones; something like (region (model ...) (model ...)
...) (merged (to from) (to from)))"*. For a monotone program it is exact: a
state's class is the union of [from … to] over its minimal generators
([Q-M1e2.7 § The third answer](../open_questions.md#the-third-answer-2026-10-16)).

The plan proposes that the grammar diff take **one** form for both directions.
`(region …)` with `(merged …)` is what `ein solve` prints (S1e2.1a.5) and what a
program states as its expected region. The second answer's `(solution (or
(model …) …))` is the same claim without the merges, so the diff reconciles the
two names rather than shipping both.

## Acceptance

- **What the rulings call for, and no more:**
  - `(solve …)`;
  - the region form, `(region (model …) …)` with `(merged (to from) …)`,
    reconciled with `(solution …)`, plus the explicit list and the region
    shapes. A run's printed region reads back as its expectation, held by a
    round-trip test;
  - `(query … :expect …)` over the projection classes;
  - a query's choice of subset (all of R, one line, one component) as the
    secondary form.

  A program-level refusal of what cannot be stratified is P1e2.1b's, if
  S1e2.1b.3 rules it. A form the rulings do not need is not built.
- **The migration is named before it moves.** Every corpus `(query …)` that
  carries `:hrules`, every `:expect`, and what each becomes: a `(solution …)`
  or a projection claim.
- **Today's forms keep their meaning**, or the change is ruled and every
  corpus `:expect` it affects is named before it moves. The register's rule
  holds: an `:expect` that states today's wrong answer stays until its fixing
  stage moves it.
- **`ein test` checks the new forms**, and `--json-report` gains the fields
  that describe them: the claim's shape, which line, and the outcome. A form
  the runner cannot check reports `NOT CHECKED` with a stderr line, as
  M1d S1d.4.3 requires.
- **The grammar page, `defined_behaviour.md`** (for any new load error), and
  `configuration.md` (for any new keyword) say so. Every new form gets a
  corpus fixture under `tests/` that exercises it.

### Task T1e2.1a.4.1 — The forms, from the rulings

Write the grammar first, as a page diff the user reads, before any parser
change.

**Written 2026-10-16**: [`region_language.md`](region_language.md). Six
questions for the user (its § 9), the rest decided there unless the user
objects (§ 10). It stands on one measurement, every corpus `:expect` read
against R (`cargo run --release -p ein-infer --example expect_under_r`):

- 49 of 72 claims mean the same over all of R;
- 2 hold only on the maximal line;
- 13 pin the facts of an owing root, where R is empty;
- 8 state a walk wrong answer.

19 of the 27 `(model …)` claims that hold pin facts beyond the goal, so
today's claims belong to the region claim, not to the query's answer. The
proposed re-estimate is 3 days.

**Ruled 2026-10-16, all six questions** (the diff's § 9): the claim lives in
`(solve … :expect (region …))`, generators are `(merged <to> <from>)` in the
user's spelling, `(owing …)` and the query's projection classes are yes, and
there are no region shapes and no component subsets. Before the build the user
asked for real examples: [`region_examples.md`](region_examples.md), eight
corpus programs whose claims are rendered from their computed R. **Accepted
2026-10-16**, with one question back: why `(owing …)` and not an empty
`(region)`. The examples' § 5 answers it, measured. Of the 31 mutation kills
that only those 13 claims make, `(region)` keeps none. **Ruled (A)** the same
day: *"ein solve has to print why R is empty"*. A top-level `(region …)`,
`(merged …)` or `(owing …)` is a load error, decided unless the user objects.
T1e2.1a.4.2 has started.

### Task T1e2.1a.4.2 — Parser, loader, runner

**First half done 2026-10-16: `(solve …)` and the keyword move.**

- **`(solve …)` is a declarator**, the twelfth lexer keyword, at most one per
  file, with an allow-list of the three lattice keywords. On a `(query …)`
  each of them is a load error that names `(solve …)`. Three load negatives
  bank the messages: `query_lattice_keyword`, `solve_twice` and
  `solve_unknown_keyword`.
- **Every reader moved**: the candidate filter (`hypgen`), the hrule
  activators, `factdump`'s targets and the trace's relevance set. The last of
  these was found by its digest moving, not by the grep.
- **The corpus move is answer-neutral, and measured.** The keywords moved off
  `(query …)` in 92 files, by a script that keeps each pair's comments with
  it. Each file's `ein solve --json-summary` before (old binary, old file) and
  after (new binary, new file) is **identical in every field** on all 92. The
  Rust tests' 43 inline programs moved the same way.
- **Two tests changed shape, not meaning.** A file whose queries stated
  *different* lattices cannot exist under (b). `test_cli`'s two
  mixed-outcome tests now get their failed and unchecked claims from one walk
  under one lattice (an obligations program cut at `-m 2`). The test comment
  says the program's third model is W5's row and moves at the switch.
- **Goldens moved, named**:
  - `zebra.golden` and `zebra2.golden`: the dump prints `(solve …)`;
  - `corpus_shapes.md5`: 1 030 of 10 275 renderings, every one on a file
    whose text changed (93 files). They are the `load`, `ir` and `dot` views
    of the program, plus the one load negative whose message lists the query's
    keywords. No trace or dump of an answer moved;
  - `expect_unknown_keyword.expected`: the keyword list.
- **Pages:** `01_grammar.md` § Solve and the § 2 table, `00_ebnf.md`,
  `06_reserved_names.md`, `defined_behaviour.md` (three new rows), and the
  count of twelve wherever eleven was written.
