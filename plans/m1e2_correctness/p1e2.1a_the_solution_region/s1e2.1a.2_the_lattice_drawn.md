## Context

The user drew W1 in words: *"a whole solutions topology {empty, A, B, C,
(B, C)} and an upper bound of this sub-lattice {A, (B, C)}"*. Nothing in
the repo draws that.
[`ein render lattice`](../../../docs/kernel/ir/03-ein-lang/04_dot_rendering.md)
runs a solve and shows survivors and their pruned siblings. Its `--view full`
falls back to the same, because the solve keeps no per-commitment DAG. The
bundle's `pages/lattice/` is one page per entering, with no picture of how
they relate. The user has said what this view is for: *"its visualization
become so important for solution correctness analysis"*.

### Scope, after the user's input of 2026-10-15

Given mid-stage, while the first drawings were being made, and quoted
verbatim:

> *"I have some imagination of how the drawn lattice could look like. There
> are facts from H1 set and other facts, e.g. hypgen can produce and can not;
> they also affect the space, but they are hidden on the search lattice; the
> simple lattice is L0, L1, L2, ... levels with n=0,1,2,... number of hyps and
> from Ln to Ln+1 goes arrows representing adding of some single hypothesis
> fact. This is a lattice, this is a cube, but this is not how the engine
> works. It represents what could be if we have only search without rules that
> produce facts during saturation, this will be the case when we have only
> rules that emit obligations and constraints, but not facts. If we have -
> then saturation can produce facts, and iff it can produce facts from
> hypothesis set - then there are "merged nodes" or better say saturation
> arrows between initial lattice nodes. E.g. sat(root + {H1, H2}) from L2 emits
> {H3, H7} then it is equal to sat(root + {H1, H2, H3, H7}) from L4. We
> probably don't want to see separate saturated node sat(root + H) for every
> pure lattice node (root + H), but want to see what saturation does in terms
> of moving inside the lattice."*

What it changed:

- **The nodes are the pure cube**, and saturation is a **`sat` arrow** `C ⇒
  cl(C)`, where `cl(C)` is the candidates `sat(root + C)` holds. A node that
  moves is drawn transient. A cube edge that adds a fact saturation already
  derived is drawn *absorbed*.
- **The oracle records each node's closure**, together with the candidates it
  negates and how many facts it holds that are not coordinates. Those last are
  the user's *"other facts … hidden on the search lattice"*, split into those
  over a candidate relation (a generator could propose them) and the rest.
- **The comparison with the lines is by state.** A recorded commitment that
  saturates onto a line node records that node.

The arrow is only honest if `C` and `cl(C)` are one state. The cube was asked:
on every corpus cube, 32 074 alive nodes move and none lands on another state
(below).

## Acceptance

- **`ein render region DIR`** reads a `--dump-region` directory and prints
  DOT, so the picture comes from the run whose answer it shows (S1e2.1.3's
  reason for `render kb FILE`).
- **The Hasse diagram**, as the user draws it: L0 at the top, one rank per
  layer, and an edge from each node to its supersets one layer down.
  - **Each node** is labelled with its commitment and coloured by status:
    dead, owing, or in R. A hole has a mark of its own.
  - **Each node's tooltip** gives the reason: for a dead node its core and
    the rule chain that reached `(false)`; for an owing node its owed
    instances.
  - **R's minimal and maximal lines** are outlined, each in its own style.
- **The edges carry the reasons.** The diagram *is* the n-cube, so every
  edge is drawn and classed by the statuses at its two ends: inside R, R to
  dead, R to owing, owing to R, dead to dead, and so on. Each class has a
  style in the legend.
  - A node on the maximal line is one whose upward edges all leave R, so the
    picture shows why it is maximal.
  - **Repair edges** (a hole up into R) are labelled with the fact that
    revives the node.
  - A node where the local test and the whole cube disagree is marked.
- **R's structure, visible.** Each component has its own colour. Each maximal
  sub-cube is listed under the diagram with its dimension (its free facts),
  and outlined in the drawing where the view allows. Each minimal hole names
  its `absent` guard.
- **The walk, overlaid.** Each node also says what this run did with it:
  - entered (`alive`, `dead_pre`, `dead_post`);
  - killed by the lookahead before entering;
  - pruned by a learned clause;
  - never generated;
  - **recorded** as a model or an open state.

  **Every learned nogood is checked against the cube.** It is sound exactly
  when every superset of it is dead. An unsound nogood is drawn and named,
  together with the alive superset it pruned. That is W3's mechanism made
  visible.

  A node on R's reported line that was not recorded is drawn as
  **missing**. A recorded node that is not on that line is drawn as
  **spurious**. The legend says which line was compared, which is
  S1e2.1a.3's ruling (a). Until that ruling exists, the drawing marks both
  lines.
- **Views that stay readable.** The full diagram has 2ⁿ nodes, so:
  - n ≤ 6 (64 nodes): the whole diagram.
  - Up to the oracle's limit: **R and its border**, meaning R, its holes, and
    every dead or owing node adjacent to R. The other nodes are counted per
    layer in the rank label.
  - At any n: a **per-layer table** page (markdown) listing every node with
    its status, its reason and what the walk did with it.

  The thresholds are measured: the stage draws `owed-seats` (n = 4),
  `lattice/02` (n = 3) and the largest corpus program under the limit,
  and fixes the cutoffs on what still reads.
- Every view goes through `dot -Tsvg` in a test, which is `dot_wellformed.rs`'s
  standard. The legend is in `04_dot_rendering.md`.

  the two files and nothing else. The legend is
  [`04_dot_rendering.md` § Region rendering](../../../docs/kernel/ir/03-ein-lang/04_dot_rendering.md#region-rendering--the-cube-and-the-walk-s1e21a2).
  - [`ein_render::region_doc`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-render/src/region_doc.rs)

  - [`ein_render::region_dot`](../../../ein.rs/crates/ein-render/src/region_dot.rs)
    draws: one function per view, the legend, and the table.
  - The shipping crates carry no `serde`, so `dump/json.rs` gained a
    reader, `parse`, round-trip-tested against its own writer.
- **One channel per question.** Fill is what the cube says, shape is what the
  search did, and border is the lines and the defects.
  - Nine edge classes come from the statuses at the two ends. On top of them
    are *absorbed* edges, `sat` arrows, repair edges labelled with their fact,
    and `✗ alive` edges from an unsound exclusion.
  - Every node's tooltip carries its reason: the core and the refuting rules,
    the `absent` premises, the owed instances, the closure, the negated
    candidates, the facts beyond the coordinates, the sub-cubes it is in, and

  `LatticeProof::promotions` was added for this: each forced positive, timed
  against the enterings before it. `walk.json` gained `promotions`, `bounds`,
  and a `state_mask` per entering. Each node is then one of:
  - recorded, root, root after the cascade, entered, or reached by saturation;
  - or never stood on, for one named reason: outside the run's universe,
    pre-killed, below the search's root, element refuted, pruned by a clause,
    past `-m`, or never generated.

  **Every exclusion is checked** (learned clauses, refuted singletons,
  lookahead kills), and an unsound one names the smallest alive superset it
  pruned. **Missing and spurious** are computed against both lines, by state.
- **The oracle's nodes** gained `closure`, `excluded`, `beyond` and
  `beyond_hypothesised`. `utils/region.py --json` computes `closure` too, by
  its own route (`--dump-answer`'s `root.ein`), and `region_dump.rs` holds the
  two routes to the same closures on the seven programs plus
  `saturation/transitive/taxonomy.ein`. The test fails if no node moved, so
  the check cannot pass vacuously.
  - **Cost:** `bijection/06` (n = 16) took 6.10 s before and 6.09 s after, on
    `ein solve … --dump-region`, 2026-10-15.
- **`region.md`** now compares lines by state, as the drawing does.
- **The bundle shows the lattice.** Each run gets `pages/region/`: the `auto`
  drawing, the `lines` drawing when `auto` is not already that, every node by
  layer paged at 500 rows, and `region.md`. Each case page carries every run's
  drawing under § 3, and each program page does too. The case page's § *The

### The thresholds (T1e2.1a.2.3), and what each was chosen on

Measured 2026-10-15 on the release build. Every corpus file was run through
`ein solve FILE --dump-region DIR`, then every view of every dump through
`ein render region` and `dot -Tsvg` (views over 400 nodes were not fed to
`dot`). 178 dumps; every view accepted.

| cutoff | value | chosen on |
|---|---|---|
| `full` | `n ≤ 6` | the stage's own number. At n = 6 (`ein-bugs/alive-set-fresh-name-declared.ein`, 64 nodes) the whole cube still reads, labels and all |
| `border` | ≤ 64 nodes | reads at 56 (`branching/05_mini_zebra.ein`, n = 9, widest layer 21 nodes); does not read at 247 (`type-exclusivity/colors.ein`, n = 8: one layer ~60 nodes, 6 400 px wide at 40 dpi). Nothing in the corpus falls between |
| `lines` | unbounded | ≤ 40 nodes on every corpus program; the legend caps each list at 12 and `--view table` has the rest |

What `auto` picked: `full` for 139 dumps, `border` for 5 (largest 56 nodes),
`lines` for 5 (largest 40), and 29 dumps are over the oracle's limit, drawn
as one note.

Three changes came from reading the drawings rather than from the plan:

- A run of layers with nothing drawn is **one rank label**. `bijection/06`'s
  border view was fifteen empty ranks.
- A dead node's saturation move is **not drawn**, because a contradictory
  fixpoint holds everything. In `mini_zebra`'s border view, every dead node
  read "⇒ +k by saturation". The tooltip keeps the move.
- The legend is a key **plus** wrapped notes, nodes named by bit past n = 4.
  `colors`' legend had stretched to the width of its longest line.

## Decisions taken without a ruling

Each can be overruled here.

1. **Missing and spurious compare states (`class`), not masks.** Comparing
   masks would call a recorded commitment spurious whenever saturation moves
   it, because the commitment is not the node its fixpoint is.
2. **Only recorded *models* are compared with the lines.** An open state is
   drawn as recorded and judged by neither line, since the engine reports it as
   not yet a model.
3. **A node's walk mark is the first match** in the order `region_doc::Skip`
   lists. A node with a pre-killed fact and a pruning clause says pre-killed.
4. **Exclusions are checked for soundness as "every superset dead"**, for a
   clause, a refuted singleton and a lookahead kill alike. The witness drawn
   is the smallest alive superset (by layer, then by mask), whether or not
   the search reached it some other way.
5. **The table is `render region --view table`**, markdown from a
   subcommand otherwise for DOT, because the bundle does not compute and the
   table needs the join.
6. **Sub-cubes are listed, not outlined as clusters.** They overlap whenever
   two share a node, which they do on `branching/15`. A node's tooltip names
   its sub-cubes, and their outline is their R → R edges.
