## Context

[`solve_interface.md`](solve_interface.md), approved 2026-10-16, switches the
default once, in S1e2.1a.5's commit, and that commit removes the last CLI path
to the walk ([§ 6 there](solve_interface.md#6-what-moves)). The plan had two
pieces of work after the switch that need the walk, or need to be finished
before it goes:

- **The flips.** 15 of the register's 26 rows record a wrong answer that is
  the walk's: W1 (7 rows), W2 (2), W3 (2), W5, W9 (2) and W11's
  `unsat-core-id-space`. After the switch no run reproduces them, so
  `wrong_answers.rs` fails on every one unless the row flips to `fixed` in
  the same commit. A row flips only against a `right` block, and those blocks
  were derived under Q-M1e.6 and Q-M1e.7 ruling A, not under R. S1e2.1a.7 was
  to restate them, after the switch.
- **The walk's wrong answers with no row.** [`walk_and_region.md`](walk_and_region.md)
  found walk answers that disagree with R, and S1e2.1a.6 was to file them.
  After the switch nothing reproduces them, so a row filed then would record
  an answer nobody can observe.

Both move here. The walk's last census runs while the walk is still the
default, and every `right` block is restated under R before anything flips.

## Acceptance

- **The walk's last census.** It covers every corpus entry and register
  witness whose R is known (route 1 under its budget, or the oracle at
  n ≤ 16), with every declared `solve` run and every `levers` cell. For each,
  the walk's answer is compared with R. It extends `--example walk_vs_region`
  from its six variants to the corpus's declared cells. Each disagreement is
  named by mechanism, using `walk_and_region.md`'s five reasons. It then
  becomes one of three things:
  - a register row;
  - a twin on an existing case;
  - an explained non-defect: a budget that says so, or an answer outside the
    oracle's universe (W4, W6).

  This is the walk's half of what S1e2.1a.6 was to do. The modes' half stays
  there.
- **The new rows.** The census's eight programs (§ 3.3 there), and its 17
  twins on existing cases (§ 3.2). `d3-unsat-core` under `-e` needs a
  register run that no corpus entry declares, and T1e2.1a.10.2 decides whether
  to file one. Each new row is `open`, with the walk's
  answer as `wrong`, R as `right`, and `derived` naming the oracle or route 1.
  A witness with an `:expect` keeps it stating today's answer until the switch
  moves both (the register's rule). `alive-empty-interlayer`'s `(false)` is
  one.
- **Every `right` block restated under R**: the 26 rows and the new ones.
  - The register grows R's fields: k_s, k_c, the maximal and minimal lines'
    states over `closes`, and the route that computed them. The schema
    version moves, and `ein-corpus`'s `register` module reads the new fields.
  - A row about something R does not change keeps its own fields, and R's go
    beside them. That covers the core (W10, W11's `d3-unsat-core`, W12), the
    candidate list (W4, W6), the saturator (W7) and the read-out (C1).
  - `derived` names the oracle or route 1. A right answer that changes is
    listed in this file with its reason.
- **Held by a test, not copied by hand.** A test holds every R field of every
  row to the library's answer: `ein_infer::region` at n ≤ 16, route 1 past it.
  The switch then flips each row against a check that has already passed.
- **The reversals.** Under R the run with the kill cache off is the right one
  ([`walk_and_region.md` § 7](walk_and_region.md#7-two-rulings-meet-here),
  which follows from [`solve_interface.md` § 0](solve_interface.md#0-the-ruling)'s
  approval).
  - **W9's two rows.** The `-K` run becomes the control that agrees with R,
    and the default run becomes the wrong one. The title and `derived` are
    restated. `question` keeps Q-M1e.7: ruling A stays true of what the walk
    records.
  - **W11's `unsat-core-id-space`.** `right` becomes R's two maximal states,
    `{(r1 o1 o5)}` and `{(r1 o4 o1)}`, where it says `Contradiction`, k = 0.
    That is the oracle's computation, and this stage asks the user nothing
    about it. The user's reading of W11 is still
    [S1e2.1.7](../p1e2.1_seeing_the_answer/s1e2.1.7_the_review.md)'s and
    [S1e2.2.12](../p1e2.2_wrong_answers/s1e2.2.12_core_id_order.md)'s
    (ruled 2026-10-16).
- **Non-monotone rows carry the stub.** A restated `right` on a flagged
  witness is the cube's, under *assumes a monotone lattice*.
  - **W3's two rows** are on § 6's flip list, and the cube agrees with their
    right answers.
  - **W8's rows are not restated into `right`.** Their question is the node
    under `absent` (Q-M1e2.2), which is P1e2.1b's. The cube's answer agrees
    with the walk today, so it goes in as a `[[case.right.alternative]]` with
    the reading it assumes, and `right` stays.
- **W1, re-read in R's terms** (moved from S1e2.1a.7). Its seven rows were
  confirmed on 2026-10-15, before the definition was written. If the restated
  right answers still agree, the confirmation stands and the rows say so. If
  one does not, it goes back to the user.
- **Nothing flips here.** Every row stays `open`, because the walk is still
  the default and still gives the wrong answer. The flips, the 46 runs
  re-pointed and every `:expect` that states a walk answer all move in
  S1e2.1a.5's commit. The register's header names the commit this stage was
  taken at.
- `./run_tests.sh` green.

### Task T1e2.1a.10.3 — R's fields, and the test that holds them

**Done 2026-10-16.**

- **Schema `ein-wrong-answers/2`.** Every row has a `[case.region]`: the
  route, the static verdict, k_s, k_c, the kind of an empty R, and both lines
  as commitments. That is R's vocabulary, the one `ein solve --maximal`
  prints. The walk's `models` over `closes` stay where they were.
  `ein-corpus`'s `register` module reads the table and refuses a row without
  one, or with fields that contradict each other.
- **`ein-infer/tests/register_region.rs`** recomputes every row's region with
  `explore`, the code `ein solve` runs, and holds every field to it. It runs in
  2.1 s in the debug build and 1.5 s in release. `EIN_BLESS=1` re-banks the
  blocks, and banked them here. A debug build skips W7's two witnesses, as
  `explore.rs` does, and the release build holds them.
- **Nothing is hand-copied.** Each block is the library's own output, and
  every row is computable: route 1 describes the five witnesses past n = 16
  in full.
